# Funded spend knowledge, tree binding and receipt privacy

**Keep the candidate outside the live admission registry and stop after the funded signed-leg measurement.** A fresh public verification tests execution of the registered polynomial argument. The 256-bit internal capacity of its balanced four-child sponge also limits a *fixed-target* structural binding game, including with an existing branch root. Correctness for one wallet opening does not resolve that issue. The three-round Fiat–Shamir transform has no demonstrated concrete extractor for the adaptive quantum spender, and the joint observer still sees the original KEM receipts. ## Exactly what needs extraction

Fix a network, approved program, protocol version, branch anchor, ordinary or leg opcode, all public fields, ordered receipt bytes and candidate root. The challenge is an accepted, current transaction for which an efficient extractor interacting with the quantum creator cannot return a satisfying spend assignment, except with a bounded probability and runtime loss. For economic authorization, that assignment must represent two slots with at least one actual authorized funded input, the proper real/dummy policy, authentic openings and paths, distinct original nullifiers and exact native/asset balance and fee. SLG1 requires a real first input, keys bound into outputs and both final owner signatures on the same pair. Node acceptance additionally binds the root to the selected branch, checks receipts and spentness against **one** state across registrations, then updates both legs atomically. Existence of *some* witness for a true statement is weaker than knowledge by the spender. Knowledge of an arithmetic path is weaker than genuine branch membership if the compression binding fails. The spend circuit expressly binds the receipt digest as a public copy. Computing it over the actual bytes and checking receipt ownership have different verifiers. [Target](../../spend-proof-decision/TARGET.md) · [Monetary and membership constraints](../../funded-whir/relation/frontend.py) · [Swap authorization](../../atomic-swap/swap_wire.py)

The adversary chooses statements after seeing other spends, can own wallets and control delivery, and can retain fork/retry histories. Quantum public-hash queries and classical interactions with wallets and nodes are permitted. Extract both legs under the same adaptive history. Failure of *either* authorization suffices. Conditional per-leg errors can be added, or one may prove a joint theorem. Multiplying them is unsuitable. Signature authenticity also cannot supply a monetary assignment. A union bound over M separate opportunities costs up to log₂ M bits if each conditional bound actually holds. For 100M this is 26.58 bits. Offered traffic is neither the number of simultaneously useful internal targets nor an adversarial hash-query budget.

## Binding of the two-permutation digest

The reviewed function initializes a width-12 Goldilocks Poseidon2 state with the ASCII tag `PQ4N/v1` in capacity lane 8 and zeros elsewhere. Each rate block is eight canonical fields added modulo p. The function absorbs 16 ordered limbs in two blocks, permutes after each, then publishes four rate limbs. Thus the rate has eight words, the capacity four, and the output four, with p=2⁶⁴−2³²+1. The pinned permutation uses the initial mixing layer, four full rounds, 22 partial rounds, four full rounds and the seventh-power S-box. Fixed arity, canonical serialization and the IV remove a length/padding ambiguity and separate the initial state from the old variable-length sponge. They supply no extra capacity. Honest nodes sort *serialized* child digests and pad groups. The arithmetic verifier constrains insertion at one secret slot. It accepts arbitrary canonical siblings as potential openings and relies on the authenticated root to bind them. Its private active-prefix length is shared by both inputs but is not checked against a public leaf count or actual depth. |---|---:|---:|---|
| Freely chosen collision in an ideal four-word digest | p² ≈ 2¹²⁸ | p^(4/3) ≈ 2⁸⁵·³³ | A binding reduction requiring arbitrary collisions is already below the targeted quantum exponent. |
| Target drawn before search, access only to an ideal four-word **function** | p⁴ ≈ 2²⁵⁶ | p² ≈ 2¹²⁸ | Useful control experiment. It omits the public inverse permutation. |
| Targeted two-block parent with an accessible forward/inverse permutation and sufficient input freedom | p² ≈ 2¹²⁸ | p^(4/3) ≈ 2⁸⁵·³³ | The four-word middle capacity supports generic matching even for a specified output. |

The cited quantum sponge paper explicitly gives the capacity-claw preimage upper bound after Theorem 2.2. Its bitstring sponge result is a construction-level guide. Our specialization uses equality of the two capacity subvectors, a space of p⁴, which survives additive field absorption just as it survives XOR absorption. A fixed IV only translates the domain. Even if one four-word child of the first block is prescribed, the other still has p⁴ possible canonical values. The four-word result leaves eight free words in a compatible terminal state. The other input block has no additional sibling predicates in this registered path. These dimensions leave sufficient degrees of freedom for the ideal-permutation query scale in the table. Honest sorting does not restrict the arithmetic verifier’s sibling domain. If alternatives were restricted to complete independently funded child subtrees, the experiment would change. The source checks only the branch root publicly. Public choice of the correct anchor, a stable nullifier, and rejection of a stale root are necessary but do not repair the parent function. [Quantum sponge §2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16887) · [Classical preimage security, §§1 and 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/csrc/media/Events/2022/lightweight-cryptography-workshop-2022/documents/papers/tight-preimage-resistance-of-the-sponge.pdf) · [Quantum collision/claw algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9705002)

This is an ideal-permutation capacity analysis and an upper bound for the generic method under its sampling assumptions. No measured inversion or spend forgery was attempted. A representative table-based quantum method would handle roughly 2⁸⁵·³³ entries. At 32 bytes per entry even its raw table would take roughly 2⁹⁰·³³ bytes before addressing and lookup. Reversible permutation gates, coherent lookup, memory, online work, time–space tradeoffs and the full monetary/owner predicates matter for physical resource claims. An unrelated creator cannot infer a particular honest user's secret just by finding a parent preimage. Conversely, a proof of a 128-bit **query** barrier for arbitrary funded-membership openings cannot invoke a random four-word digest and ignore the known inverse. The primitive's concrete algebraic properties, restricted input distributions, cross-domain interactions, accepted branch roots and choice of multiple targets require a separate analysis. For M unrelated digest targets in the ideal-function search control alone, the square-root exponent loses ½ log₂ M; do not reuse that number as a capacity-claw bound. The live parent also processes two variable blocks before its known delimiter block. An invertible fixed final step does not itself raise the interior capacity. This is an inherited structural concern, independent of whether the live proof engine is operational.

## What the actual seven-move transcript checks

The isolated FTB1 experiment specifically registers **FTL1 on side 0 and the released SLG1 on side 1**. It retains two independently authorized legs, and verification of the original leg uses its old engine. It cannot yield evidence for a two-FTL1 exchange, a new ordinary prover, or a recovered onward FTL1 spend. The node derives the selected endpoint ID, funded count and root; the proof's 32-byte context is SHA-256 of the versioned domain, network ID, endpoint ID, u64LE count and full ordered 3,584 receipt bytes. The outer verifier also compares the legacy receipt digest to public words 22–25. This authenticates the branch context but does not put an equality between the private path length and actual branch depth in the arithmetic. A chosen-context SHA-256 collision and a fixed-target substitution on a preexisting context have different loss budgets. 1. Commit to 262,144 indexed leaves. Each holds 450 witness shares, 50 hint shares and 33 degree masks, plus an independent 32-byte leaf salt. SHA3-512 leaf, internal and final-root domains use `L,N,R`; the root commitment is h₁. Distinct parties use a permutation of the order-2¹⁸ subgroup; the 117 secret evaluations use a disjoint coset. A witness polynomial through 117 assigned values and 32 independent random values has degree at most 148.
2. `G(prefix,h₁)` supplies 33 × 500 random coefficients. The 33 masked combinations of the committed polynomials are interpolated to 149 canonical evaluation rows and hashed under `X` to h₂.
3. `B(prefix,h₁,h₂)` supplies five independent batches. Each has 144 nonlinear block weights and 35,934 linear weights, including 271 padding equations. Polynomial broadcasts have degree at most 1,036 with zeros on Ω; linear broadcasts have degree at most 264 and the required sum on Ω. Their individual degree-limited masks are independent across batches. The overlapping hint offsets cover residual coefficients. The sum-zero mask has one coefficient corrected before commitment. Hashing the five ordered pairs under `A` gives h₃.
4. `I(prefix,h₁,h₂,h₃)` selects the 32 distinct openings. The verifier reconstructs each salted row, authenticates their shared frontier and recomputes h₁. It reconstructs h₂ from opened values and 117 supplied values per degree combination. It reconstructs h₃ from local checks, prescribed zero/sum constraints and both tails. EOF and canonical field checks close the parser. The maximum is 239,976 proof bytes and 261,068 signed bytes under this layout. The actual specimen has its own frontier. ## Privacy when the recipient actually receives and spends

For two challenge histories match public context, branch, role, count, expiry, payer, terms visibility, receipt sizes, permitted retries and corruption history. Give the observer the proofs, commitments and full ordered receipts, both leg signatures and allowed network/wallet responses. Compare worlds varying only eligible hidden amounts, openings, assigned recipients or input occurrences while preserving public effects and mandatory leakage. Allow adaptive later spends and honest-wallet receipt queries under a defined error policy. The view of an actual counterparty includes its privately agreed amounts and address. For identical resubmission the bytes are identical, and a reanchored/reproved input keeps its original visible nullifier. Both must be part of the leakage function. A comparison that swaps a counterparty's own expected payment with an unrelated receipt is distinguishable by design.

FR’s honest-verifier sharing argument covers a selected set of 32 local views. Independent per-proof masks and salts matter. Reusing one committed tree under differing opening subsets can exceed the sharing privacy threshold. Fresh starts must resample the proof randomness; retransmission of the same recorded packet stays linkable. Separately, one note has a 1,792-byte receipt (ML-KEM-1024 ciphertext 1,568; encrypted note 128; encrypted memo 77; version and framing 19). The two AEADs use a common KEM secret with different derivation labels and the seven-byte header as associated data. Their hidden plaintexts are related to the published commitments/outputs. The recipients' static keys derive from backup, so later seed disclosure permits retrospective scanning. Observers know each swap’s incoming/change positions even when amounts stay hidden. The two parties must check decryptability, intended spending key, type, value and commitment *before* both signatures. A public digest proof alone permits an authentic binding to an unusable receipt. [Wire fields, KDF and recovery](../../spend-proof-decision/RECEIVING.md)

A joint privacy proof needs a multi-theorem quantum-ZK simulator with correlated auxiliary inputs, recipient key privacy for actual FIPS 203 ML-KEM, two DEM invocations with the exact KDF, chosen/adversarial recipient-key policy and wallet reaction model. In a proof hybrid one must simulate proof transcripts consistently with real note commitments and receipt digest; in a ciphertext hybrid one must preserve correlated public note commitments and later authorized outputs. Ordinary KEM IND-CCA confidentiality alone does not justify either joint step. *Post-Quantum Anonymity of Kyber* establishes relevant positive results for pre-standard Kyber and hybrid PKE in its model, advancing the earlier analysis. It does not instantiate this funded bundle, its final ML-KEM variant, static seed, labels or multi-round proof system. The signed specimen provides no quantified anonymity advantage; it changes no receipt fields or metadata.
