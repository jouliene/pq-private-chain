# A single-challenge arithmetic commit-and-open comparison

An alternative compiler can change the extraction premise while preserving the *exact* fixed-parent monetary circuit. The following deliberately simple uncompressed codec uses the three-party arithmetic decomposition from ZKBoo and the ordinary commit-and-open extractor of Don, Fehr, Majenz and Schaffner (DFMS). This is a priced design. No replacement spending/verifying implementation or funded performance is claimed. It independently addresses the proof-system question. It would inherit the original four-word membership binding and receipt limits.

## One commitment then one challenge

Evaluate the actual registered matrix over the Goldilocks prime p. Its hidden inputs are *all* private assignment wires, including computation trace values. For each registered row output one residual in its published order. A linear row uses its affine form. A quadratic row computes ab−c. A seventh-power row computes x², x⁴, x⁶, x⁷ in four binary multiplications and subtracts the row output. Put every public term into share 0. All multiplications use the cyclic three-share arithmetic decomposition below. Each of 480 parallel repetitions has fresh randomness and its own sharing of the same private assignment.

1. The prover chooses uniform additive shares x₀,x₁, with x₂=w−x₀−x₁ per input. In each multiplication, party i's share of the new product is zᵢ=aᵢbᵢ+aᵢ₊₁bᵢ+aᵢbᵢ₊₁+rᵢ−rᵢ₊₁, indices modulo 3. Each party gets a fresh uniform rᵢ for that gate. The sum is ab. Linear operations apply locally. For every residual the three output shares must sum to zero. The prover commits separately to each of 1,440 complete party views and publishes, for each repetition, the full residual-share vectors of parties 0 and 1. The negative of their sum determines the third. This common first-message data is DFMS's auxiliary string a°.
2. Hash the versioned network context, shape identification, exact canonical public statement, first-message residuals and indexed commitments together. Use the low 760 bits, interpreted as an unsigned little-endian integer, and expand it to 480 base-three digits least significant first, retaining leading zero digits. Uniform 760-bit strings give a uniform subset of size 2⁷⁶⁰ of the 3⁴⁸⁰ possible opening vectors. A digit i requires views i and i+1 for its repetition.
3. Open exactly those two salted views. For each pair the verifier recomputes their commitments, all affine wires for both, the first party’s product equations gate by gate and both final residual-share vectors against the common a°. It checks every field is less than p, fixed widths and EOF. Each disclosed second-party product share is recorded in its own view; its correctness is checked under the cyclically shifted challenge. The verifier never receives all three shares of a private input in a single repetition.

The raw view is a 64-byte independent salt followed by n canonical u64LE private shares, G u64LE mask words and G u64LE product shares. All arrays follow registered order. Hash each view with fixed domain `FT3/view/v1\0`, suite byte, u16LE repetition, party byte and this fixed-width payload. Use SHA3-512 for its 64-byte digest. For the challenge use two independently tagged SHA3-512 invocations, `FT3/challenge/0/v1\0` and `FT3/challenge/1/v1\0`, concatenated before extracting 760 bits. The remaining input has a one-byte suite, locally derived 32-byte network context, pinned 32-byte shape ID, the canonical fixed public words and the fixed first message. In the ideal-oracle calculation one can realize both halves as one 1,024-bit oracle with disjoint input tags, ignoring the unused second half for commitments. Conversely a public half-oracle query is simulated by querying and uncomputing the wide oracle (at most two calls). A concrete proof treating SHA3-512 or its public Keccak permutation as this oracle is a separate assumption. DFMS Remark 3.6 permits wider challenges with truncated commitment digests. [ZKBoo §4.1, arithmetic decomposition](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_giacomelli.pdf) · [DFMS §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13730)

In fixed view data, if one repetition has all three accepting pair choices then the three product checks collectively establish the complete circuit on the sum of its three input shares. All three output vectors match the common residual statement and sum to zero. An extractor can scan each repetition, try its three pairs, and output its reconstructed assignment as soon as one qualifies. This is the S-sound* condition of DFMS Definitions 3.4/3.5. Without an extractable repetition the number of potentially accepting vectors is at most 2⁴⁸⁰ out of 2⁷⁶⁰, so p_triv ≤ 2⁻²⁸⁰ even for the restricted challenge space. For 1,440 commitments, n=512 and q=2·2¹²⁸+962 (conservatively charging half-oracle and verifier calls), Theorem 4.2's online extraction-failure bound is at most (22·1440+60)q³/2⁵¹²+20q²·2⁻²⁸⁰ ≈ 2⁻¹⁷·⁶⁸. DFMS's compressed-oracle extractor costs O(q²) times polynomial factors. This is a single adaptive output, a quantum *query* and extraction-error statement in an ideal model. It gives neither real gate costs nor system-wide or lifetime security. The factor is substantial for multiple independent targets unless a joint theorem charges the global query budget.

For an honest interactive opening of parties i and i+1 the two private-share vectors and their masks are uniform. Each uncheckable second-party product share has a fresh hidden third-party mask; the first-party product follows from the two opened shares and masks. Their computed residual-share vectors determine the remaining vector for a true zero residual. A view simulator therefore has a concrete honest-verifier starting point. Extending it to repeated noninteractive proofs and correlated ciphertexts with adversarial keys, branch history and permitted corruptions requires separate work. DFMS's ε_sim=0 means **extractor-oracle** simulation in its knowledge definition. It is not a statement of zero-knowledge for the funded payment.
