# Retain the funded MNG1 and SLG1 registrations

**Close the four-word fixed-parent TCitH branch.** A wallet created and authorized a complete funded FTL1 leg. A separately controlled wallet checked its incoming funds and signed a matched released leg. The isolated node verified and mined their exchange, and rejected either order of a second spend across registrations. The signed FTL1 frame fits the existing limit. Its concrete membership construction and noninteractive transcript do not establish an improvement in funded post-quantum security. Allowing the registration on the live network would add another way to authorize spending from the same pool of notes. Keep the measured implementation available for research. The live registrations have their own open security assumptions. [Protocol and source](proof/README.md) · [Detailed assurance review](security/REVIEW.md)

## What the isolated network accepted

The two wallets agreed to exchange 20 disposable managed units for 8 native units. The native payer also paid 3 to the miner. Each owner verified control, currency, quantity, change, both proof projections and the joint digest before releasing its own SLH-DSA signature. The experiment generated two alternative, fully signed bundles with identical monetary effects and ciphertexts. The first replaced only side zero with FTL1. The second used SLG1 on both sides. Each wallet refused an incorrect incoming amount, asset and key. A signature from one bundle failed verification on the other. Publication intentionally discloses the test agreement in the manifest. [Owner procedure and measurements](wallet/README.md)

The verifier rebuilt the branch from 12 public PoW blocks. It derived the eligible height-12 block ID, legacy anchor, auxiliary root and 22 leaves from its own database. A thirteenth, unrelated mined block raised the count to 23. Both bundles remained individually eligible against the earlier anchor. Independent disposable databases admitted them in opposite orders. The second submission in each trial hit a pool conflict, then a spent nullifier after block inclusion. Each winning height-14 block consumed four nullifiers, carried two signed frames and paid a reward of 103. Both databases recorded 18 cumulative fees and the same 14-element spent set. A heavier height-15 fork from height 11 removed the swap and its anchor. Fees reverted to 15, the spent set to 10, and both registrations rejected the orphan. Startup on a disposable copy reproduced all 18 archived wallet records, including the orphan branch. The verifier also rejected a changed public root, ordered receipt, proof commitment and owner signature. Each failed offer left the observed ledger state and pool intact. ## Which assurance improved

The candidate replaces the released FRI proof-component assumptions for side zero with an explicit threshold-sharing argument. It uses salted SHA3-512 commitments and permits an interactive classical error calculation of approximately 2⁻²⁵⁶·¹⁵⁷ for its registered parameters. Neither that calculation nor a wider proof hash establishes stronger quantum knowledge of a funded spend. The exact three-stage Fiat–Shamir protocol lacks a concrete adaptive quantum extractor at the target cost. The generic multi-round reduction already gives a vacuous numerical screen at 2¹²⁸ oracle queries. The receipts retain ML-KEM-1024, correlated encrypted outputs and public retry/nullifier linkage. There is no complete joint simulation/recipient-anonymity result for these messages. [Transcript and security assumptions](security/REVIEW.md) · [Feneuil and Rivain](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1573) · [Multi-round Fiat–Shamir](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05207)

The two-absorption parent has only four Goldilocks capacity words. Under the public invertible ideal-permutation model, the capacity-matching scale is about 2⁸⁵·³³ quantum queries, including a fixed target when the other siblings are free. The actual circuit leaves these siblings unconstrained except by the terminal digest. The node binds the leaf count into the Fiat–Shamir context. The registered arithmetic does not equate private path depth to the depth implied by that count. This evidence rules out treating the parent as a four-word random function with a 128-bit quantum target-search justification. It supplies neither a practical spend forgery nor a concrete lower bound for the actual Poseidon2 permutation. The released parent deserves a separate binding review too. [Exact assumptions and resource qualifications](security/REVIEW.md) · [Alagic et al., §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16887v2#S2.SS2)

## Next decision

The costed redesign keeps funded note commitments and the original nullifiers, widens the membership digest and capacity to seven words, uses a different domain at every level and constrains depth to the value derived by the node. This takes the generic capacity-matching exponent for this method to about 149.33. A compiled full leg at the current geometry projects to 417,772 signed bytes. The first fitting point in the bounded search requires processing 2.695 TB of field-share bytes per signed leg. This is prover work volume, not a RAM measurement. A separately priced single-challenge transform addresses the extraction premise but its simple complete encoding costs 1,189,845,092 signed bytes and still inherits the narrow parent. Neither qualifies for a funded rollout. [Wider-tree circuit, controls and price](security/WIDER.md) · [Single-challenge messages and theorem premises](security/ONE_CHALLENGE.md)

Before another funded proof, require a membership construction with a justified fixed-target quantum binding argument for the actual field encoding and level/leaf domains, and a complete wallet-cost model at the existing frame limit. Check its root, depth and common nullifiers on the retained public funding prefix. A credible cost/correspondence result would justify a new owner decision. The conditional follow-on work on a new transfer, two candidate legs and a restored onward spend stops at this gate. MNG1/SLG1 retain existing funds on the devnet. Production security, private network metadata and the global permissionless blockchain objective remain open.

A wire reader written independently of the C++ code reconstructed the funded proof's 32 sampled parties and 387 authentication hashes. The reviewer then created another 237,800-byte proof with its own independently funded disposable wallets on a different selected PoW branch. Its signed frame measured 258,892 bytes. The integration owner replayed that second public archive in a fresh source-built node and obtained both conflict rejections and the orphan rollback again. The original local decision stands.
