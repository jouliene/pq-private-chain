## The registered sharing and openings

Feneuil and Rivain's packed Protocol 8 checks two kinds of relations. A degree-`d` constraint applies independently in each of `s` slots. A second challenge checks the wired global system `Aw=t` by opening a masked polynomial whose `s` secret-slot values have a prescribed **sum**. Arbitrary sparse forms of the private assignment can mix slots. The funded compiler explicitly groups the private assignment and the nonlinear input/output ports, with global rows tying each port to its affine expression in that same assignment. Public ONE and the 31/34 statement words supply known coefficients on the right-hand side. This costs 369,050/379,125 witness words for the quadratic ordinary/leg and 64,088/66,539 for the direct degree-seven layouts, before padding each group separately. A locally consistent `c=a·b` or `y=x⁷` on substituted ports does not imply satisfaction of that global system. Each non-dummy input must open to the funded anchor and its own nullifier. A disabled input instead follows the dummy constraints. [FR Protocol 8 and §§6.3–6.4](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1573) · [Frozen encoding census](../encoding/CENSUS.json)

Use distinct public party evaluation points and disjoint secret points in the Goldilocks field. With `s=101`, `ℓ=25`, the private sharing degree is 125. Under direct degree seven, the two broadcasts have degrees 875 and 225. Independent masks vanish at all secret slots for the polynomial checks and have zero *sum* for the linear check. Masking every global slot individually to zero would disclose the slot contributions. The degree-enforcing test covers all committed input and hint shares of the advertised degree. Overlapping hint windows must cover every residual coefficient after conditioning on the `ℓ` observed shares, including the shortened terminal window. Polynomial checks alone cannot certify this.

The independent calculation gives 661 witness columns, 50 hint columns and 33 mask columns for one SLG1 tree. Worst-case bit-reversal positions expose 382 siblings. The proof carries 132,200 witness bytes, 10,000 hint bytes, 33,264 degree-test bytes, 30,000 polynomial-broadcast bytes, 8,000 linear-broadcast bytes, 24,448 sibling bytes, 800 salt bytes and 192 digest bytes. The 1,048,576 leaf field payloads alone total **6,241,124,352 bytes** in the prover's computation. Streaming and evaluation algorithms change working memory and time while preserving that committed payload. [Public byte model](../model/model.py)

The `2s−2` residual linear-broadcast words can be encoded without an unpriced exceptional point. Interpolate the remainder `R` at the opened party points and write `α′=R+VH`, with `V` vanishing at those points. The secret sum imposes one linear equation on the `2s−1` coefficients of `H`. Some coefficient among the first `s` has nonzero weight, since the `s` secret points are distinct and all evaluations of `V` there are nonzero. A deterministic pivot recovers the omitted coefficient. The audit has a canonical Goldilocks example where the first coefficient has zero weight but a later one suffices. Equivalently, send `s−1` secret evaluations, recover the last from the sum, and complete interpolation with distinct public points. Reconstruct the originally committed full broadcast before accepting the Fiat–Shamir digest. [Executable pivot control](audit.py)

## A bound independent of the search grid

Use the Eq. (18) Merkle variant. Let `W` be the sum of the unpadded group lengths, `D=s+ℓ−1`, `k=s+ℓ+1` and `K=τℓ`. Let `T` denote the separate paper-style targets, in bits, for the batch, degree test and repeated subset odds. The layout uses 8-byte base-field elements and 64-byte Merkle digests. `N<2⁶⁴` and `p<2⁶⁴` give `K≥⌊T/64⌋+1` and `ρ≥⌊T/64⌋+1` at integer multiples of 64. For `s≥2`, `d≥2`, the subset condition gives

```
2^T ≤ [C(N,ℓ)/C(dD,ℓ)]^τ ≤ [N/(dD−ℓ+1)]^K ≤ (N/k)^K.
```

Since `C(N,k)≥(N/k)^k`, degree enforcement implies `η > L+Lk/K` where `L=T/64`. Opened witness values, degree-enforcing data and the two response polynomials already cost at least

```
8 [ K W/s + L s²/K + (L+ρ(d+1))s − ρ(d+1) ] bytes.
```

This uses `ceil(W/s)` as an optimistic relaxation of the separate group padding. For the gate `s=1` raw opened witnesses already fail. For `ℓ≥2s` they again suffice. Otherwise `dD−ℓ+1≥2ℓ`, and dispersed query positions in the complete binary tree require at least `ℓ log₂[N/(2ℓ)]` sibling hashes per repetition. The subset bound therefore adds at least `64T` bytes across the trees. This is an attainable geometry for verifier tapes, sufficient to exclude a guaranteed maximum. It is not an estimate of every noninteractive proof's size.

To minimize the expression over all `K`, substitute `s=K^(2/3)u`. Every positive term increases with `K` for fixed positive `u`, leaving the constant term unchanged. Take the smallest allowed `K` and bound `1/s` from below by its tangent `2/r−s/r²`. Minimizing the resulting quadratic over the reals gives the following *lower* bounds, even before headers, hint openings and salts. All final arithmetic uses rational numbers. The result depends on the selected screening rule and digest width. A different compiler, relation or hash width requires repricing.

| Encoding and per-component target | Ordinary worst case at least | One leg worst case at least |
|---|---:|---:|
| Quadratic, 256 bits | 243,061 B | 247,031 B |
| Direct degree seven, 768 bits | 241,624 B | 245,818 B |

Conversely, the direct-leg layout at `N=2²², s=105, ℓ=24` fits even a 272-bit component target at 237,552 B. This sensitivity rules out extrapolating the quadratic bound to TCitH generally. Replacing SHA3-512 with shorter digests, fusing more gates or using a different commitment/degree check changes the cost and the relevant cryptographic assumptions.

## Quantum knowledge and receipt privacy

FR Theorem 2 uses a rewindable classical prover. Its extractor can return an assignment, a hash collision or a commitment collision. Lemma 3 concerns the interactive false positive for the two batches. At the common degree-seven profile the negative binary logarithms of the three error terms are approximately 320.00 (batch), 281.17 (degree test) and 256.17 (opened subset). Their sum is approximately `2^-256.17`. The paper's explicit component selection for multi-round KZ-style grinding is an engineering screen. These terms give neither a measured attack nor an interactive **quantum** extractor. [FR Theorem 2 and §6.3](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1573)

Don, Fehr and Majenz, Definition 11 and Corollaries 13/15, consider `c₁=H(0,x,a₁)` followed by `cᵢ=H(i−1,cᵢ₋₁,aᵢ)` in a common uniform challenge space `C`. For seven moves there are three successive challenges, even with parallel repetitions. If interactive quantum soundness `κ` were already proved for that protocol, their generic reduction bounds the Fiat–Shamir success by `(2q+4)^6/6 · (κ+6/|C|)` after summing the ordering error over adaptively chosen statements. The factor at `q=2¹²⁸` has binary logarithm **771.415**. For this upper bound to go below one, the interactive term by itself needs over 771.415 bits and the challenge range by itself over 774 bits. Both terms must jointly fit the desired success threshold. A common 512-bit digest expanded into many coefficients gives an additive contribution to this upper bound of about `2²⁶²`. Remark 14 allows enlarging the *random chained challenge* with fresh randomness. Merely expanding a fixed 512-bit seed creates no additional seed entropy. The 768-bit row above already fails the selected cost screen before this generic proof method can certify the target. This is a limitation of the sufficient reduction, not a sub-128-bit attack. [DFM Definition 11, Remark 14 and Corollaries 13/15](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05207)

Transferring that theorem requires the exact registered statement, round tags, prior challenge links, independent challenge distribution, hash commitments, expansion to field vectors and uniform distinct opened subsets. A quantum interactive *knowledge* property is a separate hypothesis for preservation of knowledge. A tighter protocol-specific theorem could change this calculation. Rotem and Tessaro's multi-round straight-line compiler with QROM security changes the transformation and would need new wire accounting here. [Rotem and Tessaro](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1724)

FR's honest-verifier privacy argument masks the shares and the two correlated broadcasts. Joint privacy in a funded spend also includes the actual ordered encrypted receipts, which share plaintext structure with public note commitments and the proof. It needs a multi-user, potentially malicious-key KEM/AEAD argument, limited corruption assumptions, and composition with later nullifier disclosure, timing and reorg/retry linkability. A malicious sender can bind unusable ciphertexts to a valid public digest. Each received note must be opened, matched to the intended asset, value and spending key, and checked before either final swap signature. The fixed prior funded hash/KEM/signature suites retain their own security bounds. Fresh challenge seeds and a smaller proof do not upgrade existing receiving keys. [Funded receipt and backup analysis](../../spend-proof-decision/RECEIVING.md) · [Historical funded transcript and recipient checks](../../funded-whir/review/REVIEW.md)

Two tighter extraction results illustrate a possible change in the decision. DFMS Theorem 5.1 applies to a three-move, special-sound Merkle commit-and-open protocol. In this TCitH transcript the prover commits to masked responses to two successive challenges before a third challenge chooses the 25 opened parties. The openings are insufficient on their own to recover a degree-125 witness column. A transformation to a single eligible opening challenge or an extractor for the whole adaptive schedule would need proof. CMS Theorem 8.6 instead uses an IOP with round-by-round knowledge. Definition 8.5 requires a polynomial-time extractor from each relevant *prefix* together with a state function controlling the next challenge on arbitrary prover oracles. FR's rewindable whole-protocol extractor does not supply that pair. A compatible BCS reduction with the same `κ≈2^-256.17` would illustrate ideal terms `q²κ≈0.89` and `q³/2⁵¹²≈2^-128` at `q=2¹²⁸`, before constants, query surcharge and multiple targets. Better margins, or a different compiler, could reverse the assurance comparison. The related SmallWood PCS/PIOP and the three-round redesign of SDitH have different transcript commitments.
