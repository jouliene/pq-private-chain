# Packed TCitH for the funded Goldilocks relation

With the three 256-bit parameter screens in Feneuil and Rivain §6.3, the direct degree-seven swap relation fits the existing signed frame with 2,180 B to spare. Its largest proof at the selected geometry is **238,904 B**, including hiding salts and a maximal reachable Merkle multiproof. The least eligible binary tree has **1,048,576** parties and its leaf commitments take **6,241,124,352 B** of field shares. This is input processed by a wallet. It is not proof traffic, a minimum RAM allocation or a measured end-to-end proof time. The equally protected quadratic encoding exceeds the proof allowance at every admissible binary tree size. ## Statement and admissible geometry

Use p = 2⁶⁴−2³²+1. Serialize each field element as exactly one canonical little-endian u64 strictly below p, independent of its magnitude. For N=2ᵇ, require N+s≤p, dD<N and D=s+ℓ−1. One convenient valid domain is the N distinct party points 0,…,N−1 and s secret points Ω = (N,…,N+s−1). A multiplicative-subgroup implementation may instead register distinct field points and a disjoint Ω. Goldilocks supports radix-two multiplicative subgroups only through 2³². Rows at higher b in the model refer to *arbitrary distinct field points*, with no implied radix-two FFT.

The relation supplies public matrix shape, coefficients and the registered 31/34 statement words. Pack the hidden assignment and each gate port vector separately from that shape, in ascending order, padding each vector with zero. Check **every padded coordinate** with an explicit one-term global linear equation. Count its challenge and matrix coefficient. Inputs in the 31/34 public words and the field ONE only appear in the known part of the linear equations. Quadratic gate packs check a·b−c; seventh-power packs check x⁷−y, each with one independent polynomial-batch challenge per packed gate block, including its last partial block. Affine wiring ties each port to its actual private/public coordinates. The hidden assignment is present even when a particular coordinate is used only in a linear equation. This does not replace or truncate the tree or reveal the selected leaves. The accompanying encoding experiment establishes the link to the funded constraints. [Census](../encoding/CENSUS.json) · [Packed relation verifier](../encoding/packed.py)

Let W be the sum of the unpadded group lengths and n the sum of their individually rounded packed lengths. For the swap leg W = 379,125 quadratic or 66,539 degree-seven. For the ordinary payment W = 369,050 or 64,088. For a degree-seven leg at s=101 there are 661 packed private columns, 222 extra zero equations, 45,325 global linear rows and 406,472 sparse private coefficients. The two party roles use a shared fixed parameter registration at the selected geometry. Each proof has its own statement, commitment randomness and challenges.

## Transcript and counting convention

1. Register the encoding, modulus, degree d, field-point order, pack groups/order, N, s, ℓ, ρ, η, τ, hash suite, layout version and association to the ordinary or leg opcode. The verifier must obtain these from its public registration. Absorb this registration plus the entire public statement and suite/domain identifier before generating challenges. Ciphertexts enter through the registered statement's digest check. Use fixed field ordering, length-delimit variable fields and reject noncanonical values, bad lengths or trailing bytes. No forgeable parameter or free-form challenge supplied with the proof is authoritative.
2. Each of the τ trees shares n columns and ρh independent hint columns, with h=ceil((d−1)D/s)+ceil((2s−1)/s). It also shares η independent degree-blinding columns. A witness pack is its degree <s interpolant on Ω plus Ω₀(X)R(X), with Ω₀(X)=∏(X−ω) and uniform R of degree <ℓ. A degree-blinding column is uniform degree ≤D. Below, “independent hints” means independent between mask instances, subject to the single prescribed sum condition in each linear instance.
3. Build each leaf from a fresh private 32-byte opening randomizer and its ordered canonical words (n shares, ρh hints, η masks). Domain-separate leaf and inner hashes and bind tree identity/position. Use SHA3-512 nodes. Aggregate roots in repetition order in a single digest h₁ (for τ=1 this may hash a single fixed root). Reconstruct the individual roots from the openings. Do not reuse masks or salts between proofs/owners. The paper's Fig. 3 writes randomized commitments; its Eq. (18) omits a separate salt term. Our candidate charges and reveals the randomizer for every opened party.
4. From h₁ and context expand independent uniform field entries for η×(n+ρh) matrix Γ per tree. Reject biased u64 samples ≥p. Compute ξ=ΓQ+u, where Q comprises the committed witness and hint polynomials and u the degree masks. Commit the ordered polynomials by digest h₂, e.g. by hashing evaluations at the first D+1 party points of every polynomial in fixed order.
5. Derive ρ sets of independent field weights for *all* polynomial blocks and global linear rows from the previous transcript including h₂, domain-separated from Γ. Each polynomial broadcast α is a weighted sum of (a·b−c) and/or (x⁷−y), plus a random polynomial of degree ≤dD vanishing on Ω. Each linear broadcast α′ is the dot product of the shared witness with weighted public matrix columns interpolated in the slots, plus a degree ≤2s+ℓ−2 mask whose slot values sum to zero. The slot sum of α′ must equal the equally weighted public right-hand side. Commit all broadcasts, in canonical evaluation form at the first respective degree+1 party points, in digest h₃.
6. From the entire h₁,h₂,h₃ transcript derive a uniform ℓ-subset of distinct party indices per tree. Use unbiased rejection and a fixed order (ascending when serializing). The proof consists of the three fixed digests, followed for each tree by ℓ salted committed rows; leaf-to-root sibling hashes once each in canonical level/index order; s further evaluations of every ξ, at Ω; then each pair of tails described below. The network proof-length field is charged by the existing wrapper. No indices, extra roots, challenge matrices, private seeds, polynomials of the witness or terminal vector are separately transmitted.
7. Recompute local ξ, α, α′ at the opened parties. Combine ξ with its s evaluations at Ω to interpolate and verify h₂. Combine each α with its s prescribed zeros at Ω and (d−1)D transmitted evaluations at the lowest available disjoint party points. For α′ transmit its first s−1 slot values and the s−1 needed extra party evaluations. Derive its final slot from the required public sum. The two tails contain (d−1)D and 2s−2 words respectively per MPC repetition. Distinct evaluation grids give nonsingular interpolation even when the sum-functional coefficient at a predetermined monomial is zero. Check all three digests against the reconstructed roots and broadcasts. These are the terminal checks.

**Mask degree detail.** Protocol 6 writes shifted hints with a shorter final polynomial. A single common degree test need only establish degree ≤D. We use an equivalent covering of the coefficient positions that keeps every final mask within its declared degree even if all hints have the full degree D. For a target mask precursor of degree t≥D use offsets oⱼ=min(js,t−D) for j=0,…,ceil((t+1−ℓ)/s)−1. Combine hints by ΣX^oⱼ Hⱼ(X). Given the ℓ opened values, divide out ∏ᵢ∈I(X−eᵢ) from each free variation. The intervals [oⱼ,oⱼ+s−1] cover all coefficients 0,…,t−ℓ. The linear map is surjective, hence takes uniform free coefficients to a uniform conditional mask. The sum may overlap in the last interval without changing this conclusion. Use t=dD−s and multiply by Ω₀ for the zero mask. Use t=2s+ℓ−2 for a sum-zero mask and condition ΣωM(ω)=0. This condition is nontrivial given any admissible opening because degree D permits interpolation on I∪Ω. The maker can solve one coefficient before commitment (the constant-shift coefficient has multiplier s≠0). A malformed precommitted linear hint introduces an additive offset fixed *before* the random row weights; a violated linear row still passes one batch with probability at most 1/p. This is a constructive specialization of the paper with the same h; the paper does not provide a separate QROM theorem for this instantiation.

## Resource and security interpretation

For each concrete geometry we calculate the least ρ, η, τ satisfying respectively p^ρ≥2ᵀ, p^η≥2ᵀ·binom(N,D+2), and binom(N,ℓ)^τ≥2ᵀ·binom(dD,ℓ)^τ using integers. The paper's interactive single-repetition bound is p^−ρ+(1−p^−ρ)binom(dD,ℓ)/binom(N,ℓ)+binom(N,D+2)/p^η. For the selected τ=1 swap geometry the individual negative log₂ probabilities are approximately 320.000, 281.173 and 256.170 bits (batch, degree and subset). Their interactive sum gives 256.170 bits before computational reduction losses. Its §6.3 separate screens address the multi-round staged grinding heuristic. Setting T=256 is a classical probability screen with generic square-root-search headroom. It does not prove 128-bit quantum knowledge extraction. Theorem 2 gives an interactive, rewindable extractor and honest-verifier hiding under PRG/commitment assumptions. Applying it to computationally hiding randomized commitments, three rounds of Fiat–Shamir challenges, NIZK simulation in the QROM, adaptive spending statements, two independently signed legs and reused historical note/receipt primitives needs additional reasoning. Stronger proof hashes cannot raise the assurance of the unchanged Poseidon commitments, ciphertext projection or owner authorization. A full package also requires local note opening and receipt binding at their original layers.

The exact maximal frontier is 2−ℓ+Σⱼ₌₁…ᵇ₋₁ min(2ʲ,ℓ), attained when the selected index prefixes disperse maximally (e.g. bit-reversed indices 0…ℓ−1). Formula (18) with canonical bytes, 3 digests and the explicit salt replaces the paper's approximate path and field-entropy terms. For the 238,904 B swap proof the components are 132,200 witness; 10,000 hints; 6,600 degree-mask openings; 26,664 ξ tail; 30,000 α tail; 8,000 α′ tail; 800 salt; 24,448 siblings; and 192 Fiat–Shamir digest bytes. Fixed leg and ordinary wrappers charge 21,060 and 3,841 B respectively. At the same geometry an ordinary proof is 234,104 B. The GGM variant in the paper, with its explicit witness correction of at least 8W B, cannot fit this signed proof even with degree-seven encoding.

The public verifier computes sparse row-weighted combinations, evaluates the public coefficient packs at the opened points, recomputes η degree-check products at each opening and interpolates the two terminal broadcasts. At the selected swap geometry it needs 251,153 sampled field challenges; 2,032,360 multiply-accumulates to project sparse private coefficients; 586,575 degree-check multiply-accumulates; and 82,625 linear dot products' scalar multiply-accumulates. Straight barycentric evaluation of the weighted public pack coefficients alone takes up to 8,345,125 more scalar MACs given the point-dependent interpolation weights. Counts in the JSON include a simple schedule which may reuse gate powers across MPC repetitions. A prover must also prepare and hash all N committed rows, compute the degree check and broadcast polynomials, store or regenerate openings, and create actual receipt and outer authorization bytes. Horner streaming can use bounded memory; a fast FFT needs an eligible domain and scratch/storage. A count of input words or projections does not measure a whole wallet or verifier. **Geometric trade-off.** With default hashes, the best grid result through N=2¹⁶ costs 325,488 B, through 2¹⁹ costs 253,456 B, and through 2²⁰ costs 238,904 B; degree 7 SLG1 is meant throughout. All admissible integer s,ℓ,τ for b≤20, including those outside the search grid, are checked against the respective decision thresholds by a second integer certificate. At 2²⁴ the best grid profile costs 198,432 B. A proof at or below the live reference of 139,386 B first occurs at 2⁴¹ with a constructive 137,776 B example (s=65,ℓ=8,τ=1,η=46). This profile hashes about 19.67 PB of field shares. At the reference threshold the two broadcast tails alone force s≤436; the six packed groups then need at least 155 witness columns, plus ≥40 hints (s≥2 at this threshold) and ≥5 masks. Hence at least 3.51 PB of leaf field input at *any* matching binary-tree geometry. At N near the field limit, a low byte count alone says little about provability. A 48-byte hash sensitivity saves 6,160 B at 2²⁰; generic quantum collision resistance drops from about 171 to 128 bits. All changed widths require a distinct registration.

For a labelled stress case set T=768 to illustrate the scale that a generic three-challenge q⁶ reduction might suggest at q=2¹²⁸. This is a deliberately optimistic *arithmetic* illustration retaining 64-byte digests and 32-byte salts; the 512-bit digest itself cannot provide 768-bit transcript entropy. No assertion that this reduction proves knowledge here or is a necessary attack cost follows. The best 2²⁰ grid profile costs 755,240 B. Even the most optimistic all-domain lower bound for the specified degree-seven binary-tree leg is 245,818 B. The live-sized frame therefore discriminates sharply between assurance assumptions.

## Integer lower bounds and validation

For any power-of-two N≤p, let K=τℓ and β=T/64 (T=256 or 768). Then K≥β+1 since the subset odds are smaller than N^ℓ, and ρ≥β+1 since p<2⁶⁴. For s≥2, d≥2, N/(D+2)≥N/(dD−ℓ+1)≥2^(T/K). The degree screen and binom(N,k)≥(N/k)^k imply η≥β+β(D+2)/K. If ℓ<2s, the largest frontier has at least ℓlog₂(N/(2ℓ)) hashes, and dD−ℓ+1≥2ℓ, so τ such frontiers carry at least 64T bytes. Hence wire ≥ 64T + 8[K W/s + βs²/K + (β+ρ(d+1))s −ρ(d+1)], using the minimal ρ. After s=K^(2/3)u the positive terms increase with K. Set K=β+1, use the tangent bound 1/s≥2/r−s/r², and minimize that quadratic exactly over real s. For s=1 the open witness alone costs ≥8W; if ℓ≥2s it costs ≥16W. This certifies both quadratic encodings over the signed cap at 256 and both degree-seven encodings over that cap at 768. The ordinary wrapper has more headroom than the signed-leg budget. These are geometry/encoding/profile-specific limits.

The separate finite certificate uses the broadcast terms and opened columns to bound all possible positive s and ℓ at a requested byte threshold. It cheaply lowers η and τ with integer powers-of-two bounds, then applies exact binomial repricing to every surviving case. At each fixed N,s,ℓ the least allowed τ,η,ρ gives the smallest encoding.
