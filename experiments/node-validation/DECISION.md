# Payment validation for independent nodes

**Decision: defer integration of both tested constructions. Keep CP03 as the executable payment reference.** Pursue a verifier that exploits the fixed structure of the registered payment matrices before investing in a complete masked prover. The reason for prioritizing that route is measurable: one of its wire schedules fits a block-only home node and has a plausible wallet memory layout, while the implemented generic verifier already exceeds the catch-up CPU allowance. The transparent aggregate has a favorable post-inclusion network model but requires a stronger outer verifier and a producer capable of discharging a much larger workload. This decision leaves both research directions open. No new complete payment was proved by either backend, and no new backend was installed in the wallet or ledger.

The baseline is `7c2d0fc8b68c566d13e3e341c1f49bacaad527f4`. CP03's existing complete spends and encrypted receipts remain the functional oracle. The 15.39% transport experiment and wallet synchronization are historical results. The validation experiments change no reference protocol code, fee rule, proof parameter, consensus rule or wallet format. Global blockchain development remains **ACTIVE**.

## Primary route: authorization in the wallet

An independent wallet would prove both owners' authority, both canonical occurrence nullifiers, membership in the funded depth-40 tree with each position below the public count, both output commitments and bounded integer conservation including the fee. The verifier must select the ordinary 2x2 program, bind the full canonical 528-byte statement before challenges, and check both actual encrypted receipts against the statement's two slot-specific digests. Binding only the 49 projected field elements would leave nonce/receipt context unchecked. At admission the node independently checks the chain and valid root/count window, minimum fee, unspent and distinct nullifiers, public state effects and atomic accounting. Receipt delivery/decryptability, recipient anonymity and network privacy have additional assumptions. The proposal preserves CP03 note, receiving and nullifier semantics.

**Measured and executed.** The real registered AIR lowers to 614,387 R1CS constraints and 612,532 private variables. An honest full public-fixture trace satisfied the resulting arithmetic; changing one trace word produced 33 nonzero residuals. A straightforward EF5 lift enforcing the base field separately for every trace word would increase the constraint count to 19,587,059. The candidate also tested a less expensive committed subfield check that binds four masked base-field evaluations in the final source linear form. Two small, real masked R1CS proofs exercise the full transcript structure on their respective small circuits. They cover the masked outer polynomials, all three terminal claims, the dense batched form, a real code switch with its OOD answer, an independently blinded terminal and authenticated openings. Neither small circuit is the payment relation. [Lowering and full-size component timings](dimensions/README.md) · [Transcript map, parameter sweep and controls](primary/README.md).

**Derived full-payment wire.** Independent recalculation finds a separate-salted-tree schedule with **262,850.011 expected bytes** and a **272,556-byte maximum** for the complete body, including framing, the statement, both ciphertexts, every direct message, salts, openings and Merkle frontiers. This assumes the specified implicit grammar and fixed verifier-owned parameters. The raw values, all tree nodes and salts across its stages occupy 2,350,907,072 bytes before code, matrices, scratch and runtime. On actual public coefficients, encoding two full-length first-stage rows took 0.330 and 0.335 CPU seconds each; each row contains 83,886,080 output bytes and was cross-checked by direct evaluation at four points. Sixteen such transforms are required at the first stage. A separate paired-terminal schedule projects 168,608 bytes expected, with a maximum of 172,540, at the cost of 136.90 GB of raw encoded values. That is a positive tenfold sizing sensitivity, with neither an executed complete payment nor a measured peak RSS. [Independent schedule calculations](review/ASSESSMENT.md).

**Node CPU is the discriminator.** The generic verifier precomputes its public sparse matrices once. On three different published statement inputs, contracting 6,047,930 nonzero entries into the challenge-specific public linear form took 0.186–0.196 CPU seconds. Including four guard contributions and the first fold of the main form brought the measured time to **0.245–0.261 CPU seconds per payment**, before all later opening checks, receipts, state and I/O. Public challenge tapes are reproducible diagnostic inputs; the measurement includes no private witness. Live traffic alone permits 0.631 CPU seconds per payment on the selected home allocation. Replaying one year in 90 days while staying live requires 16.031 payments/s and permits 0.12476 CPU seconds. On those three original timings, the component alone projects to 232–257 days to catch up with two dedicated cores, or 88–95 days with all four and zero costs elsewhere. This excludes the tested generic verifier from that service profile. It says nothing about the running time of a future structured verifier. [Raw per-instance CPU results](dimensions/MATRIX.json) · [Role calculations](roles/README.md).

**Security gate.** The paper's interactive reduction masks the three terminal values over EF5 and reduces the joint claim to a linear form over the same encoded witness. The retained code handles this form directly. A hiding PCS that exposes an arbitrary MLE point does not implement this interface. The extra four-challenge subfield guard has a specific fixed-list interactive error calculation. Its conditional simulation with later openings, the exact compiled transcript's noninteractive knowledge argument, multi-payment/receipt-correlated privacy and concrete losses in the QROM remain open. The modeled interactive error is not a post-quantum security rating. The complete byte budget and generic-verifier measurement justify deferring expensive full-payment integration under the declared profile. [Security correspondence and upstream references](primary/README.md).

### Proposed complete public transcript

The complete candidate would instantiate the following fixed statement and transcript. The small proofs execute these message classes on much smaller matrices; the payment schedule and all of its intermediate oracles are budgeted, not implemented end to end.

1. The verifier chooses the versioned relation, field/basis, domain, matrix and program identities, guard layout, hash, code geometries and query schedule from the installed profile. Parse the entire expected payment statement, compare its canonical serialization and arity, and recompute both receipt digests. Absorb these public bindings, including chain and nonce, before the initial commitment can influence any challenge. Matrix dimensions and digests are dependencies of the public verifier, never a program chosen by the sender.
2. The first salted RS root commits the ordered private witness, full-extension inner and outer masks, encoding randomness, and four subfield blinds. After the commitment, the prover sends the outer mask sum. Absorb it, then draw all four base guard challenges together; the prover sends four canonical masked base responses. These must later bind to this same initial message. A missing guard would admit extension witnesses outside the intended Goldilocks relation.
3. After absorbing the four guard replies, draw the outer sumcheck challenges (epsilon and the beta vector). For all 21 rounds send the masked polynomial, check its Boolean sum against the previous claim and draw the next EF5 challenge. Exclude 0 and 1 at the last outer point. Send the three masked terminal matrix contractions; the random joint claim includes these, the remaining sumcheck mask and the four guard equations. Only the public matrix contributions are subtracted. The terminal masks are independent full-field multiples of the vanishing polynomial at the last coordinate.
4. Each inner folding step binds the incoming **linear form** to the same encoded witness, with a fresh mask-sum message, masked round polynomials and transcript-derived challenges. Each code switch sends the next salted root and a padded, masked out-of-domain answer. The sampled prior columns, salts and ordered Merkle frontier must authenticate under the prior root and reduce to the same new claim. At the terminal, bind an independent fresh blind oracle, send its blind target and the challenge-weighted combination of both messages and seeds, check the final form, authenticate the terminal openings of both trees and check the encoded values. Canonical lengths, multiplicity handling, labels and EOF are part of this wire model.

The source argument concerns uniformly queried interactive oracles with specific randomized encodings. Fresh coins, the allowed observer's complete joint view and the pre-challenge list are essential premises. A concrete Fiat–Shamir instantiation also exposes the sequence of roots, authentication siblings, opened salts, public ciphertext-related statement and repeated/adaptive payments to the observer. The [message-by-message correspondence](primary/README.md) and [independent review](review/ASSESSMENT.md) distinguish the source lemmas from the added guard and the remaining compilation work.

## Fallback: validation of public wallet proofs in a block

Each submitting wallet would send its own complete public CP03 authorization proof and ciphertexts. The builder could verify and aggregate without a spending secret. The block-only node would check one transparent proof against a locally chosen guest and parent, and check available, ordered public effects and ciphertexts. Invalid or conflicting inner payments, journal substitution, unresolved recursion/accelerator assumptions, delivery withholding and historical availability require explicit handling. A transaction digest alone cannot establish that the wallet proof passed or that a recipient can recover its ciphertext.

**Executed discriminator.** RISC Zero v3.0.6 produced a 400,392-byte transparent **accelerator** seal for the 67 actual sponge states of one CP03 Merkle leaf. The proof used 15.24 seconds elapsed and 30.54 CPU seconds with two workers. A fresh process verified the expected claim; changed public input and damaged cryptographic proof both failed. Parsing 96 existing genuine ordinary payments gave a floor of **3,600,891 nonfixed Merkle permutations** before transcript, field and state work. Even with cross-payment packing at the largest approved accelerator geometry, at least **2,749** internal seals must be generated and resolved for that workload. These measurements price the tested accelerator configuration. A 200 kB final block proof is still only a sizing input. [Real seal, resource evidence, dependencies and reproduction](aggregate/README.md).

**Security and production-cost blockers.** The stock outer proof has a 2^−100 optimistic FRI query term and an upstream 97-bit conjectured target for a documented geometry. It does not establish the study's 128-bit interactive-component screen or a quantum composition with CP03. Choosing a larger hash or dropping Groth16 alone cannot repair those parameters. The full fixed guest, authenticated transparent recursion/assumption discharge, extraction/composition, actual final receipt size, and timely production of a complete block remain unverified. The small circuit is genuine; its cost and the pinned source parameters suffice to reject integration of this concrete fallback. They do not preclude stronger block arguments or specialized hardware.

## Participation at 100 million offers/year

The home profile is a **research assumption**: four physical cores with two core-seconds/s allocated to validation, 16 GiB RAM, 4 TiB usable disk, and 100/20 Mb/s service down/up with 80% available to the application. One simulated year has 365 days. The central case includes 100% of offers with 96 payments per block, one outbound full copy per active phase and six inventory peers. The 95% case keeps all offered wallet uploads and mempool costs. The owner supplied the workload and set no annual traffic ceiling.

|---|---:|---:|
| One originating wallet upload per payment, with framing | 262,862 B | 1,751,277.75 B mean |
| Wallet submissions summed across all senders/year | 26.286 TB | 175.128 TB |

| Cold validator, one-year link-only recovery alongside live traffic | 33.20 days | 0.82 days |

At 95% inclusion the canonical archives project to 24.977 and 0.675 TB/year respectively. The fallback's canonical archive assumes historical acceptance can be rechecked via the aggregate alone. Keeping independently re-verifiable inner evidence for included payments increases its one-copy requirement to **175.350 TB/year** at 100%. Reorganizations may require retransmitting an evicted submission. Direct builder submission exposes connection/timing metadata and depends on reachable independent intake. Both designs still need available public receipts, commitments and spentness data. The pruned-disk figures are engineering projections; remote pruning and its sustained I/O were never demonstrated. The role model keeps the 256-block recent history, staged fork and replay costs visible.

A wallet restored from its retained-key backup must fetch and scan about **502.242 GB of common history** for one fully included year before charging endpoint/query framing and retry. At 80 Mb/s the one-year transfer alone takes 13.95 hours. It also needs receipt trials, local witness construction, authenticated branch history and the selected validating source. Unknown receiving keys cannot be recovered by this procedure. Neither construction demonstrated new receipt or subsequent spending. The working CP03 cycle remains separate. ## Independent reproduction

A reviewer who did not implement either probe reran both from a separate clean checkout on the same machine. At the masked-route freeze all three full-size public matrix digests and all eight independent direct polynomial checks matched. The reviewer measured **0.2234–0.2286 CPU seconds** for the guarded matrix component and generated and verified a fresh small guarded proof in separate processes. At the final executable freeze `6efae8b29ed9a854768a5e5ec5d69fbceee216c0`, a freshly linked accelerator generated the same 400,392-byte seal on the fixed public input in **15.50 seconds elapsed**. Its verifier accepted the retained seal and rejected the changed claim and corrupted seal. Independently scanning 96 bodies reproduced the workload file byte for byte. The reviewer reused pinned dependency builds, including its separately approved CP03 reference installation. Review corrected the documented order of the mask sum and subfield guard messages to match the executed transcript. ## Next decision-changing experiment

Implement a verifier-owned evaluator specialized to the fixed ordinary-payment program that computes the **already folded** matrix linear claim, including the guarded subfield equations. Exploit repeated Poseidon, membership and range-check row structure and the multilinear form of geometric guard weights. Keep full-statement/receipt and program/profile binding unchanged. Compare its output at identical independently chosen EF5 challenges against the sparse oracle in this repository for the complete 2x2 circuit. Validate the identity symbolically, then test timing in a fresh process with setup charged separately. The first gate is to bring this component below **0.12476 CPU s/payment with explicit headroom** for the remaining verifier and ledger work on the stated two-core/90-day scenario. In parallel the guard requires a joint simulator and a construction-correspondent Fiat–Shamir/knowledge plan. Only a survivor should proceed to three genuine new payments, public verification, receipt and recipient spending. Wallet recovery tuning and full consensus are outside this decision.
