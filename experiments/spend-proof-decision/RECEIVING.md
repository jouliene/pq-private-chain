# What the receipts conceal and what the observer can still link

**Offline receiving works in the funded experiment.** A 32-byte backup derives the spend credential and a separate deterministic ML-KEM-1024 key pair. An exported 1,601-byte address carries the version, a 32-byte spend public commitment and a 1,568-byte encapsulation key. The counterparty learns this address. The sender makes a fresh encapsulation for each output. Seed restoration can decrypt recorded outputs and reconstruct their nullifiers and witnesses with the common branch history. The baseline reviewer identified the two actual incoming swap commitments and newly spent both. Later compromise of the static receiving key allows retrospective scanning of recorded receipts. The off-chain negotiation journal and private terms are additional data for resuming an unfinished swap. | Field in a funded payment output | Public bytes | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Version, suite, diversifier index | 7 | One u8, one u16 and one u32. Every observed tuple was (1,1,0). |
| Component length prefixes | 12 | Three little-endian u32 lengths. All three payload sizes are visible. |
| ML-KEM-1024 ciphertext | 1,568 | Encapsulates the shared secret for the supplied address. |
| Encrypted note | 128 | Native amount and token quantity as two u64s, rho32, opening32 and spend public commitment32, plus a 16-byte tag. |
| Encrypted memo | 77 | Asset ID32 and 29 bytes for a change marker or padding, plus a 16-byte tag. |
| **One receipt** | **1,792** | Two per transfer and four per swap. |

For each of the two ChaCha20-Poly1305 invocations, the implementation computes 44 bytes by hashing the ASCII domain `wallet-aead`, a big-endian u32 block counter starting at zero, and the shared secret, label and little-endian suite. The first 32 bytes are the key and the next 12 the nonce. The labels are `note-aead` and `memo-aead`. The seven public header bytes are the associated data. The context relies on per-output encapsulation randomness and key separation. The KEM seed derives from a domain-separated Poseidon2 hash of the wallet seed. The chain binds the ordered receipts through the public digest of `qnero/ct`, the count, and each length and ciphertext, using the pinned field-oriented Poseidon2 byte encoding. It also checks the format/registered suite. The circuit accepts this public digest. An honest receiver must independently decrypt, validate the expected spend public key, rebuild the typed commitment and compare with the funded output. A payer able to prove a valid spend could create an unusable receipt. The two swap wallets check their incoming amounts, assets and ability to spend before signing. **Observed from public bytes.** Across the new funded swap and one subsequent transfer there were six distinct encapsulations, note ciphertexts and memo ciphertexts. Neither transfer nullifier equalled any of the four swap output commitments. The reviewer separately demonstrated a real funding link using the recipient's local restored wallet. The two swap frames disclose that they belong together, the network, sides, which side pays the fee, expiry, the shared salted terms commitment, both authorization keys and signatures and their two anchor references. The swap format identifies the first output of each leg as the counterparty's payment and the second as change. An ordinary private transfer discloses its fee and five zero operation fields; supply changes disclose asset and quantity. Fresh ciphertext and proof randomness cannot erase an exact retransmission, a repeated spending nullifier on a competing branch, or the information held by the two negotiating parties. The common history request hides owned-note indices; a contacted node can observe connection, timing and access patterns. No anonymity-set size follows from this sample. **Security boundary.** FIPS 203 defines ML-KEM and its parameter sets. RFC 8439 defines this AEAD and its nonce requirement. Neither supplies a key-privacy, robustness or joint simulation theorem for the spend proof, multi-recipient ledger receipts, authorized viewing, wallet error behavior and subsequent spends. Grubbs, Maram and Paterson analyze anonymity and robustness of particular KEM–DEM transformations, including implicit rejection and pre-standard Kyber. Applying a result here would require matching the final ML-KEM variant, deterministic key derivation, two derived AEAD key/nonce pairs, honest/adversarial key registration, decryption interface, transcript and quantum adversary model. Using ML-KEM-1024 by itself does not assign 128 post-quantum bits to recipient unlinkability. The same receipt system and qualifications apply to the challenger.
