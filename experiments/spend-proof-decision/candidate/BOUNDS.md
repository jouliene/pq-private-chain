# What the oracle change establishes

The emitted Merkle nodes are 48 bytes, exactly SHA3-384 over a typed leaf, internal-node pair or general field input. Merkle cap words are observed using the pinned injective seven-byte chunk encoding (48 bytes = six full seven-byte chunks plus a six-byte remainder). With an ideal 384-bit function the usual single-target generic *collision* exponents are 192 classical and 128 quantum oracle queries. For a fixed digest the ideal *preimage* exponents are 384 and 192. These are distinct events. Replacing the old 32-byte digest improves that generic arbitrary-collision comparison from 128 classical / about 85.3 quantum queries. The comparison does not establish that any such collision lets an adversary spend a particular funded note, nor that an adversary must find such a collision. The key, ciphertext, nullifier, inventory and original algebraic public-input hashes retain their own bottlenecks.

Both registered profiles have degree bits 14 and rate bits 5, so the largest LDE domain has 524,288 positions. With 64 independent uniform-position queries sampled with replacement against one **pre-fixed** agreement set of size at most 131,072, the chance every query hits that set is at most (1/4)^64 = 2^-128. At one-half agreement the same calculation yields 2^-64. If the sampler reduces a uniform canonical Goldilocks word modulo the LDE size, residue zero is one atom heavier: the quarter-set worst-case mass becomes (131072 × floor(p/524288) + 1) / p for p = 2^64−2^32+1. Under 100 million independent attempts, or just a union bound over that many fixed events, subtract log2(100,000,000) ≈ 26.58 bits from either exponent. This example is conditioned on uniform underlying draws and a pre-fixed agreement set. Neither list soundness for the committed FRI codewords nor opening knowledge for this noninteractive PLONK transcript follows. The 64 draws are not proven independent of an adaptively selected statement and prover commitments in a quantum random-oracle experiment. A reduction must account for the actual challenger's full absorb/squeeze transcript, the sampling adjustment and all Fiat–Shamir queries.

The field extension still has order p² (just under 2^128). A degree-d polynomial evaluated at a uniformly sampled extension point has a basic root-probability upper bound d/p² only in the applicable game and under the necessary independence hypotheses. At d=16384 the simple term is roughly 2^-114; the relevant degrees, batch multiplicities, and independence of two challenge repetitions must be derived from the actual proof argument. The example does not identify a successfully exploitable 114-bit forgery. Increasing FRI queries leaves this field unchanged.

The experiment keeps the original algebraic inner public-input hash; the prover and verifier observe that digest via the new external challenger. Replacing it by a byte hash would require appropriate public-input gate logic and independent checking of all public data. The 16-word SHAKE256 transcript map has eight rate words and eight retained state words; rejection sampling removes the bias of reducing SHAKE output modulo p. The FRI draw-to-index mapping is a separate reduction. This non-bijective pseudopermutation, row blinding, FRI leaf hiding and any simulator/extractor remain research assumptions. A complete adaptive multi-user PQ work bound for the measured candidate is unavailable.
