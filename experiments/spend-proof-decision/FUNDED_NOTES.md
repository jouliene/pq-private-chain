# Consequences for existing funds

**Decision for the current chain.** Keep the active MNG1 and SLG1 verifier registrations and the existing single spent set. The evidence supports continued research transfers, mint/burn and swaps. Wallet owners have no migration action. Nodes can replay already accepted states under the rules in force at their heights. A consensus upgrade would need an explicit network/version activation and retained historical registration data. The challenger deliberately ran in a separate test context and cannot be submitted to the current chain.

| Change under consideration | Consequence for a funded owner and for validation |
|---|---|
| Only the proof/argument transcript changes | A holder can make a new proof from the old private opening, witness and spend keys when the new relation accepts exactly that legacy note. An old proof does not acquire new validity by changing its version bytes. Every version consuming the same liability must derive the same canonical nullifier and use the same selected-branch spent set. A new proof cannot improve the binding of an old commitment, the confidentiality of a recorded old receipt, or the security of a historical mint. |
| New receipt or address suite | Version recipients and exports. Old ciphertexts remain necessary to restore old incoming notes. Keep the old decoder and deterministic key inventory for their lifetime. A later change of receiving key cannot erase copies of the old ciphertext or add forward secrecy to the static old key. |
| New note, nullifier or tree definition | A new commitment to the same amount is not evidence of ownership of an old liability. Define a versioned transition proving the old opening, canonical old nullifier and old membership, plus the exact amount and asset relation into new commitments and the native fee. Debit the old liability and create its replacement atomically, constrain mint/burn deltas, and undo both on a reorg. Specify historical anchor eligibility, synchronization, old/new receipts and recovery before activation. A sunset requires a policy for unconverted balances, and accepting legacy authorizations indefinitely preserves their assumptions. A bridge cannot retroactively establish a unique opening if legacy binding fails. |
| Swap in progress at a version boundary | The two original signatures bind network, terms, expiry, fee side and hashes of the complete ordered leg bytes. Re-proving a leg changes that signed message. Both owners must approve any new authorization, or the upgrade must explicitly define survival of the old transaction until expiry. The compact offline experiment restores the *same* original bytes and signatures. Reusing an economic intention across distinct messages would require separate checks. |

**What was exercised.** The matched challenger consumed legacy funded openings, produced spendable private outputs on the same note rules, restored both owners and re-spent in both proof profiles. Its old fixed supply was 70, its signed managed registration and mint followed by burn left 330 outstanding. The full K384 payment and two swap frames exceeded the active admission limit. No owner funds were moved between live networks. The independently replayed live trial continues to use the original registrations. A research compatibility test provides evidence about opening formats and nullifiers at the tested values. It cannot establish the soundness of other histories, alternative-opening resistance or a migration deadline.
