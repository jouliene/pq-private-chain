# What a payment exposes

**A node that sees both authorizations can link a stale payment to its replacement.** In two published one-real-input trials, each replacement preserved exactly the first nullifier and changed the second nullifier, both output commitments and both ciphertexts. A separate DLF1 conflict preserved both real nullifiers even though the sender changed the destination and amount. The link identifies shared funding. Identifying the same commercial intent requires more evidence. Fixed-size receipts, hiding proof rows and archive pruning leave these public comparisons available. ## Conditional cryptographic claims

The native commitment is a hash of a hidden note inner value and amount. Its inner value binds the recipient spend identifier, rho and random blinding. Output rho derives from both input nullifiers and the output index, so its secrecy cannot be credited as entropy. The real nullifier uses the recipient's secret nullifier key and the note's rho and blinding. Hiding amounts, spend keys, a Merkle membership path and the real/dummy indicators from the DLF1 proof requires zero knowledge for this instantiated, repeatedly used circuit with auxiliary public outputs. DLF1 requests both random witness blinding rows and salted witness-dependent FRI leaves. These source flags and a conjectured FRI query screen alone establish neither an adaptive multi-proof simulator nor an anonymity-bit estimate. The archive additionally needs recursive authorization soundness. [DLF1 profile](../../payment-architecture/direct/profile.rs) · [Note derivations](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/crates/qnero-note-core/src/note.rs) · [FRI soundness assumptions](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1071) · [Concrete analysis](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1161)

The pinned recipient implementation uses RustCrypto `ml-kem 0.3.2`, ML-KEM-1024 and deterministic encapsulation fed derived fresh randomness. Two separately labelled ChaCha20-Poly1305 encryptions protect the note and padded memo. Current AEAD associated data covers version, suite and diversifier index. Ordered context is checked separately by the proof-authorized digest of the final receipts and the receiver's equality check against the selected commitment. The address encodes spend identifier and encapsulation key. The ciphertext carries the recipient spend identifier inside the encrypted payload. Address presentation, malformed keys, malicious combinations of spend/encapsulation identifiers, voluntary disclosure, decapsulation errors and response timing all require an explicit observer definition. [KEM adapter](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/crates/qnero-pqcrypto/src/ml_kem.rs) · [AEAD codec](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/crates/qnero-pqcrypto/src/note_encryption.rs) · [Recipient check](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/crates/qnero-notes/src/encrypt.rs)

A suitable **honest-participant privacy test** compares two equal-public-leakage histories with alternate hidden recipients, funding or asset values. Hold public versions, fees, schedule, membership availability, disclosure to corrupt parties, retry equality pattern and eventual inclusion fixed. Sample honest challenge keys independently, allow other addresses and adaptively chosen sends, and specify what recipient-scan outputs a caller can observe. Querying the challenge receiver for a plaintext or exposing its seed trivially discloses the answer. A malicious recipient sees and can disclose its own opening, identify that transaction and correlate an off-chain response. Its decryption feedback belongs in the adversary view whenever the protocol exposes it. The remaining claim concerns distinguishing the two distributions by an external adversary. It requires commitment hiding and nullifier pseudorandomness, proof zero knowledge, a multi-user recipient/key-private KEM/DEM argument accounting for the content that includes the recipient's own spend identifier, and bounds on receipt authentication errors and auxiliary data. A corrupted sender already knows its destination and amounts. Adaptive corruption with seed exposure gives no retrospective secrecy for the corresponding incoming notes. Unconditioned network-level anonymity is outside this game. [Key privacy and robust PKE](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/708) · [Component KEM standard](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final)

**Quantum qualification.** Maram and Xagawa prove anonymity for the Kyber construction and associated hybrid encryption they model under stated post-quantum assumptions. Transferring that result needs an explicit mapping to final FIPS 203's transforms, the pinned key-generation/encapsulation wrappers, the two AEAD messages, multi-user model and concrete query/corruption limits; recipient public-key anonymity is stronger than message confidentiality. A quantum payment claim further needs the exact hash, proof simulation/extraction and quantum random-oracle composition across adaptive wallet transcripts and both proof layers. A classical observer can perform the equalities measured above. Giving it a quantum computer cannot hide a public equality. The earlier EC+PQ receipt hedge would need a versioned combiner and separate recipient-anonymity analysis. [Post-Quantum Anonymity of Kyber](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1696) · [FIPS 203 and its change history](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.203.pdf) · [Multi-round Fiat–Shamir in the QROM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05207) · [Hybrid receipt assessment](../../payment-architecture/review/RECEIVING.md)

## Fixed native and token lanes

The preregistered TKN1 version places two balances, native and one defined token, in every note. The amended registration allows a real note plus a dummy with zero native and token value, or two real notes. This removes the original design's requirement to reveal two real inputs on every transfer and permits a lone privately received vector to fund a spend when it carries the fee. The credible funding set can still be tiny when public genesis amounts and keys are known. The hidden token lane and raw blinding feed a typed digest that replaces the existing randomness in the commitment and nullifier. [Registered TKN1 relation](../candidate/PROFILE.md) Use the same public verifier/profile, input/output count, receipt length, fee and wire tags for native-only, token-only and mixed transfers within that version. Bind both hidden balances into the note commitment and authenticated receipt, prove bounded integer conservation per lane, charge fees in native units, and have the restoring recipient recompute the exact versioned commitment from both balances. Every comparison must allow the adversary to know the network/profile and its asset universe. A user who only uses a token-specific chain advertises membership in that smaller pool. A tiny public funding distribution, an issuance event, owner information or a known invoice can imply an otherwise hidden balance. Different wallet policies for fee funding, amount selection and reanchoring can produce different observable arrival or retry patterns even with a fixed 2×2 shape. An implementation preserving pinned TKN1 funding across a reanchor would repeat the real nullifier for a one-real transfer or both real nullifiers for a two-real transfer. The standalone candidate has no persistent retry outbox. [Monetary scope](../money/REVIEW.md) · [Native retry evidence](OBSERVATIONS.json)

For the isolated inventory, genesis, verifier identity, typed note commitments, profile-aware decoding and branch eligibility should keep prior native notes, old authorizations and native D8 certificates outside the new relation. The plan preserves suite-1 receipt framing and reinterprets the old encrypted asset-ID word as the second balance. A standalone ciphertext header therefore cannot dispatch the correct semantic decoder. The recipient must obtain the enclosing profile from the authenticated selected history and recompute its typed commitment, including for token amount zero. TKN1 shuffles the two outputs and puts an empty padded memo in both. It can recover owned balances from the seed, but this fixture supplies no authenticated self-change label or recovery of private invoices. [TKN1 wallet](../candidate/wallet.rs) · [Pair encryption](../candidate/pair_codec.rs) Reusing a public address or identifiable funding claim across networks can still link the owner's presentations. A future transfer of asset value between profiles requires atomic retirement/mint authorization and explicit disclosure rules. A holder receiving only tokens and no native balance must obtain native fee funding before spending, making any identifiable funding interaction part of the observer model. A transparent deposit/withdrawal exposes its asset, amount and time; low volume can identify a later private withdrawal by elimination. A network with a public asset ID on every transfer instead partitions liquidity/anonymity by that ID. A general hidden asset-ID relation would have to prove per-asset conservation and policy without exposing the selected ID and would change witness, receipt and proof costs. Atomic swaps would also expose terms to the actual counterparties and require a jointly authorized indivisible effect. The fixed-pair prototype alone provides neither arbitrary assets nor that swap protocol.

An acceptance comparison on the two-lane candidate should use separate honest transactions transferring different lane values and compare the observable wire grammar, constant sizes, paid native fee, and any profile/issuance marker. On the receiver side check that both balances recover from the same retained bytes, both commitments are independently validated and a fresh spend succeeds under the new profile. Reject an attempted historical-suite import before inventory changes. A tiny successful fixture demonstrates encoding and delivery. The assurance games above still require separate proofs.
