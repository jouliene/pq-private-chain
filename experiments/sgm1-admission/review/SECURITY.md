# SGM1 authorization and privacy under admission

**Keep SGM1 at research assurance.** The algebra ties a fresh ordinary proof to the two funded openings, their exact monetary statement and both receipt bytes. The observed implementation accepts genuine proofs. A native verifier can reduce admission cost and independently check the encoding. A quantitative quantum knowledge theorem for the accepted bytes and an adaptive simulator for the joint payment history remain open.

This review concerns the four-group ordinary registration from public baseline `beae20b4b9b81598708262cc407f21553ca7ae94`. The reviewer examined the monetary compiler, projection algebra, commitment and transcript, receipt implementation and published experiments independently of their authors. New admission measurements and acceptance results belong in the milestone's public reproduction record. [Published circuit and scope](../../structured-keccak/DECISION.md)

## What an accepted packet must establish

An adversary may create funded notes, choose the next payment after observing earlier proofs, request authorized payments, retain rejected branches, submit malformed records and observe retries. An unauthorized debit, two debits of one occurrence on one branch, and inflation are separate adverse outcomes. Extracting an arbitrary satisfiable circuit assignment would be insufficient. Extraction must identify an opening of each actual input under the selected anchor and the same ASK used for its owner and real nullifier. The output commitments, asset rules, unsigned integer conservation, fee and intent must match the packet. An ownership proof for a stale eligible anchor still requires current unspentness checked atomically with state changes. Conditional security also depends on the relevant owner, occurrence, tree and commitment binding assumptions. The statement includes the chain, root, count, fee, both nullifiers and both output commitments, intent and authorization. Intent includes the canonical envelope and both full receipt ciphertexts. The public role accepts registered rules selected by the verifier. ## The Artin–Schreier terminal

For a committed base symbol $x$, its bit $i$ is $\mathrm{Tr}(u_i x)$ for the trace-dual basis $u_i$. The verifier rewrites one wide affine terminal as 256 base-field linear forms $L_h$, including the original trace coordinates. The traces of these forms must equal the 256 public coordinates of the target. Independently choose $m_h,d_j$ in $F$ and commit them and their squares $q_h,e_j$. The prover publishes $v_h=L_h+m_h+q_h$. For honest squares, $m\mapsto m^2+m$ is two-to-one onto the trace-zero subspace. Each $v_h$ is uniform on its publicly specified coset. Independent masks give the joint product distribution. After the challenges $a_h$, the sole clear wide value $U=\sum_h a_hm_h+\sum_j\beta_jd_j$ is uniform even given all $v_h$, because the 16 independent pads span $K$ over $F$.

Assume the commitment has already bound a well-defined assignment. If some $q_h\ne m_h^2$, the identity between the two authenticated formulas for $U^2$ is linear in the independent uniform $a_h^2$ with at least one nonzero coefficient. Its success probability is at most $2^{-256}$. The precommitted differences $e_j+d_j^2$ only change its constant. The 258 final random weights, sampled after $U$, compress all linear identities with a separate error at most $2^{-256}$. If the squares and linear identities hold, checking every trace of $v_h$ enforces the intended terminal. A union bound for these two local tests is $2^{-255}$. This argument conditions on an extractable commitment and independent challenges. The paper's Figure 12 instead reveals masked multilinear partial evaluations at a rank-tested hiding point. SGM1 uses the trace-coset argument above and has no Figure 12 hiding-point transcript. ## Where quantum Fiat–Shamir correspondence is still missing

The source examined was the November 12, 2026 preprint, PDF SHA-256 `6a650832d5f90e5a9f09c56857851cbbb22eb2116ae25ff30d8d716ab899bfec`. Definition 9 and Appendix D.1 give the extractor a chronological sequence of prover hash queries **and answers**. A verifier observing public packets has no such log, and a quantum adversary can query in superposition. Theorem 1's classical bad-oracle term is $3q(q+1)/2^{2\lambda+1}$, with hiding term $q/2^\lambda$. SmallWood+ supplies neither a quantum extractor for this commitment nor the trace-coset terminal used here. [SmallWood+](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2880)

Two possible reductions have distinct missing premises. Chiesa–Manohar–Spooner Theorem 8.6 requires an interactive oracle proof with round-by-round knowledge and the BCS transform. A correspondence must give its oracle messages, prefix state, extractor, query budget and hash tree domains for this ordered sequence of commitments, degree digest, GKR, trace-coset responses, Q and openings. Their extraction bound includes terms proportional to $t^2\kappa$ and $t^3/2^h$, with verifier queries charged. As a sensitivity example, substituting $t=2^{128}$ and $\kappa=2^{-255}$ makes the first term 2. This arithmetic leaves the success bound inconclusive. Don–Fehr–Majenz Corollary 15 assumes an already quantum-secure interactive proof of knowledge and the specified multi-round transform. Its generic success loss scales as $(2q+1)^{2n}$ for $n$ challenges. Neither premise follows from replaying a genuine proof or from using SHAKE256. [CMS, Theorem 8.6](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/834.pdf) · [Don–Fehr–Majenz, Corollary 15](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/282.pdf)

For 100M included operations per year, a 90/10 ordinary/two-leg mix over 20 years yields 2.2 billion proof-bearing legs. A separate uniform conditional error bound for each leg loses 31.035 bits under a union bound. The analogous 4.4 billion receipt slots cost 32.035 bits. A proof already quantifying one global adaptive query budget needs its own aggregation analysis. Accepted operations do not bound the number of unsuccessful submissions, oracle queries or wallet scans.

## Multi-proof privacy and recipients

The joint challenge view includes receipts, full proofs, fees, anchors, nullifiers, public issuance, public swaps, later spends and network-visible scheduling. Compare histories with matched allowed leakage and independently generated challenge owners. The prover needs fresh commitment salts, GKR blinds and ring pads on each new proof. Reusing a real input deliberately reuses its nullifier, including across differently encrypted retry packets. The original payer knows their recipient. Later disclosure of the relevant wallet seed allows historical receipt decryption. Public issuance or an adversary controlling most eligible rows can sharply reduce the effective anonymity set. [Joint monetary games](../../fresh-spend/binding/GAMES.md)

Two resident wallet processes independently generate and retain their seeds, spending keys and ephemeral signing keys. Both validate the signed funding prefix, replay the selected history, derive the same salted terms from the advertised keys, owner IDs, assets, quantities, cash, fee and expiry, then retain their own draft digest. Each signer verifies both proofs under local eligible anchors, checks both negotiated envelopes and decrypts the incoming slot-zero receipt to its own descriptor. It requires the exact incoming asset, quantity and native value before signing. Both approvals cover the chain and the complete ordered cores, including proofs. Each wallet subsequently verifies the included pair and identifies its scanned incoming note and change. The public FSW1 frame links the legs. Expiry, current unspentness of all four nullifiers, anchor eligibility and atomic admission are ledger checks. Rejected signed pairs and retries can remain observable. The coordinator sees the offers, both receiving descriptors, the salt and both draft cores, including unconfirmed proposals. Its channel carries no swap private keys or private ownership witnesses. The public receipt contains a 1,568-byte ML-KEM-1024 ciphertext, 208 encrypted note bytes and a 16-byte GCM tag. The 44 HKDF-SHA384 bytes derive the 32-byte AES key and 12-byte nonce using the descriptor-dependent salt and chain/occurrence/slot/commitment/ciphertext-dependent context. A malicious payer can authorize an unusable receipt.
