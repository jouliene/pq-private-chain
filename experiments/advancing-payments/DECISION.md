# Payment selection for the first local PoW development network

The engineering choice is how a lone wallet can submit a complete private payment against a moving chain without entrusting its spending witness to a block producer. A separate economic question is how much independent archival participation this payment format would require at 100 million offers per year. Acceptance of an annual traffic or storage budget remains with the project owner.

**Local integration candidate: direct APV2 singletons with wallet-created proofs.** Give each local producer a direct intake independent of the optional builder. Use the two-second local block opportunity. The initial four-intent baseline offered one fresh payment every twelve seconds. The full-inventory test then offered one every ten seconds. Keep four-way aggregation off the critical inclusion path at this cadence. Keep CP03 and WHIR executable for comparison. This is a payment choice for a first local PoW devnet; actual mining, network selection and issuance remain separate work.

### Independent acceptance

A non-implementer rebuilt the executable in a clean checkout, reproduced the exact binary and all 15 public verifier artifacts, generated new wallet keys and new proofs, then reconstructed both sets of public histories. The implementation and non-author ran on the same host in separate installations. In both twelve-intent runs, all 12 payments confirmed on 60 advancing blocks at **one fresh intent per ten seconds**, leaving zero queued. The author and reviewer last commits were at 120.081 s and 120.042 s after the first scheduled intent. Maximum creation dispatch lag was 4.58 ms and 4.06 ms. In the independent test the warm four-way proof became ready after 17.233 s, again too late for height nine. The scoped recommendation is direct service at this **0.10 offers/s finite demonstrated load**. Three prepared four-wallet cohorts worked at 0.150–0.154 offers/s when the producer controlled the block opportunity clock; that throughput cannot be used as a two-second PoW inclusion claim. ## What the payment authorizes

APV2 derives a separate deployment from the pinned Qnero spend circuit. Four isolated wallets generated distinct keys in separate private directories. One trusted laboratory supervisor selected public funding and recipients and scheduled all four. This demonstrates separate spending authority on one host; it gives no isolation against that operating system or a hostile supervisor. A wallet privately proves ownership of its two funded notes, derives their nullifiers, proves quaternary-tree membership under an anchored header and constructs two bounded-value note commitments with a fixed fee. The candidate admits fee three in native units, and the ledger sums it. The ledger consumes both nullifiers and publishes both outputs atomically. Ordinary cryptographic verification uses an operator-approved registry derived from source for the leaf, singleton wallet proof and each of three aggregate widths. The nonhiding leaf proof and spending witnesses stay inside their wallet. Builders verify and aggregate only completed blinded wallet submissions. A receiving wallet opens an encrypted note against its commitment and can authorize a subsequent genuine spend. The registered circuit permits zero-valued dummy inputs; each experimental payment used two funded, positive notes.

APV2 uses a note-derived nullifier and a sorted quaternary tree (maximum 2⁴⁰ leaves). The ledger enforces unique funded commitments and commits to the ordered transition separately from the membership root. CP03 and WHIR use occurrence-bound nullifiers and ordered depth-40 membership. They remain separate protocol versions. An activation would need an explicit migration of funds. A fixed research fee accumulator supplies no PoW payout mechanism. ## What an advancing node verifies

An eligible historical anchor is an exact selected-branch block, at most eight heights behind the prospective child. The input proof can remain eligible across unrelated blocks. Branch identity, current spentness, previous commitments, all effects in the candidate block and current accounting are checked in the same durable transaction. The node performs incremental base-four frontier updates and indexed admission. The payer in this version still obtains the common commitment list and reconstructs its own tree. Hiding the membership path from a producer and making that path inexpensive for a cold wallet are different obligations.

For the same history of branch selections both storage modes preserve the same state on restart. The rollback floor is a **local retention policy**. It depends on the highest tip that this node has previously selected. Two nodes can see the same H4 tip with floors 0 and 4 if only one previously reached H12. An attempt below the local floor requires a separate historical replay/bootstrap decision. The floor cannot decide consensus validity or finality. Reusing it as a network-wide fork-choice rule would split participants. The first devnet must keep this signal local and explicitly surface the need for replay. ## Offered work and inclusion

The lone-payer run uses a warm wallet, four fresh payment intents at seconds 0/12/24/36, and an independent 2 s local block clock. With the preferred endpoint stopped and no builder process, the direct node admitted all four. The author measured **10.044–10.083 s per intent** to durable inclusion; 8.649–8.762 s was wallet construction. The four transfers spent their anchors at ages 5/6/6/6 blocks. Nineteen empty blocks advanced while the payer worked. Combined application traffic was 920,840 B uploaded, 128 B of acknowledgements and 924,412 B on a single block relay stream. The maximum observed block completion lateness was 0.084 s. The intent clock starts after registration, genesis, wallet setup and endpoint setup. [Public opportunities, timings and role meters](evidence/direct/DIRECT.json) · [Separate public-only verification](evidence/DIRECT_PUBLIC_REPLAY.json)

The full-inventory test used all twelve separate funding pairs, a 120 s offer horizon and the same two-second opportunities. The sender offered **0.10 fresh payments/s**. All twelve were committed; the queue drained. The author measured 10.037–10.081 s intent-to-inclusion, maximum dispatch lag 0.0046 s, last commit at 120.081 s and 48 empty blocks. The preregistered completion tolerance was 122 s. This is an observed finite operating point with one warm sender. It does not specify a long-run network rate or a stochastic PoW delay bound. [Fresh paid blocks, resource and queue measurements](evidence/paced/DIRECT.json) · [Twelve public authorizations reverified](evidence/PACED_PUBLIC_REPLAY.json)

The bounded scheduling rule waits up to 200 ms for companions when the preferred intake handles a lone, eligible local payment. A direct producer endpoint bypasses that wait. On the test host the direct service can include an eligible, uncontested singleton at the next block opportunity. No finite wall-clock promise follows under hash-rate variation, competing mempools, censorship, packet loss, an unavailable direct producer or a partition. The observed failover uses separate Unix processes sharing local durable intake; independent hosts, routing and standing producer availability require a devnet test.

A separate outage regression queued two acknowledged preferred submissions before the endpoint disappeared, then accepted a third wallet through direct ingress. The earlier dispatcher attempted to look up an absent width-two builder and stopped before inclusion. The corrected producer falls back to the oldest already authorized singleton and leaves the remaining offers in the durable queue. It repeats normal ledger admission and current nullifier checks on each one. The three independently funded payments produced six spent nullifiers and exactly nine fee units. A missing worker, a worker that exits and a disconnected worker pipe are transport/service failures. A malformed returned aggregate or a failed payment verifier remains an explicit error. A live but indefinitely unresponsive optional worker requires an external service timeout; a direct-only producer does not call it. [Initial fixed-case evidence](assurance/PENDING_FAILOVER.json) · [Public regression](assurance/pending_failover.py)

A producer served three preproved bursts of four (12 distinct payments) at 0.14994 offered payments/s, one group every 26.677 s with zero deadline misses. The last group completed 70.584 s after the first scheduled arrival. That test advances the selected chain on its own service schedule. An independent two-second clock changes the result. With all four proofs already prepared at the freshest possible anchor and the builder warm, the new four-way outer proof became ready after **17.421 s**. It verified cryptographically and matched all four complete wallet statements. Its prospective height was **9**. The eight-height rule rejected it atomically and the chain continued. Starting earlier, freezing the chain, accepting expired anchors or quietly widening the eligibility window cannot be credited to this configuration. [Prepared-service results](evidence/main/RESULT.json) · [Advancing-clock trial and genuine aggregate](evidence/clock/CLOCK.json) · [Public clock replay](evidence/CLOCK_PUBLIC_REPLAY.json)

The author's warm four-way builder used 33.76 process CPU-s and peaked at 2,130,980 KiB in that advancing-clock trial. The separate direct trace ran one funded wallet at 0.08333 new payments/s. Neither experiment sustained the 3.171 offers/s implied by 100M/year. That target also needs at least 0.793 full four-wallet blocks/s before empties. End-user proof preparation is distributed, while builder capacity, anchor lifetime, remote availability and relay remain different constraints.

## Recovery, fork and disk evidence

A main wallet restarted from its 32-byte seed at height six. It opened six unspent notes and spent two at height seven. Its earlier outgoing payments could supply change. A dedicated test endowed only the sender at genesis and stopped the recipient before two incoming payments. A new recipient process recovered from the seed and independently scanned each payment and their union, obtaining **one, one and two** unspent notes. Those were its only funds. It created a fresh proof and spent both at height four. Current spentness then left one new change note. The public verifier reproduced all three authorizations from genesis without a private key. The cold wallet needs historical receipts, spent nullifiers and authenticated funded commitments for private local membership. Recovery of random synthetic genesis openings or unknown keys is outside the seed guarantee. [Incoming-only raw measurements](evidence/incoming/RECEIPT.json) · [Complete payment and branch traces](evidence/main/TRACE.json)

The public main replay tested 31 selected, rejected and rewound operations on both storage modes. It retained eligible common-branch funding, rejected orphaned, conflicting, corrupted and expired payments, and checked all slot effects atomically. A nonempty partial replacement was on disk when a separate test killed the writer with a live rollback journal. Restart recovered all tables, counts, fees, nullifiers and bodies of the original H7. Retrying installed the alternative H6. A real post-commit lost acknowledgement caused two full uploads but only one economic inclusion. Roots matched the independent full-tree implementation at 16 prefix sizes from one to 16,384 commitments. After 20 blocks the author archive occupied 2,457,600 B. The pruned SQLite file occupied 2,220,032 B before copying compaction and 61,440 B after it. The nine retained bodies were all empty at that point and totaled 1,368 B. This observation cannot price a year's spentness and funding indexes. At 100M inclusions, just the two new 32-byte commitments and two nullifiers per payment add 12.8 GB annually before headers, indexes, database overhead and retained bodies. The six-block recovery needed 3,084 B of genesis and 1,423,388 B of complete archived blocks for independent history verification. Its internal receipt scan took 43,776 B of rows and 768 B of spent nullifiers; the next spend consumed 3,840 B of common commitment payload. A pruned node still validates all live blocks and needs an archive to serve old receipts. The new standalone packet is 56.26% larger than the earlier native experiment, and the pair is 48.62% larger. These are complete serialized artifacts with their encrypted receipts; the subtraction includes changed framing and public inputs as well as proof parameters. Each wallet additionally uploads a 4-byte size field and receives a 32-byte acknowledgement. Every selected APV2 block adds 152 bytes to its packet, plus four bytes per relay edge. CP03's existing first-copy projection of about 175 TB/year refers to its own payment format. Under the preregistered low mix (85/10/4/1% of nonempty blocks by width, one empty per paid block), the weighted cost is **191,522.91 archive B per included payment**. The busy mix (5/10/15/70%, one empty per nine paid) gives **70,806.88 B**. At 100% inclusion these imply 19.1523 and 7.0807 decimal TB/year for one canonical archival copy. At 95% of offers included, they imply 18.1947 and 6.7267 TB/year, while one framed upload per original offer still costs 23.021 TB/year plus 0.0032 TB of acknowledgements. A pruned peer still receives about 19.1530 or 7.0808 TB/year at full inclusion. These are projections using exact measured bytes; the experiments did not operate a 100M/year network.

Measured hardware was a six-core Intel i7-8700K, 12 logical CPUs, 31.15 GiB reported RAM and local ext4 on NVMe. Isolated proof roles used two Rayon threads. For comparison, the project's hypothetical home node has four physical cores, two allocated to validation, 16 GiB RAM, 4 TiB usable disk and 80/16 Mbit/s usable down/up. One live selected-block relay edge at 100% inclusion projects to 4.86 Mbit/s in the low mix or 1.80 Mbit/s in the busy mix. Checking final proofs alone projects to 0.0314/0.0194 CPU-s per second from the finite peer sample. These figures exclude large-history database work, initial data, inbound wallet verification, miner traffic, peer fanout and reorgs. They cannot establish independent operation at the target. A four-wallet upload burst measures 920,840 B before inclusion and a full resulting block relays 251,154 B on each edge. At 100M inclusions a 5 TB/year copy allows 50,000 B per payment; 1 TB allows 10,000 B. Even with continuously full four-payer blocks and no empties, the candidate needs 62,787.5 archive B per payment. With the current receipts and framing, the four-payer proof itself would have to fall from 236,653 B to at most 185,503 B for 5 TB, or 25,503 B for 1 TB. These are design thresholds. Sparse occupancy calls for a substantially smaller independently admissible wallet proof as well. A useful next proof experiment must measure that complete artifact and its completion time against advancing eligible anchors.

## Security and privacy limits

The measured FRI schedule uses 43 queries, inverse rate 8, no grinding, Goldilocks EF2 and two challenges at every layer. Row blinding and FRI leaf hiding are enabled for every proof exported by a wallet or builder. The constructor value 128 is a conjectured component check. The protocol lacks a concrete noninteractive quantum knowledge and zero-knowledge bound for the composed recursive proof. Original Poseidon's concrete security is a further qualification; the 128 setting cannot remove it. The note/tree hash uses Poseidon2. Encrypted receipts use ML-KEM-1024 and ChaCha20-Poly1305 with the pinned KDF and context. An observer can see source/timing on the intake, anchor, occupancy, public effects and ciphertexts and can match a forwarded payment to its included slot.

A verifier checks the actual ciphertexts against the ordered digest in the proof. A dishonest sender can bind a ciphertext that does not deliver the intended note. Recipient-side opening and the demonstrated respend establish the honest path. An application that requires enforceable receipt by the intended counterparty needs an additional recipient protocol or verifiable encryption relation. Publicly labelled transmissions and known synthetic funding in these experiments cannot measure payer or recipient anonymity. ## Extensions and release boundary

The proposed token version binds asset identity into note, authorization, nullifier, change and receipt. It separately conserves the private asset and collects fees in the native asset. The proposed swap version requires both owners to authorize the same blinded agreement, payees and role effects. The only admissible item is the complete joint proof/atom. A block with one residual slot must defer both legs. The structural controls exercise splitting, duplication, reordered roles, changed agreements and branch handling. Only ordinary native transfers have executable cryptographic authorization in this release; there is no spendable token, exchange market or production issuance.
