# N3B1 fixed binary proof profile

Use the three-party Boolean ZKBoo decomposition of the entire N3R1 2x2 relation, elementwise hash commitments and **one** challenge vector for all 560 independently randomized parallel repetitions. This is a specific priced companion backend. The underlying classical sigma protocol is Proposition 4.2 (3-special soundness and special honest-verifier ZK). The commit-and-open extractor in DFMS Theorem 4.2 applies under the premises below. No consortium, cache, trusted setup or private verifier input is part of this profile. [Giacomelli, Madsen and Orlandi §§3–5](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity16/sec16_paper_giacomelli.pdf) · [Don, Fehr, Majenz and Schaffner §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13730)

## Relation and counter

Private input to each repetition is s[48], common asset[32], then for each of two ordered inputs salt[48], native:u64, token:u64, O[48], index:u40, 40 sibling digests[48] in leaf-up order. Then for each output A[48], salt[48], native:u64, token:u64. Total **4,378 bytes**. The circuit reads the public network, anchor, count, fee, NFs, and output commitments. FS binds the entire typed packet including both ciphertexts and all swap policy fields. Framing, branch membership of the public anchor, fee policy, ciphertext length, duplicate NFs and signatures are external public checks. The single circuit output bit must be 1. Every real input is consumed once by current branch rules.

Use FIPS 202 Keccak-f[1600], 24 rounds, 104-byte SHA3-384 absorption rate, suffix 0x06, final rate byte XOR 0x80. For each permutation chi uses 25 × 64 = 1,600 one-bit AND gates per round; theta, rho, pi, iota and the remainder of chi are affine. A complete permutation takes 38,400 AND gates in this fixed implementation. Hash calls and other operations follow the source order in [relation.py](relation.py). Within each round order chi gates by y, x, lane bit from least significant upward, linear index x+5y. The part left over after a byte vector's final bit has canonical zero padding on the wire.

The fixed profile includes gates on constant-padded words without optimizing them away. Use left-to-right, low-bit-first OR reductions with a OR b = a XOR b XOR (a AND b). To check equality, append one AND into the running valid bit for each XNOR of a pair of bits; initialize valid to 1. A path switch computes u = (left XOR right) AND indexBit once per lane bit and XORs u into both inputs. For an addition compute a XOR b XOR carry and next carry (a AND b) XOR ((a XOR b) AND carry); two gates for each of the 66 bits including unused final carry. At index comparison, pad the index to 41 bits and use the count's checked low 41 bits. Subtract bitwise with next borrow = ((NOT a) AND b) XOR ((NOT (a XOR b)) AND borrow). Final borrow is index < count. Use wire ordering from each function's source, and for each hash complete the whole sponge before returning. The clear evaluator and its negative cases returned the declared counts. The 3-view C probe checks recombined Keccak-f against OpenSSL for the empty SHA3-384 message and rejects a changed first-party gate share.

## Transcript and fixed encoding

1. Register N3R1/N3B1 together under a distinct packet version. Use ASCII labels below exactly as written followed by a single zero byte. The unsigned statement S is the complete fixed-format packet through proof_length (the proof and any joint signature are excluded). Its packet magic, public chain, kind, approved relation, receipt bytes and any swap fields are bound. There are no selectable codec parameters. For each repetition j = 0..559 choose a fresh 64-byte private witness-sharing seed and three independent 64-byte tape seeds. Use SHAKE256(label N3B1/shares || u16LE(j) || witness-sharing seed) to generate two W-byte uniform-looking share vectors; compute the third by XOR with the witness. Put all public bit values, NOT offsets, and the Boolean constant one into party 0. Use SHAKE256(label N3B1/tape || u16LE(j) || party:u8 || tape seed) to generate each party's per-gate random bits in little-endian bit order. These are domain-separated PRG instantiations; the underlying perfect-privacy statement assumes independently uniform masks.
2. At AND gate g with inputs a,b, party i outputs zᵢ = (aᵢ AND bᵢ) XOR (aᵢ₊₁ AND bᵢ) XOR (aᵢ AND bᵢ₊₁) XOR rᵢ XOR rᵢ₊₁, party indices modulo 3. AND outputs XOR to the true product. Each raw view V consists of tape seed[64], one witness-share vector[4378], g-bit packed AND output shares[479673], with unused high seven bits of its last byte zero. Hash (SHA3-512) label N3B1/view || u16LE(j) || party:u8 || V to obtain the 64-byte commitment. A first-message record per repetition is three commitments in party order then one byte holding the three final output shares in low bits, high bits zero. Require their parity to be 1. F is all 560 first-message records concatenated, 108,080 bytes.
3. Compute two SHA3-512 digests over label N3B1/challenge || b:u8 || S || F, b=0 then 1. Interpret their concatenation as one little-endian integer. Keep the low 887 bits and expand to 560 base-3 digits, least significant first, retaining leading zeroes. For digit i reveal complete views i, i+1 in that order. The proof wire is F followed by all revealed views in j order. No hidden-party seed, prover-supplied circuit, encoded challenge or length-dependent compression is accepted.
4. Public verification checks fixed widths, canonical padding, EOF and output-share parity. For each pair regenerate their tapes, recompute both view commitments, all shared affine gates, and **every** opened first-party AND using both tapes and both opened input shares. Compute the second-party output wires from its disclosed AND shares. Match both final bits to F. A change to the unopened consistency equation is covered by other challenge choices. All three accepting challenge responses for the *same* complete view array in one repetition reconstruct a valid witness. Abort atomically on any mismatch. Receipt authentication by the beneficiary is a separate mandatory wallet check.

The 560 × 2 × 484,115 response bytes plus F cost **542,316,880 bytes** fixed. Ordinary nonproof framing costs 3,769 bytes including two receipts. A signed leg adds 17,173 bytes for expiry, fee-payer byte, terms hash, fresh SLH key and signature. A pair adds 12 bytes of bundle framing, producing **1,084,675,656 bytes**. A direct frame cannot fit even **one** 38,400-gate permutation's pair of transcript vectors over 560 repetitions (5,376,000 bytes). Changing the view codec, repetitions or proving system changes this calculation. [Complete arithmetic and controls](PRICE.json)

## Public-history quantum and privacy assessment

**Knowledge for a single adaptive statement, ideal oracle.** For fixed committed witnesses/views lacking an extractable repetition, at most 2^560 challenge vectors pass (two local choices per repetition). The chosen challenge space has size 2^887 with distinct trit encodings. Thus p_triv ≤ 2^-327. If all three pairs pass in one repetition, the three checked gate equations reconstruct the full circuit on the XOR of the three input shares. This gives the required offline S-sound* extractor for an entire monetary witness in that repetition. DFMS Theorem 4.2 for elementwise commitments gives online extraction failure ≤ (22ℓ+60)q³2^-n +20q² p_triv with ℓ=1,680, n=512. Model a single 1,024-bit wide ideal oracle truncated for commitments, with disjoint tags for the two challenge halves and the view family; a half-oracle query costs at most two ideal calls by compute/uncompute. This is an oracle comparison, not a construction of an ideal oracle from SHA3. Counting q=2·2^128+1,124 yields <2^-64.67. A crude union across 2^40 separately bounded success events is <2^-24.67 only when each game's side information is covered by its ideal-oracle argument. The single-statement bound itself does not prove adaptive many-proof privacy or extraction with ongoing corruption. The extractor has O(q²) polynomially weighted simulation overhead. No claim about concrete quantum runtime or real Keccak indifferentiability follows. [DFMS Theorem 4.2 and Remark 3.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13730)

**Funded binding and authority.** Even a valid extracted assignment might rely on an alternate opening, substituted parent or different count/root preimage. A first divergence reduction gives a distinct pair with equal tagged SHA3-384 digest, or an authentic occurrence controlled by the extracted spend secret. For an ideal 384-bit function the generic any-pair quantum collision work scale is 2^128 (O(q³/2^384) success upper bound up to constants). A fixed-target preimage costs about 2^192 queries; against T accessible independent target descriptors/hashes a search estimate is 2^((384−log2 T)/2), about 2^172 at T=2^40, or 2^175.98 for 4.4 billion outputs in the shared 20-year scenario. These target counts conservatively allow public lookup; not every NF gives a short independent target oracle. Overlapping target-defining queries or adversarially chosen parents need the collision game and actual reduction. Public honest table collisions separately cost O(T²/2^384). Tags distinguish note, authority, NF, inner node, empty leaf, funded leaf and count root; secret mixing and random salts have separate domains. A 48-byte uniform backup avoids a 256-bit master-seed multi-wallet search ceiling. This does not strengthen the 256-bit KEM shared secret, AES key, GCM tag or independent approval scheme. No margin above 128 generic collision query bits is claimed. [Zhandry, *A Note on the Quantum Collision and Set Equality Problems*](https://www.rintonpress.com/xxqic15/qic-15-78/0557-0567.pdf) · [Boyer et al., *Tight bounds on quantum searching*](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605034)

**Joint hiding.** In the interactive binary decomposition, for a preselected opened pair the two input shares are independent of the clear witness. Simulate their two tapes, simulate each second-party AND share using the masked unknown third-party contribution, compute first-party equations normally, and obtain the missing final share by parity. Replace the unopened view commitment by its hiding simulation. For the actual NIZK require computational analogues for the PRG, large-entropy commitment inputs and QROM programming of the single FS challenge over many adaptive, receipt-correlated statements. The extractor simulation bound is not a ZK theorem. Further hybrid steps would replace ML-KEM recipient encapsulations and DEM plaintext for equal-shaped honest-recipient challenge transactions while keeping the correlated C, NF, receipt and proof distribution jointly simulatable, including chosen valid descriptors, adversarial history and delayed/repeated spends. FIPS 203 gives the KEM spec, not key anonymity or multi-key robustness; HKDF key separation and a GCM authentication tag do not establish committing encryption for maliciously chosen keys. Salted C and fresh signed terms are independently needed to avoid offline dictionary openings. More than separate marginal hiding experiments is required. Residuals include corrupted payers knowing their own transactions, timing of wallet actions, online scan/view disclosures, active malformed receipt probing, and retrospective compromise of the wallet seed. These obligations block assigning the entire funded system a 128-bit quantum assurance level. [FIPS 203](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final) · [DFMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13730)
