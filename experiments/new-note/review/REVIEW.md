# Independent design review

This review compares the funded baseline at `ceab1e4fd786e8f9ac0a99cc9a805423fe4a3b73`, the SHA3-384 reference N3R1/N3B1, and the new N7P1 arithmetic pool. The deciding security question is whether an accepted public transaction spends an authentic occurrence with authority over its complete opening and conserves its value on the selected branch. The wallet must also be able to discover and spend an honest payment from its backup.

## Accounting and SHA3 reference

I fetched both historical funding archives anonymously and checked their SHA-256 values against the release manifests. A separate byte reader split both bundle length prefixes, the 107-byte outer headers, 17,088-byte approvals, inner tags, proof length, statement and two receipts. Both released SLG1 frames are 160,446 bytes. Their 139,658-byte proof payloads include a u64 count of 34 and 272 bytes of public words, leaving 139,378 bytes of cryptographic body. Both released ordinary packets measured 143,233 bytes. The two FTL1 frames measured 259,212 and 258,892 bytes, including proof bodies of 238,120 and 237,800. A mixed pair costs 419,671 or 419,351 bytes. The corrected denominator leaves the deployed frame sizes intact. [Historical release](https://github.com/jouliene/pq-private-chain/releases/tag/funded-tcith-2026-11-21)

The N3B1 counted encoding charges 99 Keccak permutations × 24 rounds × 1,600 bit products, plus 35,777 other products, per repetition. An opened party sends 64 + 4,378 + ceil(3,837,377/8) = 484,115 bytes. The complete proof costs 560 × (193 + 2 × 484,115) = 542,316,880 bytes. Even the two disclosed bit vectors for a single permutation across these repetitions cost 5,376,000 bytes. This lower bound alone excludes the fixed N3B1 view format. It applies to the specified decomposition and disclosures. [Profile and gate count](../sha3/PROOF.md)

I checked the DFMS PDF, Theorem 4.2 and Remarks 3.6–3.8. The ideal analysis can treat the three output-share bits as extra first-message data bound into the challenge, partition the view and challenge hash domains, and limit challenge randomness to 887 uniform bits. For a fixed array without a consistent repetition each coordinate admits at most two choices, bounded by 2^560 admissible vectors. With q = 2 × 2^128 + 1,124 and 1,680 elementwise commitments, the reported bound is about 2^-64.678. Extending the calculation to 2^40 conditional trials gives about 2^-24.678. This argument assumes the protocol's offline extractor and ideal challenge/commitment oracles. Real Keccak, pseudorandom masks and a joint receipt-proof simulator have additional obligations. [DFMS, PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.13730)

## N7P1 proof and ledger

The circuit follows a single spending secret through both owner comparisons, input commitments, both 40-level ordered paths and both nullifiers. It rejects equal input positions. The checked room and distance-from-two bounds enforce 2 ≤ count ≤ 2^40. Each index has 40 bits and count − 1 − index fits in 40 bits. The input digest must reconstruct the actual public root using a distinct parent tag at each level. A private arbitrary sibling is allowed by the mathematical relation. Its interpretation as a real funded occurrence still depends on computational binding.

Every asset limb has 32 bits and every amount is below 2^62. Each lane sum is below 2^63 before fees. Even the largest output sum plus 3 is 2^63 + 1, below the Goldilocks modulus 2^64 − 2^32 + 1. The product of two positive token amounts cannot vanish in a field, so the pairwise asset checks enforce a common asset identifier wherever token value is positive. The wire/ledger supplies the correct public fee for each kind. Two fees sum to exactly three for an admitted swap.

The verifier reconstructs the circuit from its parsed public statement and pinned code. The entire fixed statement enters the circuit's domain separator, while the witness-derived effects must yield the seven-word statement digest fixed in its constraints. The SHA3-384 domain-separator digest, constants/permutation cap and degree enter the outer circuit digest. The fork's BytesHash converts all 48 bytes injectively into seven-byte little-endian chunks for transcript absorption. FRI configuration, circuit digest, empty public-input hash and wire cap precede the first PLONK challenges. The SHAKE256 challenger rejects noncanonical field samples. The four-word inner Poseidon digest here represents the fixed empty public input. Building the approved verifier locally is essential: upstream's circuit digest omits an encoding of the gate algorithms. The compressed serializer emits the proof body followed by its raw public fields; with zero fields it contributes zero vector-framing bytes. [Pinned builder](https://docs.rs/crate/qp-plonky2/1.5.5/source/src/plonk/circuit_builder.rs) · [Pinned verifier](https://docs.rs/crate/qp-plonky2-verifier/1.5.5/source/src/plonk/get_challenges.rs) · [Pinned serialization](https://docs.rs/crate/qp-plonky2-verifier/1.5.5/source/src/util/serialization/mod.rs)

A signed N7P1 leg carries 3,841 ordinary nonproof bytes, 85 swap metadata bytes and a 17,088-byte signature, for 21,014 bytes above the compressed proof body. A compatible 262,144-byte frame therefore leaves 241,130 bytes. For the two-owner swap, the two proof contexts each include the ephemeral public key, terms digest, payer, expiry, kind, network, outputs, nullifiers and exact ordered receipt bytes. Each owner checks both genuine proofs, its prepared leg, incoming opening and change, then signs one ordered digest of both complete proof-bearing cores. The pair validator independently checks distinct keys, both signatures, current spentness, output uniqueness, capacity and fees. Distinct ephemeral keys alone do not demonstrate independent economic ownership; the fresh scenario's one-seed wallet isolation supplies evidence for that scenario. Hashing receipt bytes binds authorization to the ciphertext chosen by the payer. Honest receipt semantics require trial decryption, commitment and recipient checks.

N7P1 derives its nullifier from (secret, index, commitment). The ledger rejects previously seen commitments, including already-spent ones, on the selected history. Hence a particular accepted commitment occupies one position on that history and has one spend identity under the opening/authority assumptions. If a payment is re-proved and moved to a different index on another history, the nullifier changes. A cross-branch retry algorithm cannot rely on a position-invariant identity. N3R1 binds an explicit funding occurrence into the leaf and nullifier, supports repeated commitments with distinct credits, and preserves identity under relocation of the same occurrence. Eligibility in N7P1 compares the selected current note root/count and spent set. Equivalent commitment prefixes can occur on distinct block histories. A new different root invalidates pending offers. The revised node checks a separately supplied founding identifier, recomputes the network identifier from public grant policy and nonce, and includes the exact genesis digest in each proof context. The operator must obtain an authentic founding identifier. Its internally reconciled issued totals are meaningful relative to that selected genesis.

Both real N7P1 inputs must be positive and controlled by one secret. A wallet with only one note cannot spend by this relation. In the recovery scenario a separately funded genesis note complements the restored transfer note. No production coin-selection or arbitrary-denomination liveness follows. The initial review identified that authenticated unusable plaintext and extra or empty owned notes could interrupt scans or pair selection. The revised wallet treats invalid openings as misses, retains valid zero change for agreement checks, skips zero-value inventory and selects two distinct eligible notes whose bounded change fits both lanes. Its N7A1 descriptor checks the key scope before sealing. The seven disclosed genesis credits have four owners with counts 2/1/2/2. The first spend has at most three eligible owners even before considering side information. The tree's 2^40 capacity measures a different property.

## Frame shape and lifetime assumptions

With 32 distinct upper five-bit prefixes, every capped tree has its maximum possible occupied-prefix count at every depth. In the telescoped sibling formula all internal coefficients are positive. At a terminal level its leaf payload exceeds the cost removed by eliminating the last sibling, so that coefficient is also positive after aggregation. The same placement maximizes the full encoding across all seven tree heights. My independent calculation gives a 246,120-byte maximum compressed body and 267,134-byte signed leg, 4,990 bytes over the historical limit. The separately proposed 272-KiB isolated frame would accommodate the maximum with 11,394 bytes to spare. Uniform independent indices give a mean body of 232,113.59 bytes. In 100,000 deterministic simulated index vectors, 93 exceeded the signed frame threshold (95% Wilson interval 0.076%–0.114% under the sampling model). These are index vectors, with no funded proofs generated for this statistic. A wallet can check the encoded length before requesting signatures. A retry policy adds latency, changes proof bytes and requires new approvals when a party has already signed. Run `python3 -IB experiments/new-note/review/frame_risk.py`. The table uses the random-function model, with quantum collision and multi-target search query scales. These exponents concern oracle queries and particular target distributions. The actual field sponge exposes a public permutation and inverse, uses additive field absorption and restricts each message shape. The N7P1 squeeze even crosses a permutation after its five output words. Any application to funded honest commitments, adversary-selected siblings, proof-bearing chosen statements or seed-derived credentials needs a reduction to the corresponding game. The 384-bit backup caps ideal single-seed search at about 192 quantum query bits, while the approval and DEM use independent standardized primitives with different claims. The 4.4-billion population is an illustration. The 100M/year owner workload does not fix the number of eligible independent cryptographic targets. [Quantum sponge models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16887) · [Poseidon2 construction](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/323) · [Quantum multi-solution search](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605034)

The backend counts ordinary and permutation-product blinding rows from its FRI geometry, samples private rows and salts hidden commitment leaves. A joint zero-knowledge argument must account for the entire opening vector, correlated permutation/quotient values, all FRI layer openings and final polynomial, the public commitments/nullifiers, later spends and ciphertexts. An explicit simulator and a quantum same-witness extractor for the exact specialized PLONK–FRI transcript remain open. The generic multi-round Fiat–Shamir result has hypotheses on the underlying interactive protocol and a query-dependent loss. The N3B1 commit-and-open analysis covers a different protocol. [Pinned blinding](https://docs.rs/crate/qp-plonky2/1.5.5/source/src/plonk/circuit_builder.rs) · [Multi-round Fiat–Shamir](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05207)

FIPS 203 defines the KEM. It supplies no theorem for anonymity between maliciously selected receiving keys in these complete protocols. N7P1 derives its AES-256-GCM key and IV from a tagged SHAKE256 of the KEM secret, ciphertext, recipient key and fixed associated data; N3R1 instead prices a descriptor-bound HKDF construction. Each needs its own adaptive, multi-user recipient-hiding and cross-key robustness argument. Shared wallet seeds enable retrospective decryption and spending after compromise. Fixed swap output positions, fees, timing, sender knowledge and later nullifier conflicts remain observable. Distinguish verification on a published history, functioning trial decryption and genuinely fresh backup-only spending from proof of these privacy/knowledge properties.
