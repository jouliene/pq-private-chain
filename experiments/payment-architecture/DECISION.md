# Payment proof decision after the first PoW wallet

**Adopt DLF1 as the next direct-wallet research construction. Keep APV2 and its ordinary wallet as the running reference. Defer activation on a general permissionless network.** The registered single-stage hiding spend proof reduces the complete direct transfer from 230,206 to **143,187 B (37.80%)** and reduces prover work on the measured machine. It supports a lone wallet without a builder, independently constructed payments, branch changes and seed-only recovery. The tested compact lattice candidate loses required receiving and membership properties. Deferred aggregation is a real archive-saving mechanism with a different cost distribution. None of the demonstrated designs delivers a complete 1 or 5 TB canonical year at 100M inclusions. The owner has set no archive or traffic limit. The first two primary routes address different limits of prior work. MatRiCT+ dispenses with the generic hash circuit and proves a native lattice relation. The deferred route decouples inclusion from the measured 17.4-second pre-inclusion wrap that expired at the ninth opportunity and changes what the PoW core authenticates. The direct pivot removes one complete recursive verification layer and applies hiding at the witness-bearing spend circuit. Its higher FRI expansion and four-point folds trade per-round work for fewer query openings. The 455 unblinded gate positions and 3,052 blinding rows fit 4,096 rows (degree bits 12); the APV2 wallet wrapper has 32,768 rows (degree bits 15). Previous CP03 and masked-WHIR complete-payment experiments remain executable. Their measured costs and unresolved compiled-security questions gave no reason to recompute unchanged profiles. ## Direct-wallet evidence

The core run started at zero spendable premine. Three independent wallets each earned two distinct 100-unit rewards. The lone sender produced its complete proof at H6. A validator accepted it after another branch advanced and a miner included it at H8. Two further senders proved and submitted concurrently from the common H8 state. Both appeared in one H9 block. A conflicting, independently proved authorization of the same two real inputs first appeared on the losing H9 branch. The winner rejected it in the mempool and after inclusion. The nodes selected the winning branch by work. A newly restored offline recipient opened three 150-unit receipts, proved a 250-unit payment from two of them, paid 3 and retained **197** (one 150-unit receipt and change of 47). Source and owner-local observations establish that two positive inputs were used; external verifiers learn only the public effects. The fresh verifier accepted both PoW branches, found eight selected nullifiers, 1,100 units issued and 12 in fees. Canonical-length proof, public-height and ciphertext mutations were rejected without advancing monetary or spent state. [Campaign](direct/campaign.py) · [Replayer](direct/replay.py)

The separate full-block experiment funds four wallets and constructs four direct authorizations on the unchanged complete circuit. Each privately spends two mined rewards. A normal validating node accepts the four public uploads and a miner can include them all without any proof aggregator. The public-only checker independently validates the block from genesis. The full-block run mined an unrelated H9 block while all four H8 proofs were being constructed. All four reached H10, whose 572,986 bytes give 143,246.5 B per included payer. The four wallet proof intervals overlapped by at least 3.61 s. Their session took 7.62 s. A measured block fetch used 94 request B and 573,017 response B. [Busy trace](direct/evidence/busy/BUSY.json) · [Public process](direct/evidence/busy/REPLAY.json) These finite paid opportunities and the controlled H6–H11 trace do not measure sustained production arrival rate. [Full-block experiment](direct/busy.py) · [Published fresh captures and readback](https://github.com/jouliene/pq-private-chain/releases/tag/payment-proof-architecture-2026-11-19)

In the author core run, the four canonical wallets averaged **2.46 CPU-s / 1.32 s wall** to set up and **7.13 CPU-s / 3.63 s wall** to prove. The observed proof process peaked at **452,384 KiB**. A previously measured normal APV2 spend with two positive inputs used 7.19 + 17.20 CPU-s, 4.50 + 8.85 s wall and 1,090,928 KiB. Each CLI invocation pays setup; the two native phases are separate in the raw records. DLF1's source-derived verifier setup on this host used 2.41 CPU-s, 1.30 s wall and 200,576 KiB peak. Five warm public standalone verifications averaged 12.31 ms CPU in the author replay. Repeated block checking, database reads and VM scheduling also cost resources. These timings do not establish a generally faster verifier. ## Who pays at 100M included transfers/year

The sparse scenario mixes 85/10/4/1% of **paid** blocks with widths 1/2/3/4 and one empty per paid block. The busy scenario mixes 5/10/15/70% and one empty per nine paid blocks. Both assume 100% inclusion for the modeled uploads; retransmissions, competing groups and alternate relay edges are extra. One full-requesting relay peer, independent local verification, and an available archive/compact-history service are the accounting roles. The earlier home-node scenario used four physical cores (two reserved for validation), 16 GiB RAM, 4 TiB disk and 80/16 Mb/s useful down/up; this is a research scenario. A real cold wallet scanned ten blocks and three payments, 14,291 B of historical payload. It sent 1,171 B of requests and received 17,634 framed bytes. Restored witnesses and spending were local. A separate public client fetched all eleven selected complete blocks, 575,206 payload bytes with 1,081 request B and 576,314 response B. Keeping the competing branch brought the raw public capture to 718,619 B. A storage-only copy of the twelve-block SQLite archive measured 843,776 B; removing bodies older than its last two heights and compacting gave 270,336 B while retaining the history projections. A running baseline node retains full bodies. A real pruned service needs a retention policy, external historical validation/DA for deep forks and bootstrap, and extra disk for database indexes, transaction lookup, frontiers, retained undo, forks and cache. Serving a full cold year from a materialized history index adds the projected 384–425 GB plus its indexes to the header/effect floor. A two-full-block live window can itself contain 1,145,972 B. Scaling a twelve-block SQLite file linearly is unjustified. [Exact role model](direct/costs.py)

100M inclusions/year requires 3.171/s. A two-second average opportunity with four slots supports at most **63.072M/year** even at full occupancy. Matching 100M with the registered mixes requires mean opportunities every 0.191 s (sparse) or 0.993 s (busy), leaving mean eight-block windows of 1.53 or 7.95 s. The sparse window is shorter than the measured one-user proof. A successful four-wallet burst cannot establish 100M/year service or finite wall-clock inclusion on PoW. One warm native verification pass per included offer projects about 0.039 cores and independent sender setup/proving about 30.4 core-years per year in aggregate. Mempool-plus-block rechecking, parallel forks, background load and proof retries increase those values. [Capacity check](review/BUDGET.json)

## What the proofs establish, and what remains open

- **Authority, membership and uniqueness.** The direct circuit enforces the pinned Qnero spending-key relation, two private sorted quaternary paths to the funded root (capacity 2⁴⁰), stable note-derived nullifiers, two output commitments and derived output randomness. The validating chain enforces branch identity, anchor age, uniqueness of funded commitments and current nullifiers atomically. A raw proof cannot establish current unspentness or chain freshness. Public nullifiers intentionally link conflicting/reanchored spends; immutable receipts and transaction IDs identify rebroadcasts. The development network discloses miner reward keys; end-to-end network metadata privacy is open. [Pinned spend relation](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/crates/qnero-circuit/src/circuit.rs)
- **Conservation and receipts.** Values and fee have 62-bit bounds keeping both sums below the Goldilocks modulus. The node additionally requires fee three; only PoW subsidy creates money. The proved public statement binds the digest of both ordered ciphertexts and the external verifier recomputes it. Honest receiver opening checks the resulting note commitment. A malicious payer can make an unreadable or incorrectly addressed receipt; the proof does not establish correct KEM encryption to a human's intended payee. The offline tests cover honest encryption and actual seed-only spending. [Independent statement and receipt analysis](review/REVIEW.md)
- **Hiding and security.** Direct DLF1 has one witness-bearing layer with row blinding and salted wire, product and quotient FRI leaves. Public constants/sigmas remain unsalted. The native width, degree, 26 public words and all opening dimensions were decoded independently. Neither transcript inspection nor different proofs of the same statement prove adaptive zero knowledge. The implemented conjectured FRI screen gives 26×5 = 130 query bits capped near 128 by the degree-two extension, compared with APV2's 43×3 = 129 before that cap. Reduced query count, more expansion and a new folding schedule require a new analysis. A ~256-bit ideal hash output has generic classical collision cost near 2¹²⁸ and quantum collision-query cost near 2⁸⁵; this is an idealized bound, not a lower bound on these Poseidon instances. Fiat–Shamir/QROM knowledge and soundness, classical algebraic reductions, many-payment losses, masking composition and concrete post-quantum recipient unlinkability remain open. [Parameter and transcript review](review/REVIEW.md) · [Concrete noninteractive FRI analysis](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1161)
- **Receiving hedge.** Current payments use ML-KEM-1024, with separate note/spend cryptography. A versioned MLKEM1024-P384 combiner would add 97 B per output and public receiving address, or **194 B/payment and 19.4 GB per archive-year**. This is a budgeted proposal; no new hybrid suite was activated. PQ confidentiality after EC failure relies on the PQ component and the actual combiner. A component revealing recipient identity can break anonymity even when the combined plaintext remains secret. Both long-lived decapsulation keys must restore consistently from independently labelled derivations. [Hybrid design and primary sources](review/RECEIVING.md)
- **Future assets and swaps.** A new version must commit to a hidden asset identifier in each note and constrain conservation **per asset**, including an explicit native-fee leg. Issuance remains separately authorized. A two-owner swap needs a single versioned joint statement binding both authorizations to the same network, legs, quantities, fee and session, with a single all-or-nothing ledger effect. Taking unrelated surviving slots from an aggregate would break indivisibility. These extensions remain designs. [Versioned examples](../wallet-created/EXTENSIONS.md)

An independent decoder found 26 distinct initial query indices in the first direct proof. Each initial query opened 243 base-field words across fixed columns, wires, product and quotient oracles and their salts. Those evaluations alone cost **50,544 B**. An encoding that only deduplicates Merkle paths and still transmits those evaluations cannot meet 5 TB/year in that packet, even before receipts. Recomputing or replacing the fixed oracle, changing the circuit width, or using a different PCS would change this bound and introduce new verifier costs or proof obligations. [Independent proof decoder](review/direct_wire.py)

## Next decision-changing experiment

Build one **eight-payment delayed certificate over DLF1 authorizations** on a versioned, ordered statement-committing PoW suffix. Each sender keeps its own witness, can go offline after acceptance, and can enter directly when no archivist is online. Keep both complete receipts per slot plus the statement IDs and recovery service. Use eight real payments from different blocks and include competing-history/withheld-data tests. A fresh verifier should rebuild the complete selected monetary state after deleting only the canonical original authorization sidecars, with the retained alternative sources explicitly charged. With eight used slots per certificate, 32-byte statement IDs, 226 charged bytes per core block, 3,584 B of receipts per payment and 13 B certificate framing, the outer-proof ceiling is **368,070–370,485 B for 5 TB** and **48,070–50,485 B for 1 TB** under the two mixes. These ceilings assume complete utilization, one certificate distribution/copy, and one 16-byte archive header amortized over the year; incomplete batches and forks spend more. Stop before integration if measured outer bytes, verification cost, background capacity or availability fail. This experiment could reduce the settled archive; the original DLF1 upload obligation would remain. [Scenario formulas](direct/costs.py)
