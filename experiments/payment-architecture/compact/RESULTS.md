# Compact lattice RingCT decision

**Defer MP10 as an ordinary wallet authorization.** Three real 2-input/2-output MatRiCT+ proofs passed independent public-process verification after lossless encoding at **53,728 bytes each**. The useful saving over APV2 comes from proving a private choice among ten public pairs directly in the lattice algebra. It costs input privacy and reusable address semantics. An honest refresh with new decoys exposed the unique original input pair. Separately, two distinct sets of minted coins controlled by the same receiving keys yielded exactly the same serials. Admitting the first spend would make the second funding unspendable under a ledger that enforces serial uniqueness. [Registered candidate](REGISTER.md) · [Captured results](RESULT.json)

## Executed gate

From the repository root, run the following on x86-64 with AES-NI, AVX2, Python 3, GCC and OpenSSL development files. The runner downloads the pinned source and checks its SHA-256 before compiling into a temporary directory. An existing byte-identical archive can be supplied with `--source-archive FILE`. All new secret material remains in the disposable native producer. The other native processes receive public inputs and proofs only.

```sh
python3 -I -B experiments/payment-architecture/compact/probe.py
```

The producer set up the same public matrices as the verifier from a fixed public seed. It ran the authors' KeyGen, Mint and Spend on two positive inputs and two outputs. The three public rings and all output coin openings were freshly sampled. The first two proofs used the same input coins. The third proof used fresh Mint commitments with different input values under the same two receiving keys. Each transfer's private values totaled 100 on each side. Separate fresh verifier invocations accepted all three after decoding. Changing a public serial or the proof hash challenge in the public verification fixture caused rejection. The harness enforces field widths in both directions and checks bit-for-bit round trips. The source calls SHAKE256 through OpenSSL in place of XKCP, with a known-answer check. The prover and each verifier regenerate the registered setup. ## Bytes and ledger work

| Part of an independently submitted 2×2 body | Bytes | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Proof | 53,728 | Observed, redecoded and verified. Exact field breakdown in JSON. |
| Two output accounts | 16,128 | Each account has four public-key and five value-commitment polynomials, each with 256 coefficients encoded in 28 bits. |
| Two serials | 64 | Observed native bytes. |
| Public ring references | 160 | Twenty u64 ledger positions in ten ordered pairs, resolved against the same selected anchor. |
| Profile, packet tag, branch anchor, fee | 76 | Proposed 4 + 32 + 32 + 8. |
| Two receipt envelopes | 4,128 | Proposed 2 × (1,568 + 480 + 16). See below. |
| **Conditional wallet body** | **74,284** | The proof and account encoding were executed. The positive-fee and reusable-address construction remain open. |

An authority verifying this body must reconstruct 161,280 bytes of packed public ring account data, or obtain equivalent cached authenticated state. Sending the full ring in addition to the body would cost another 161,280 bytes. Hashing against an anchor requires agreeing on positions, canonical accounts and the branch, and rejecting duplicates and ineligible or unavailable accounts. Each wallet creating arbitrary rings also needs local access to eligible account data. Fetching only the chosen rows leaks more to the serving node. Public common-data sync or a locally validated full account store costs at least the new 16,128 bytes of accounts per 2×2 rather than the two 32-byte APV2 commitments. For seed scan, retaining accounts, serials and the two candidate receipt envelopes costs 20,320 bytes per payment before branch and reward data. Pruned nodes still need eligible decoys, current serials and the retained reorg state, or a verifiable way to obtain them.

At 100M included payments, that hypothetical format costs 7.4284 TB of wallet uploads, plus roughly 7.435 TB of full blocks at width four or 7.465 TB under the sparse occupancy comparison. At 95M inclusions the corresponding archive projections are 7.063 and 7.092 TB. This assumes the prior 222-byte block base plus four bytes per inclusion, and excludes side branches, persistence overhead and peer replication. A node forwarding a complete packet once before distributing a block carrying the same body sends both copies. The unmodified WHIR transfer was 264,108 bytes on its first measured payment; APV2 uploads 230,206 bytes. Their private full-tree membership and working offline receive paths are material qualifications to this size comparison. The fixed 20,556 bytes outside the MP10 proof already exceed the 10,000-byte/payment comparison at 100M inclusions. Even at full occupancy the 5 TB comparison leaves about 29,384.5 bytes for the entire proof. The authors estimate approximately 29 KB of **proof** for a compressed variant at 11 rows, including changes such as rounding. Their running C implementation uses the uncompressed scheme. Its published size estimation also exploits expected response entropy, while the measured serializer above uses fixed widths, so the two results cannot be compared as interchangeable wire encodings. The public ABI's fused output contains one lazy-reduced polynomial with observed negative coefficients; its transcript hashes four-byte coefficients. This encoder preserves that field exactly in signed 32 bits, rather than reducing it after proving. A smaller profile or smaller account encodings could approach 5 TB but would still need to satisfy the receiving and privacy conditions below. ## Spend, receipts and privacy

The native input list is a 10 × 2 matrix. One selector chooses both accounts in the same row. The source knows the two short openings needed for their public keys, and the openings of their committed binary amounts. The CRT slots and carry proof enforce native zero-fee conservation. The public inputs of the native hash include the ring, serials, both new keys and both commitments. They omit the proposed external profile, branch header, public fee and encrypted receipt bytes. A separately serialized fee of three does not prove the paid fee. A positive-fee variant would have to prove that the bounded integer input sum covers both outputs plus three. One concrete design is a third value-3 output whose opening is publicly checked, with consensus crediting that output only to the block reward. The 2×2 code hardcodes both output polynomials in the prover, verifier and transcript. The extra output relation, compression cost and fee-recycling state transition require a separate candidate. The accepted 62-bit individual amount bound also needs enforcement when translating from native 64-bit amounts. Ordinary one-funded-coin payments require a compatible one-input relation or a proven safe dummy. [Native Spend](https://gitlab.com/raykzhao/matrict_plus/-/blob/b24f3176d2db15ca55d91c8c9cbe1cef201c5d2d/n10/spend.c) · [Public verifier](https://gitlab.com/raykzhao/matrict_plus/-/blob/b24f3176d2db15ca55d91c8c9cbe1cef201c5d2d/n10/verify.c)

* **Inputs.** Ten possible pairs give at most log2(10) ≈ 3.32 bits of conditional row uncertainty under a uniform prior. They do not give 100 candidate pairs. Even the largest shipped n50 profile gives only 50 rows. A fixed public pairing makes its real pairs reusable but cannot express arbitrary independently owned pairs. Covering all ordered choices for E eligible notes in this exact no-shuffling selector would require E(E−1) distinct rows or a different relation with independent private selectors. Honest reauthorization in a new public ring made the intersection exactly one pair for the two equal serials in this fixture. Pinning the whole ring on retry prevents this particular disclosure while those decoys remain eligible. A reorg may remove a pinned decoy, and unrelated transactions can disclose more through already identified participants or publicly known ownership. The full-tree APV2 relation publishes no note-specific decoy list. Native proof randomization cannot erase the public membership sets.
* **Ownership.** The receiver's native key is `pk = G r + H(serial)`. Fresh Mint samples a coin opening for a private amount under a public key, but Spend copies the key's serial into the authorization. In the measured third proof the two native public keys were the same as before and both commitments were different. Both serials stayed equal. On a static publicly advertised native receiving key the output's public key can also be compared directly to the address. A private shipment of a one-use key is a different wallet service.
* **Receipts.** A value coin's opening has 9 × 256 ternary coefficients. Five trits fit into one byte, so 461 bytes plus 8 bytes of value fit in 480 bytes of padded plaintext. The budget uses ML-KEM-1024, a fresh 1,568-byte encapsulation, a 16-byte AEAD tag and a nonce derived with a domain-separated KDF. The receiver would validate the commitment by recomputing it locally. No receipt was encrypted in this gate. Relaying an opening for a static key does not change its serial. The paper's Appendix C sketches masking a receiver's public key by adding a freshly generated key and transferring its secret. With two native KeyGen outputs the sum contains `H(serial1)+H(serial2)`. Knowing both openings does not directly give a new opening of `H(serial3)` to use in this verifier. A separately specified one-time account/serial relation, compatible witness norms, KEM anonymity and seed-only recovery are needed. Noninteractive reusable receipt derivation remains unproved for this integration.
* **Binding and replay.** MP10 has a single fixed setup. Upstream's setup encoder writes q at the qhat field and omits several constants; no application version, chain or recipient ciphertext enters the native statement. A network must register complete parameter and verifier identities and bind all public effects and exact receipt bytes to the payment transcript, check the selected anchor and ledger membership, require two distinct unused serials, and update the branch atomically. Publishing a hash beside a native proof does not bind an unabsorbed field. No transaction was admitted to a network or spent by a restored recipient in this failed-profile gate.

## Assurance and the next lattice gate

The paper proves balance and within-ring anonymity against classical polynomial-time adversaries in the programmable random-oracle model, assuming a family of short-vector and learning-with-errors lattice problems including asymmetric Module-SIS. Its lattice estimates give root Hermite factors 1.0043–1.00469. For the authors' final challenge, the implementation selects 11 signed positions in each of four groups of 64. Its support is at most 2^44 × binom(64,11)^4, or 201.74 bits. The authors' separate first challenge exceeds 192 bits and their independent random challenge-difference noninvertibility estimate is below 2^-93. None of these quantities bounds the complete adaptive quantum success probability. In particular Theorem 1 forks several proofs and programs classical oracle replies; Theorem 2's statistical rejection-sampling terms and MLWE reductions apply to the stated no-shuffling anonymity game. Theorem 2 assumes ordinary rejection sampling (Algorithm 1). The executed Spend calls optimized RejOp (Algorithm 2), whose acceptance depends on the sign of an inner product over masked responses and witness-dependent shifts. Appendix G explicitly leaves this variant’s joint-view anonymity analysis open even for a coin created and spent once. Reauthorizing the same input can increase that exposure. Signing the same ring again would prevent public set intersection from narrowing, but would still need a joint transcript argument for this sampler. The native sampler and concrete AES/SHAKE expansions need appropriate assumptions. Observing different proof bytes establishes fresh output, not computational anonymity. WHIR's interactive hiding analysis and APV2's registered FRI profile have independent open NI/QROM and hash-assurance obligations. The new native construction supplies no whole-payment PQ claim. [MatRiCT+, §§III–V and Appendix G](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/545) · [Active RejOp](https://gitlab.com/raykzhao/matrict_plus/-/blob/b24f3176d2db15ca55d91c8c9cbe1cef201c5d2d/n10/gaussian_avx.c) · [WHIR assurance](../../wallet-created/structured/ASSURANCE.md) · [APV2 assurance](../../advancing-payments/assurance/README.md)

Two alternatives help distinguish the selector issue from an inherent lattice limit.

* **Abelian PQRingCTX.** The maintained full-privacy encoder at the pinned revision charges 1 + N × 128,713 bytes for each ELR signature, where N is the ring length. Its 64-byte seed, 16 × 640-byte response with a one-byte vector length, and eight 37 × 400-byte responses each with its own length give 128,713 bytes per member. Two size-one signatures are already 257,428 bytes, before the full balance proof, the two commitments, output encryption or matrix effects. Two size-ten signatures cost 2,574,262 bytes. The implementation supports a separately tagged pseudonym mode, which changes the required disclosure. These are exact source-derived partial sizes; no Abelian transaction was generated. [Parameters](https://github.com/pqabelian/pqringctx/blob/d526987fdaa5326250bd83b69f4736e3eb0196bd/param.go) · [ELR cost](https://github.com/pqabelian/pqringctx/blob/d526987fdaa5326250bd83b69f4736e3eb0196bd/mlpsignature.go) · [Witness cost](https://github.com/pqabelian/pqringctx/blob/d526987fdaa5326250bd83b69f4736e3eb0196bd/mlptxwitness.go) · [Coefficient encodings](https://github.com/pqabelian/pqringctx/blob/d526987fdaa5326250bd83b69f4736e3eb0196bd/serialization.go)
* **SMILE.** Lyubashevsky, Nguyen and Seiler estimate a roughly 30 KB transaction at 2^15 candidates per input using a different tensor set-membership argument. Its public matrix contains the candidate list. A growing common full-chain set requires a strategy for committing to and evaluating that changing matrix, verified spentness and receiver privacy. A downstream SMILE implementation reports 70,272 bytes for 2×2, 7.29 s build, 481.09 ms verification and a maximum group of 32 on its implemented profile. Those are the downstream authors' observations, not measurements on our node. A discriminating next experiment would require two independently funded, spendable one-time accounts from **one public, seed-restorable address**, an implicit common set at two increasing ledger sizes, no public narrowing of candidate inputs on refresh, and a measured fully authenticated fee-paying packet. Without all four, the 30 KB figure does not justify replacing the current wallet.
