# Structured Keccak for wallet-created spending

**Advance the proof construction as a research candidate. Keep C on the funded PoW chain.** The new SGM1 proof fits the signed-leg envelope with 48,992 B to spare. We produced a complete, wallet-created two-input private asset payment in 183,224 B including both encrypted receipts. The implementation took about 2.13 CPU seconds per warm public proof check. At 100M included operations/year, that alone needs about 7.42 cores under the stated mix. Profile C handles the same reference load at much lower validation cost. C and the N7P2 comparator retain their original consensus, notes and verifiers.

## What was proved

All four paths through the same four random leaves were proved against one independently computed fixed root. Each of the two parents hashes the exact FS01 tag, level and ordered 512-bit children, with SHAKE256 padding, zero capacity and 24 actual Keccak-f rounds. Every bit of the first digest feeds its privately selected position at the next level. A negative test recomputed the first parent on fresh leaves and the second on the original inner digest. Both hashes were individually correct, the expected root stayed fixed, and a fresh public-only process rejected the fully serialized disconnected proof. Mutating the expected root or a proof opening also failed. Tests covered ragged batches, two separate roots, internal round and checkpoint corruption and all bit-to-packed trace basis directions. [Four-path harness](harness/run.py) · [Captured two-parent proof and independent root](evidence/link/) · [GKR and circuit tests](gkr/README.md)

SGF1 then proved two newly funded inputs against one depth-40 tree with 13 occupied rows and capacity 2^40. A pinned genesis supplied eleven authorized native grants. Two independent post-genesis fixed-supply issues carried genuine SLH-DSA-SHAKE-256s signatures. The verifier replayed those rules and computed the ordered root and count from public records. The same private wires bind each ASK, owner, typed commitment, occurrence, index, path and nullifier. The two selected indices are distinct and below count. The verifier rejected the proof for the other valid ordering of the issues on the same genesis. The isolated funding fixture makes grants and issue records public. This test does not require a full tree or real anonymity set of size 2^40. [Funding circuit](funding/plan.py) · [Fresh funding results](evidence/FUNDED.json)

SGM1 compiled the full 99-permutation FS01 relation. A wallet process generated a proof using the private openings of one native and one asset input from a separate fresh signed prefix. It sent 7 units, paid 1 native unit in fees and produced the corresponding token/native change. Two 1,792-byte ML-KEM-1024/HKDF-SHA384/AES-256-GCM receipts opened in separate recipient helper processes with their corresponding backup material. A fresh verifier read only public evidence, recomputed the anchor and intent, and checked the full proof. Changing either ciphertext, the fee or proof bytes caused rejection with intact packet framing. Twelve additional invalid **clear assignments** included 64-bit wrapping, invalid asset type, out-of-range index and altered ownership context. Three positive clear assignments exercised two real inputs, the inactive duplicate policy and sums above 2^64. [Complete circuit](payment/monetary.py) · [Ordinary payment fixture and measured bytes](harness/payment_run.py) · [Canonical public verifier](harness/payment_role.py)

The experimental magic and proof domains select new local registrations. The ordinary packet has the same field widths as FS01. Nothing here admits a new suite on the deployed chain. Live work selection, current spentness, duplicate economic identities, fee credit and joint swap inclusion remain ledger duties. The complete payment was verified against the signed funding prefix. The two-owner price retains two separate proofs and signatures; a negotiated, mined swap under this suite was outside this run.

## Where the bytes go

This is a round-batched GKR using the bilinear Keccak layer from SmallWood+ §6.2. We flatten the 24 rounds along the public batch axis and reduce adjacent groups to the same committed checkpoint coordinates. Our binary-to-packed opening uses a new trace-based ring switch for arbitrary wiring. It differs from the authors' Fig. 12 hiding-point MLE protocol. The November 12, 2026 PDF has SHA-256 `6a650832d5f90e5a9f09c56857851cbbb22eb2116ae25ff30d8d716ab899bfec`. [Feneuil, Provin and Rivain](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2880) · [Pinned source and limitations](../hidden-membership/smallwood/SOURCES.md) · [Round transitions](gkr/README.md) · [Exact ring protocol](ring/README.md)

The frozen DECS/LCS uses base field GF(2^16), challenge field GF(2^256), pack 91, 60 distinct opened columns, 4,096 commitment leaves, 75 degree-test combinations, 64-byte hashes and 32-byte leaf salts. The complete proof has 783,808 committed binary coordinates and 584 wide trace masks at count 13. Its 647 packed witness/trace/pad rows cost 120 B each, or 77,640 B across all openings. The masked GKR/selection/side transcript is 18,688 B. The remaining **82,906 B are shared once** across all permutations. Those bytes comprise 12,840 opened masking rows, 1,920 salts, 46,080 individual sibling hashes, 13,650 degree completion, 7,744 Q, 128 digests and 544 for the two public ring-switch messages. All terminal projections and masking coordinates enter the size. Public funding records and verifier code are required separately. The anchor commits to 13 publicly checkable funding records in this experiment, represented by a 2,424-byte genesis and two 31,932-byte issues. They need not accompany each payment. No additional prover-supplied table or secret verification key is assumed. [Independent serialization calculator](costs.py) · [Full geometry](COSTS.json)

## Security and the next decision

The public Fiat–Shamir transcript binds the locally registered suite, field parameters, flattening and binary/trace dimensions. A funded statement additionally pins the whole genesis, branch-derived chain/root/count, note hashes, nullifiers, intent, fee and authorizing digest as appropriate to the selected role. GKR's cubic row and quadratic map reductions consume separate precommitted masks. Its terminal projections, fixed bits, direction-product constraints and masked quotient are authenticated in one LCS opening. In the ring reduction each of 256 disclosed base-field values is padded by an independent Artin–Schreier image. Its trace reveals one already-public RHS bit. A separate basis-sized pad makes the next disclosed extension-field projection uniform in the honest interactive view. Random weights drawn after both disclosures bind the component equations and all claimed pad squares to the commitment. The independently reviewed argument covers these clear-message distributions and local checks. [Algebra and negative controls](ring/README.md) · [GKR masks and linearization](gkr/README.md)

The registered column degree and subset terms have exponents 267.44 and 256.19. They cover separate interactive errors. The two new ring consistency/batching terms add at most 2/2^256 under fixed binding and independent interactive challenges. A single degree-three sum-check round over the chosen challenge field gives a factor up to 3/2^256. Row equality and multiple rounds add other terms. Neither the configured field width nor those two exponents establish a 128-bit quantum money bound. The SmallWood+ DECS extractor reads a classical prover's ordered hash queries. The Chiesa–Manohar–Spooner QROM result requires a corresponding IOP knowledge-prefix property and a source-matched BCS transform. That correspondence has not been shown for these interleaved masked GKR, trace-square, degree and Fiat–Shamir messages. In the illustrative loss terms at 2^128 quantum queries, multiplying a putative interactive error by 2^256 can erase a local-only bound. At 100M operations/year over twenty years there are 2.2 billion legs; a separate uniform per-leg union bound would cost about 31.0 bits, unless the reduction already handles that global budget. A larger 512-bit field with adjusted opening parameters would price at 222,896 proof bytes in the calculator. It remains an unimplemented sensitivity case and still needs its own theorem. [Primary QROM result](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/834) · [Source/assumption review](../hidden-membership/assurance/REVIEW.md)

The proof carries private asset IDs, amounts and child orders. An observer also sees the anchor, fee, nullifiers, ciphertexts, timing and swap role/approvals. Reusing the same inputs reveals matching real nullifiers, and a later backup disclosure opens corresponding receipts. The 13-row experiment provides no quantitative anonymity set. Complete adaptive zero knowledge with this transcript and receiver-key privacy in the joint ledger/receipt view both remain open. [Receipt, retry and funding games](../fresh-spend/binding/GAMES.md)

In a separate instrumented replay of the valid 183,224-byte packet on the same host, Python cProfile recorded 2.743 s inside the proof call. Of that, 1.391 s was in `ring_switch._word_coefficients` transforming the public terminal coefficients, and 0.717 s in `Context.finalize` aggregating wire and trace constraints. These are inclusive instrumented intervals for two disjoint calls. Even eliminating the entire first call would leave more than the 100M budget in this replay. ## Reproduction

Run the command below on Linux x86-64 with CPython 3.12, NumPy 1.26.4, GCC, Python headers, OpenSSL, GNU time, curl, xz, PCLMULQDQ and SSE4.1. The runner downloads Node v24.22.1 with a pinned SHA-256, rebuilds the native field module, checks a public proof, creates new keys and signed funding, then reruns the branch, ring and complete monetary checks. All private data stays in memory or pipes. Public test artifacts and role timings go to the specified new directory.

```sh
bash experiments/structured-keccak/reproduce.sh "$(mktemp -d)/run"
```
