## Meaning of the parameter change

I inspected the four 1,481-byte serialized keys. Both B roles have 32 FRI queries, a rate of 1/32, a height-four cap with 16 hashes, zero grinding, two PLONK challenges and five four-way folds. Their A predecessors have 26 queries. Both generations use 135 wires, 60 routed wires, a quadratic Goldilocks extension, row blinding, hiding leaves and a configured 128-bit threshold. The trace changes from 4,096 to 8,192 rows, the LDE from 131,072 to 262,144 points and the terminal polynomial from four to eight extension coefficients. The upstream blinder budgets ordinary and copy-linked rows separately and rounds the trace size to a power of two. Each additional query reveals more positions. Under the library's conjectural estimator, the query portion changes from 130 to 160 bits and the extension-order cap leaves both configured screens at 128. With a fixed acceptable set occupying one quarter of the domain and independent uniform samples, the exponent instead moves from 52 to 64. Neither calculation evaluates the probability of adaptive acceptance of an invalid funded spend, quantum knowledge extraction or the joint distribution of the proof with real receipts. The node's framing covers the serialized proof **including** an 8-byte vector length and 31 or 34 public words. Subtracting those fields gives 139,378 B of proof-only material under A and 179,000 B under B, for both roles. ## Funding and privacy

Reusing the note commitment, occurrence, spending authority and real-nullifier derivation lets either eligible proof consume a note already in the same tree. A holder needs its original 32-byte spending backup and a complete authenticated history. One current branch spent set covers both suites. The version is visible, and proposals sharing the same real nullifier remain linkable across versions. Paying a migration operator, disclosing note ownership, or creating a separate postfork genesis would change the experiment's trust and privacy conditions. The existing FRI, four-word Poseidon2 and receipt assumptions remain consequential. The circuit does not independently bind the count and its implied depth, index bound, canonical sibling ordering and per-index padding. Recipient acceptance also needs a locally decrypted, owned opening. Public signature and proof checks alone do not reveal who controlled a particular note or the privately exchanged amount.
