# What an accepted C supply packet establishes

**Scope.** Candidate height 25 onwards, one selected local profile-C ledger. The registry records public outstanding quantities. The hidden quantity in a funded commitment, the ability to identify that commitment on a spend, and the ability of a particular recipient to recover and spend it are three different properties.

**Evidence labels.** **Source-checked** identifies a deterministic check or a state update in the deployed implementation. **Kernel-proved** identifies a result about the definitions and hypotheses in the named Lean files. **Tested** applies to the exercised projections or fixture histories. **Assumed** identifies a cryptographic or execution-interface premise. **Open** identifies a representation/refinement step whose general case the measurements and cited earlier theorems do not establish. In particular, source correspondence and finite wire tests alone do not certify a universal refinement of a native ELF or SQLite.

## What the executable refinement checks

For `FundedSupply.Gates`, `ri/rj/ru/rv` come from the native and token range checks. `nativeEq` and `tokenEq` use the two balance wires. `encoded`, `emptyBurn` and `publicBinding` refer to the split public delta, the zero-magnitude burn exclusion and the four selected ID limbs. `issuerInput/issuerDummy` come from the issuance products. `zeroIdGate` connects equality of the whole **private** asset ID to zero with equality of `available` to zero. The four `tag` predicates use the zero-token selection inside the typed-opening hash. `di/dj` use both dummy-zero products and their safe Boolean selector (`circuit.rs:78`). The public reply makes the descriptor observable. The quantities and tags of the four openings require the actual satisfying witness. [Gate definitions and integer-lift proofs](../funded-supply/Supply.lean)

In the signed path, generated `main.rs:96–109` dispatches `V || pk || digest || signature` to `qnero_notes::encrypt::issuer_verify`. Generated `encrypt.rs:226–236` requires exactly `32+32+17088` body bytes, parses the public key and signature and verifies bytes 32–63 as the message. Failed parsing or verification does not yield the success byte. That concrete branch supplies the worker verdict used by the executable registry policy. It does not create a second supply slot. [Issuer verification source](../managed-assets/native/issuer.rs)

`parse` consumes the full MSP3 frame, reconstructs it byte for byte and checks the MNG3 payload boundary. `decodeReply` takes that inner and a 3,798-byte worker result, checks the ciphertext suffix against the packet, and decodes the nonzero asset, signed delta, public effects and depth. Each successful `Decoded` carries proofs of the positive magnitude, exact high-bit decomposition and equality between the sign and the opcode. `fixedId`, `managedId`, `message`, `authFrame` and `commitRow` implement the concrete byte domains and registry calculations. For an exact pre-state row and first published NF, `preflight_refines` maps the executable four-opcode policy to `Rule`, including the newly computed row. The signature verdict here is the answer of a parameterized worker to that exact `V` request. [Executable refinement](Refinement.lean)

Two interface boundaries matter when reading that result. First, `ctx.opens` relates a worker request for the very same inner bytes to the descriptor and its **four public NF/CM fields**. Matching this projection does not identify all the hidden values, keys and paths of an arbitrary Lean `Payment`. A witness must satisfy the registered relation and open the corresponding historical commitments. Second, the Lean decoder requires 63-bit asset limbs. The public Python effect parser checks canonical field limbs. The tighter limit holds for a successful active registered circuit through its split and selector constraints. The byte-for-byte re-encoding guard is also a concrete condition on Lean parser success. Source correspondence and differential replay support these interfaces. Neither constitutes a universal proof that every accepted Python/Rust byte string reaches the Lean decoder. [Public replay driver](replay.py) · [Independent executable decoder](Check.lean)

`residuals_gates` proves `Gates` from the executable arithmetic residuals of a separately supplied, range-typed `Opening`. Field operations, Boolean flags and tagged amounts are checked or constructed there. A public worker response alone cannot supply that opening. `vm` checks the selected public head, activation, anchor height/hash/count/depth, fee, distinct effects, unspent NFs and fresh CMs. `vm_fresh` extracts the six public effect predicates from a successful check. `derive_admission` then uses `Represents` and `BirthFacts`. The former requires a chronological vector of full notes whose CM projection equals the ordered public output vector and whose permutation equals the earlier formal inventory. Both public CMs and spent marks are duplicate-free, and the public marks permute to the ghost marks. The latter supplies bounded indices of real occurrences in the exact prefix at the selected ancestor, old live-NF uniqueness, dummy separation and fresh true output identities. `toInput` uses the decoded published NF as its abstract true NF. The assumption matching that note to the real funded occurrence carries the secret-key/NF correspondence. `extend_projection` preserves this representation for appending two slot outputs and two published marks in physical order. Authentication of that prefix and enforcement of cross-slot conflicts by the deployed node retain their implementation and cryptographic correspondence obligations. ## Selected SQL state and note inventory

* **Ordered births.** The physical slot order is the order in the decoded block vector. `chain.py:374–380,395–419` appends each slot's CM0 and CM1 in order, then one reward CM. SQL stores these in `outs(pos,cm,h,ct,reward)`, with unique position and unique CM. Selected rows retain spent commitments. The quaternary frontier is reconstructed from the preceding count/frontier and updated in precisely this order. `experiments/advancing-payments/state.py:18–66` hashes sorted groups and pads absent children with zero. An oldest-to-newest birth vector can model the frontier. The reverse list used by earlier `settle` models only membership and holdings sums. An unordered set or a bare permutation cannot in general serve as the frontier.
* **Consumption and registry.** `spent(nf PRIMARY KEY,h)` receives **both** published NFs, including a dummy's. The old note CM is never deleted on spend. All accepted nonzero supply deltas append `assets(id,sequence,mode,authority,supply,h,tx)`. The highest selected sequence is the logical row, with fixed and managed policy intact at quantity zero. Transfer and reward append no supply row. The aggregate `fees` counter is historical. The one reward separately receives the subsidy plus this block's fees, with zero tokens. Node-side order and the payment-level `settle/write` projection agree on these quantities once the public effect vector is matched to distinct historical openings. Different asset updates commute on latest-row lookup because a block may have at most one nonzero update per ID.
* **Rejections and branches.** `chain.py:209–218,395–420,456–505` reconstructs the candidate from its selected parent in one SQLite write transaction. Invalid blocks roll back, losing candidates roll back their selected rows and may retain checked archive records, and preferred forks drop selected rows above their common prefix and replay in order. `txloc` can contain detached packets. Current supply comes from selected event rows. A reorganization can legitimately restore old notes/NFs/issuer heads and admit an old packet again if it meets the new branch checks. Per-branch NF uniqueness makes no assertion about two alternative branches. A retained validated block's idempotent lookup is distinct from verifying a new block.

This SQL projection is specified from released code and tested on the [prior selected and reorg histories](../funded-supply/README.md). Its formal use requires a simulation relating selected SQL rows to the Lean state. It does not give a verified SQLite implementation, a general proof of fork choice, or a cryptographic argument for every possible historical input.

## When true nullifiers can coincide

For a compatible real opening, write `r = H(OPEN,asset,token,raw)`, `pk = H(PK,H(AK,ask),nk)`, `cm = H(CM,H(NOTE,pk,rho,r),native)`, and `nf = H(NF,nk,rho,r)`. Distinct CMs alone imply no separation of true NFs. The NF preimage omits the native amount and pk. Two abstract note openings with equal `(nk,rho,r)` hash **the identical input**, even if their CMs differ. No hash collision is needed for that mathematical observation. It provides no evidence that such unqualified openings are both reachable births on a valid selected chain.

There is stronger structure in the registered ordinary/supply birth rule.

1. For each output, `circuit.rs:313–325` computes `rho = H(RHO,NF0,NF1,slot)`. The sender chooses pk and raw, but has no separate output-rho assignment. Slots 0 and 1 differ in their last preimage field. Distinct payments on one selected history differ in their ordered NF pair because every previously settled NF remains spent and two concurrent slots of one block must have pairwise distinct published NFs. Hence distinct transaction output occurrences have distinct **rho preimages** for these birth rules.
2. For comparable, compatible openings, no collision on those rho preimages implies distinct rho values. Distinct rho values imply distinct real-NF preimages even if senders reused `nk` or effective randomness. Equality of their true NFs would now require a hash collision. Changing raw, native, or pk does not remove the rho argument from the NF preimage.
3. A reward derives rho by byte hashing `AST1/reward/rho || context || pk || reward_le64 || zero_le64`. The context includes a network domain, selected parent, height, miner pk and packet-vector hash. There is one reward per selected height, so different such heights have distinct context preimages. The actual byte-to-field encoding begins with the little-endian 32-bit word for `AST1` (`0x31545341`). The transaction rho encoding instead begins with `RHO = 0x716e0006`. They are distinct field messages. This argument additionally uses the SHA-256 context and Poseidon2 binding assumptions. Selecting a new nonce on another branch does not grant per-*branch* uniqueness across forks. [Hash encodings, domains and independent reward reference](../funded-membership/reference.py) · [Funded chain and birth sources](../funded-supply/MAPPING.md)
4. The historical AST1, managed and signed-leg builders also invoke the common `build_constraints` and inherit output-rho derivation. The B generator changes the proof profile, and the C generator adds funded-depth checks. Swap pair admission rejects duplicate NFs and CMs across legs before the block-wide check. These observations identify relevant birth rules, but do not extend the C supply kernel theorem to the old proof systems or swap settlement. [AST1 source](../user-assets/native/profile.rs) · [Managed source](../managed-assets/native/profile.rs) · [Signed-leg source](../atomic-swap/native/profile.rs) · [B generator](../funded-upgrade/build.py)
5. Against an old published **real** NF, uniqueness requires the earlier spend to have a compatible funded birth by the same argument. Against a **dummy** NF the preimage starts with `NF_DUMMY`, distinct from `NF`. Provenance must cover any imported A/B, swap or other accepted output origin and reward, or be an explicit condition on the prefix. It cannot be inferred solely from two current MNG3 statements or from looking up a fresh CM. Within one proof, deriving each real NF from the same funded opening is what converts duplicate consumption of one note into duplicate publicly visible NFs.

**Consequence of an alias.** The earlier Lean holdings function excludes **every** funded note whose true NF occurs in `spent`. If two positive openings shared it, settling it once could exclude both even though the payment equation debited only its own witnessed input. A fresh NF of one non-dummy input could also coincide with an already used identity and strand value. Pairwise uniqueness, birth provenance and domain separation are materially used in the lift from local monetary equations to that projection. We have not established a same-NF pair of distinct admitted transaction births without a violated binding/origin assumption. No alias was constructed against the verifier or node.

**Open output-key obligation.** The output pk is an arbitrary private digest in the accepting relation. Recipient-side knowledge of `ask,nk`, existence of an efficiently available opening of that pk, and decryption of the ciphertext to the intended note are not output gates. Even binding does not imply a preimage for an arbitrary supplied pk. The fee-paying real-input proof concerns the inputs only. Thus `∃ trueNF` for every created output under the earlier *total* opened-note representation is not established by public acceptance. Opening coverage for selected consenting fixture wallets is evidence only for their receipts. A future representation could keep the face value and occurrence identity of every birth and assign spending identity only when justified by an opening, then debit the matched occurrence. That change would need a separate argument that authorization, hiding, matching and selected-state conservation are preserved. It is not a property claimed of the current chain.

## Partial burn with token change

The consensus equation allows positive token change on a burn. The released native C wallet already implements it. Generated `wallet.rs:81–86,113–119,306–316` subtracts the public burn magnitude and puts the remainder in change. Its generated relation tests already include the 7-input, 5-output, 2-burn case. The released high-level redemption path deliberately requires an exact returned receipt and zero token change (`devnet/redemption.py:6–32`, `managed_wallet.py:144–169`, `asset_wallet.py:98–130`). This local request policy does not enter the proof, wire or node. A test with a consenting funded owner can call the same released `CWallet.compact_payment` on its own genuine selected input and witness, use an unsigned burn envelope and submit through regular validation. Such a run establishes concrete partial-burn support only when the owner opens the input and surviving output and the node selects the corresponding payment block.
