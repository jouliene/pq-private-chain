# Direct Qnero roles at default wallet occupancy

The table prices the demand for 100M offers in a 365-day year. Neither inclusion scenario fits the published chain unchanged. At the public 120-second target, the payment needs 361.49 or 380.52 inclusions per block. Normal dispatch is limited to 75% of 5 MiB, or 3,932,160 bytes. At 154,508 B per complete standalone extrinsic, that permits at most 25 per target block before competing normal traffic, or 6,570,000 per year. Even the entire block disregarding class reservation fits only 33. This is an optimistic byte ceiling. It is separate from the 6-second block reference-time limit, 75% normal-class reservation and 2,048-output per-block cap. The 12-second development preset is not the public target. [Runtime](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/chain/runtime/src/configs/mod.rs), [block time](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/chain/runtime/src/lib.rs), [profile](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/docs/PROTOCOL-PROFILE.md).

The projections use decimal units and the existing researcher profile of 80/16 useful Mb/s, two validation core-seconds/s, six inventory peers, one requesting payload peer, 72-byte inventories/requests and 12-byte object framing. The peer also requests one full object upstream. The table charges inventories per phase and inventories/requests per block, without charging unknown shared header, mining, other extrinsic, TCP or P2P payload bytes. *Wallet/source.* The wallet-to-builder alternative still sends the complete private proof and ciphertext pair. The ordinary wallet's fee is eight public pool steps at this output length. Different payment sizes within the normal private range do not change these widths. A full uncompressed RPC response carries two hex characters per byte plus JSON. Randomized secrets and network timings change; reproduction should reach the same payload lengths and semantic outcomes. Distinct relays/retries, fanout and TLS costs are additional. One-time proof setup was 3.341 s on the sampled machine.

*Live builder and peers.* For standalone inclusion a block author and peers acquire and verify the submitted transaction. The pallet checks at transaction pool admission, pre-dispatch and dispatch. The block-only role skips pool intake, checks twice on inclusion and reads/writes actual state. Using the measured isolated native verifier once per check gives about 0.050/0.075 core-seconds/s of cryptographic component work at 100% for block-only / pool-and-block. Upstream's production WASM execution, proof parsing, payload sponge, DB, block production and invalid arrivals carry additional cost. There is no measured complete throughput result. A surviving **public byte** cache could elide a second payload transfer if receiver and sender implement compact block reconciliation and the whole object survived. Each block-only/incomplete-cache peer still needs missing bytes. Cache hits in this scenario save no cryptographic verification or availability duty. The bare sum of one copy of 600 s of offered objects is 294 MB at mean, 2.94 GB at 10×, and 9.27 GB for 100 offers/s. The independent 100/s stress exceeds the chain's byte ceiling by orders of magnitude. [Weight and verification path](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/chain/pallets/shielded/src/weights.rs).

*Pruned validator.* Body pruning after validation frees old proof/ciphertext data locally. Nullifier state, commitment tree, block creation heights, pending state, headers, recent bodies and rollback support still need backing. New settled nullifier/commitment values plus leaf heights alone are 136 logical B/payment, about 13.6 GB/year at 100% before hashed keys, maps, trie nodes, current and historical state versions, allocator and database amplification. Retaining 256 target blocks at the normal-class byte ceiling entails at most 989 MB of these payment extrinsics; at the hypothetical demanded 100% density the corresponding 256-block payment-only window needs 15.05 GB and would not fit the unmodified chain. A 256-block anchor window does not guarantee 256-block history service or a rollback horizon. No full production DB or pruning policy was measured. A pruned peer with neither old bodies nor a separate full archive cannot serve a cold wallet.

*Cold full validator.* A locally pruned node cannot reconstruct old ciphertexts or verify historical block contents from roots alone. The rows give payload floors against 90 days while continuing to follow live messages, using public bytes from an archive or a surviving complete byte cache. The native CPU floor does not measure a full replay. The exact accepted branch, authenticated acquisition, archive availability, PoW, state execution, disk throughput, forks and validation/recovery of missing data remain necessary.

*Cold key-only wallet.* The seed supplies spending and viewing material. A birth checkpoint can shorten scanning when it precedes every relevant note, while a seed alone needs history from genesis. The wallet fetches headers and authenticates links, note positions via trie proofs and relevant block bodies by their extrinsics roots; it tries ciphertexts locally and compares the resulting commitment. A surviving public cache must retain complete authenticated bodies and required public state history/proofs; a body or endpoint hash alone yields no plaintext. It still needs a trusted chain-selection/freshness source or a validating node. No PoW is checked by this wallet. A 100% one-year uncompressed JSON-hex payment-only stream takes at least 858 h at 80 Mb/s before RPC responses for trie data and other traffic, even if it could make progress.

There is an earlier hard failure in the default wallet: synchronization replaces its spentness set by enumerating the entire `Shielded::UsedNullifiers` prefix. Its RPC client accumulates entries across pages against a global ceiling of one million entries, one million trie-proof nodes and 64 MiB proof bytes. One occupied private batch settles two distinct nullifiers. Above 500,000 such payments, an honest full set exceeds the entry cap; proof-size/node limits can bind earlier. At 100M/year the entry-count boundary alone is 1.825 days (1.921 at 95%). Even 25 per target block exhausts it in about 27.8 days. The 1,024-header chunk limit can be traversed in one sync and the separate 100,000-entry shield-origin labeling limit has no spend-selection effect. Neither removes the full-set limit. Existing receipt on the four-leaf laboratory history cannot be extrapolated to one-year production recovery. [Wallet spentness and header walk](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/crates/qnero-wallet/src/wallet.rs), [prefix accumulation](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/crates/qnero-wallet/src/rpc.rs), [proof caps](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/crates/qnero-state-proof/src/lib.rs).

For spending, the native private default rebuilds the **whole commitment tree** locally from a public leaf range at the selected anchor, even if the wallet already owns its notes. At 100M accepted payments plus one coinbase per target block, year-end leaf values alone weigh 6.408 GB per rebuild, excluding indexes, storage proofs, hex framing and pre-existing state. Two 1,792-byte receipts per payment by themselves would total 358.4 GB/year at 100%, but the default native authenticated-body scan reads the embedded proofs too. Requesting only each owned membership path would change who learns the selected inputs. [Tree rebuild](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/crates/qnero-wallet/src/chain.rs).

This default occupancy misses both the 1 and 5 TB/archive-year screens at 95% and 100%. Neither screen is an owner traffic limit. The archive data, block cap and spentness cap identify distinct redesign work, even on a host with fast cryptography.

Upstream also preserved a runtime-WASM component campaign on a different machine. Its median 150,908-byte private parse+verify was 23.225 ms once and 45.825 ms twice in one runtime call. At 100% demand the latter is 0.145 wall-seconds of sequential component service per live second and 0.735 per second for 90-day catch-up while live, before state, contention and blocks. These are upstream measurements and derived service figures, distinct from the native verifier child CPU here. They do not change the serialized block-length ceiling. [Measured WASM components](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/docs/bench/2026-09-16-runtime-components.json).

**Native pre-inclusion gossip.** The pinned Qnero transport pushes one SCALE vector containing exactly one canonical extrinsic in each `/transactions/1` notification to each eligible full peer that does not know it, subject to channel capacity. Light peers are excluded. Thus the encoded application notification is 154,509 B with the one-element vector prefix. At 100M offers one unknown full peer consumes 3.920 Mb/s of upload on average, six consume 23.517 Mb/s before block relay, encryption, packet framing or retries. The inventory/request scenarios above reproduce the cross-design participation profile; the native protocol has its own push behavior. No transport overhead or actual peer count was measured on a real network. [Peer implementation](https://github.com/DigitalGuards/qnero/blob/f04c91307837c0231668383bec76ba98d89d2652/chain/client/network-transactions/src/lib.rs).
