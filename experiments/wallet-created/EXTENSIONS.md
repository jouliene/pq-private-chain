# Versioned transfers and indivisible two-owner exchanges

These are candidate relations for later circuits. This milestone implements only ordinary single-asset payments. New statements, commitment namespaces, wallet codecs, verifier IDs and fees require coordinated version activation. A peer cannot choose a verifier key or program. A validating node maps the installed network, activation height and operation tag to one approved relation, then checks public effects against its current branch.

## Token payment with a native fee

A note opening contains a version, canonical asset ID, amount, owner authorization and fresh blinding material. Bind these fields in the funded commitment, its occurrence, the private membership proof and the encrypted receipt. Use a single spentness namespace across operations, so a funded occurrence cannot be consumed once as a transfer and again as a swap. Migration between note versions must consume the old nullifier. Asset creation, issuer policy and authorized mint are separate relations. An ordinary transfer has zero authorized issuance.

For example, spend a 70-unit token note and a 12-unit native note. Pay 60 tokens to the recipient, return 10 tokens and 9 native units as change, and charge a public native fee of 3. This example requires a 2-in/3-out version or an explicitly priced additional transaction. A 2-in/2-out version can serve exact native fee funding: spending 70 tokens and 3 native units gives two token outputs of 60 and 10 and a fee of 3. Reject the same example with a 4-native-unit output, or with the fee taken from the token. For a fixed number of slots the circuit can compare hidden asset IDs by equality, group equal IDs under one canonical rule, prove each input/output belongs to exactly one group, and conserve bounded integer sums in each group. Add the public fee only to the native group's output sum. Range-check amounts and carries or bound each group total below the field modulus, including intermediate sums. A native/token selector must be constrained to the actual asset ID, with no duplicate aliases or unassigned slots. Zero-value padding, eligibility, public fee arithmetic and total supply limits require explicit rules.

## Two-owner swap

Alice owns 10 units of asset X and four native fee units. Bob owns 7 units of asset Y. They agree privately to exchange 10 X for 7 Y, charging a native fee of 3 to Alice. Her leg creates the 10 X note for Bob and a 1-native change note. His leg creates a 7 Y note for Alice. A fixed shape can pad the three outputs and input slots, with explicit constraints for padding. Both retain their own keys, paths and private non-hiding witnesses.

1. Both parties agree on a fresh session salt, network, versions, expiry, fee payer, fee, exact terms, participants' chosen output authorities and output order. Use a domain-separated hiding commitment to these terms with fresh sufficient entropy. Low-entropy trade terms hashed without blinding permit dictionary tests.
2. Each wallet constructs its own input nullifiers and output commitments and checks its own amounts. Exchange the ordered public effects. Form a common intent digest over the terms commitment, both distinct leg ordinals, accepted anchors and **all** ordered commitments, nullifiers, receiving contexts, fee and expiry. Before encryption the context omits the ciphertext-digest fields, which avoids a circular reference.
3. Each party seals its outputs in the agreed slot contexts. Exchange both sets of ciphertexts and compute a final statement digest over the intent and all slot-specific ciphertext digests. **Before authorizing its own leg, each wallet decrypts its incoming payment**, checks the commitment, amount, asset, future spending authority and role, and verifies the final digest. This consent step matters: a commitment to an arbitrary ciphertext alone cannot guarantee receipt or later spending. A party withholding its ciphertext or proof prevents execution.
4. Each wallet creates a hiding proof for its own authorized inputs and exact promised outputs, binding the same final digest, terms opening, fixed role and fee assignment. A block builder receives only the public bundle and both hiding proofs. The node requires exactly both complementary roles in one operation, matching commitments and public effects, both valid proofs and anchors, no overlapping or previously spent nullifiers, and the correct fee and expiry. It checks everything before publishing either leg, charging the fee, or appending any output. Reorganization undoes the whole operation. Ordinary one-leg admission cannot interpret these versioned authorizations. A builder repeating one leg, changing either destination, selectively settling a segment, or mixing signed intents across sessions must fail.

This design does not require a party to disclose a spending key to a counterparty or a block producer. Bilateral negotiation reveals terms and timing to both parties. The public bundle may reveal its swap shape, grouping, fee and expiry. Authorization privacy, extraction, fairness under abort, sound composition of both proofs, network metadata and future ciphertext/asset encodings require their own evaluation. No token or swap benchmark is charged to the ordinary-payment estimates.
