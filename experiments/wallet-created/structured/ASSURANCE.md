# Guarded complete-payment transcript assurance

The implementation proves the registered private 2×2 payment relation. The selected profile has an estimated 130.490158-bit **interactive** total error under the cited list-decoding parameters. The complete wallet executions establish functional proof creation, fresh verification, receipt and later spending. A concrete QROM anonymity or authorization strength for this implementation remains open.

## Relation and fixed choices

The ground field is `p=2^64−2^32+1`; EF5 is its degree-five power basis modulo `X^5−3`. The verifier owns the exact two-input PaymentAir, one constant plus 49 public words, 296,448 trace words, 316,084 deterministic product intermediates, 614,387 constraint rows and their projection. When original trace words are base-valued, all honest products and the witnesses to a satisfied triangular lowering are base-valued too. Canonical wire parsing, expected chain, valid anchor and its authenticated count, live spentness, output receipt-hash matching, fee and atomic admission remain part of the ledger. No MPC, proof aggregation or prover-selected program participates in this proof.

The transcript binds the complete canonical 528-byte statement, its nonce and both receipt digests. Its fixed domain lists field, arity, geometry and q values. A registered SHA-256 source manifest over the installed ordered payment crate sources and frozen lowering and the actual compiled verifier's fixed program/preprocessing identity material are hashed into the SHA3-512 initial transcript. Changing any of these changes all later coins; a matching installed library and source tree is a build/approval premise. Running test fixtures supplies neither the ledger's chain identity nor an authenticated anchor to the verifier. ## Joint interactive view

Consider first genuine independent uniform verifier coins, private independent field randomness, and the honest verifier with the query access allowed by CFW. Fix the full public statement and any authorized **base** witness for it.

1. The initial oracle encodes one message consisting of the witness, three EF5 terminal masks, the 110 EF5 outer masks, four independent base blinds and zero padding. Random RS seeds and correlated four-fold mask rows have the distributions required by the input encoding of Theorem 10.2. At a fixed base challenge vector b, the map from the four independent uniform blinds to the four base replies y is a translation of `Fp⁴` depending on the witness. Thus the joint reply vector is exactly uniform. Condition on b, y and the witness: its four blind coordinates are now determined; the independent inner and outer masks, RS seed words and encoding mask randomness retain their original distributions. This conditioning is essential when y and the later oracle observations concern the very same commitment.
2. Condition on b, y and the honest witness, then apply the outer simulator in Theorem 12.2. The non-Boolean last row challenge makes each masked A/B/C terminal independently uniform in EF5. Condition on these terminals: the rank/surjectivity property of Lemma 6.2 for the other masks makes the mask sum and all 21 direct polynomials uniformly distributed subject to the public sumcheck consistency equations. This statement covers the *joint* sequence, including dependence between the previous sum and subsequent polynomials. Consequently, the simulator samples public coins, four independent base outputs, three extension terminals and a consistent outer polynomial sequence. The combined public linear form and target, including guard coefficients and blind coordinates, follow deterministically.
3. Replace the initial bounded RS query view conditional on that explicit output and expanded message. The RS low-degree seed Vandermonde on distinct queried domain points has full row rank for at most q columns. Interleaved row seeds are independent. Hence at fixed message, including the guard blinds conditioned above and the spare mask coefficients, the source query distribution used by the bounded observer is simulatable. The outer reduction made no implicit-oracle query; direct outer and guard replies depend on message coordinates and public coins, never on initial encoding randomness. Therefore that encoding remains fresh conditional on the explicit view and the message, giving the premise for CFW's composed inner simulator. This is the argument for treating the guard as a refinement of Section 12, rather than asserting that four separately uniform replies imply joint privacy.
4. At each four-round inner fold there is one fresh multilinear mask with ten EF5 coordinates, represented as two correlated mask values per Boolean row. A single masked sum and four consistent degree-two messages are disclosed. Use the conditional simulator of Construction 6.4, retaining induced uniform source-column queries and folded mask/seed correlations. The next oracle encodes the folded main and auxiliary words, two folded mask coordinates, folded seeds and fresh pad words. Its OOD form uses a geometric row on the old coordinates and an independent uniformly nonzero vector on the fresh pad block (lengths 16, 2 and 1 respectively). Conditional on the old message and the OOD challenge, this pad makes its scalar answer uniform. The next encoding samples its independent row seeds; the code-switch simulator and fresh-output claim of Lemma 9.10 apply with this coordinate permutation. Actual prior-oracle queries feed the combined form. Counting seed privacy separately for every later transcript in isolation would miss the dependency handled by Theorem 10.2.
5. In the terminal protocol the independent fresh mask includes both a whole independent message and fresh seeds. The blind target and the public affine combination of the full 325 words are correlated. Their single linear equation and *both* authenticated code openings are exactly the observables in Construction 7.2. The terminal simulator conditions on that equation, including the previous oracle's induced queries. The recursive uniform-query simulators in the proof of Theorem 10.2 then yield the complete interactive view. Public code proofs assume fresh inputs and particular query observers throughout. The implementation uses separate salts even for the two terminal trees.

This extends the outer interactive simulation for the base guard and spells out which inner composition theorem supplies the remaining conditional distributions. ## Knowledge and compilation

Before the four coins, fix a candidate decoded message near the initial oracle. If an original word is nonbase, project onto a nonzero `EF5/Fp` coordinate. Each guard reply then imposes vanishing of a nonconstant univariate polynomial of degree at most 296,448, with its own arbitrary constant from that blind. The highest nonzero source term cannot be cancelled by the constant. Four independent base coordinates give a bound `(296448/p)^4`. Account for the first list bound 11,088 before using this probability. The resulting guard term alone is about `2^-169.85`. A dishonest reported base value changes the corresponding linear functional. The rho challenge comes after all masked terminals and guard replies. A fixed nonzero vector of discrepancies survives the eight-term batch except with probability at most `7/|EF5|` per candidate. The same decoded vector supplies every membership, owner, guard, nullifier, conservation and receipt-bound statement relation. The standard interactive extractor must first recover this vector from the constrained-code chain and then discard masks and intermediates. The list bounds, extraction and round-by-round state transition must be carried through the extra challenge and response round.

The prover commits the entire initial oracle **before** receiving its four base challenges. Each logical verifier packet has a label and a transcript digest. It expands through SHAKE256. Field limbs use rejection sampling; the last outer challenge excludes zero and one, the OOD pad vector rejects only an all-zero vector, and the power-of-two query domains use independent samples with replacement. Four guard coins and all per-stage queried positions/batch seeds are sampled as their respective packets without a prover choice between coordinates. The only round-changing prover inputs are framed roots and direct field messages. Merkle rows, salts and frontier witnesses are checked against prior roots and do not steer later coins. Each leaf binds tree identity, position, 64-byte salt and the complete canonical row. Each internal node binds tree identity, depth, ordered children and a separate domain. Exact proof sections and EOF are verifier-determined. The wallet samples 64 fresh bytes from the OS per proof and expands its private coins with SHAKE256. Computing the ideal independent distribution exactly therefore becomes a PQ PRG/XOF assumption in the implementation.

For the standard salted BCS transform, CMS Theorem 8.6 transfers round-by-round knowledge and HVZK into an argument of knowledge and ZK in the QROM. The theorem specifies its hash-chain/game; Remark 8.2 explicitly discusses dependence on the chain variant. The concrete application must map the domain-separated SHA3-512 chain and SHAKE256 coin packets, grouping/rejection sampling, mixed direct messages and multiple Merkle commitments to that compiler or prove an equivalent bound. It must also handle selectable statements, multiple users/provers, correlated receipt ciphertexts and adaptively selected repeated ledger transactions. Multi-theorem privacy must permit the adversary's auxiliary state and oracle history, including known notes and later key exposure. A proof for a single fixed valid public statement by itself gives no recipient transport anonymity, resistance to active invalid receipts or post-compromise confidentiality of past ciphertexts. The previous ledger/receipt assumptions, including malicious-payer behavior, still apply. [CMS theorem and hash-chain qualification](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/834) · [receipt and ledger boundaries](../../../docs/network-feasibility/OBLIGATIONS.md)

Even **if** the mapping holds, the theorem's generic bound uses terms of order `t²κ + t³/2^λ` for quantum oracle-query budget t, with verification queries and theorem constants also charged. Taking `κ=2^-130.49` as a conservative single bound for the selected interactive error vector, the first term reaches order one around `t=2^65.25`. At `t=2^40` that term is about `2^-50.49` before constants, hash terms or multi-user losses. These are sensitivities of a sufficient reduction and do not exhibit an attack of that cost. With λ=512 the collision term is far smaller at these two points under the ideal-output interpretation. SHA3-512 and SHAKE256 are concrete fixed functions, not the theorem's explicitly programmable quantum oracle. A stronger approved work-factor requirement changes the proof schedule and can change the measured network result. No system-wide or 128-bit post-quantum claim is made.
