# Monetary and joint-view assurance

FS01 begins from an agreed fresh founding record with honest grant authorization and independently validated fixed-supply registration. The public branch has an ordered occurrence log, authenticated root/count/ancestry, one spent set, supply registry and fee ledger. Both candidate backends are priced against the same private relation and lifetime NF rule. Authorization of pre-existing C or N7 funds requires the separately assessed legacy rules. The fresh design assumes no such funds or imported ownership promises. [Prior C analysis](../../funded-pq-assurance/binding/GAMES.md) · [FS01 relation](RELATION.md)

## Three adaptive monetary games

An adversary sees the entire selected and abandoned public history, can create accounts, choose issuance records and payments, act as payer/recipient, request authorized spends and proof verification adaptively, submit competitors, and choose when to retry or reorganize within consensus rules. It may compromise accounts outside the designated challenge. Each lifetime challenge fixes what was authorized when the victim was uncorrupted. A subsequent backup disclosure changes the threat model. Honest funding is backed by its creation-side opening, recovered by the challenger or obtained through the applicable knowledge assumption for adversarial creations.

| Game | Winning outcome on one retained branch | Required reduction or invariant |
|---|---|---|
| US, unauthorized spend | A positive debit lacks its uncorrupted owner's actual authorization of the selected funded occurrence and exact statement, or has no positive funded occurrence. | The accepted registered proof gives the same ASK/O/typed opening across ordered membership, NF and auth. Replay/reorg validates exact branch/count/height with current spentness. Any alternate opening breaks the specifically required node, CM or owner/key binding. Authorization cannot be inferred from knowledge of a receipt plaintext or public A. |
| XV, a repeated financial effect | Two retained debits of the same creation occurrence survive together. This includes different backends, roles, one-input padding, or a replay at another index. | Every real debit uses H(nf,ASK,O); it omits profile/anchor/index. One canonical branch occurrence has one issuing source and one typed opening. O collisions, different valid secrets/notes for one leaf, distinct per-role identity domains and non-atomic spentness updates are independent failures. Same CM under two independently authorized O values represents two credits. Two spends on mutually exclusive forks do not win. |
| IN, inflation or misallocation | A retained branch violates creation-side exact native or per-ID circulation/fee/issuer policy. The adversary may create both openings before funding. | Audit each asset using bounded integer amounts and original, coherently extracted openings. Native grants + authorized new subsidy (zero in the funding fixture) equal live native plus uncredited fee reserve. On reward payout recycle that reserve once. Fixed registry cap equals issued asset amount and never rises. Authenticate each issuance with its pinned key; enforce O and registry uniqueness, creation-to-spend consistency, two-leg atomicity, proper rollback and fee settlement. |

Changing only an opening's owner can win US. An opening that introduces a different amount can win IN. A colliding nullifier between distinct notes, a recipient who failed to preserve their seed, or a malicious payer publishing an undecipherable receipt typically destroys availability. These events become monetary wins only if they also satisfy the corresponding winning condition. A future bridge debiting the same old occurrence must consume its original NF atomically. Rotating the verifier or widening new identities must never assign a second spend key to retained old value.

A full reduction must bound conditional failures of the exact proof, public state header, ordered tree compression, mutually disjoint empty/occupied encodings, O, CM, owner/ASK, NF, intent/auth, swap signatures, issuer identity/signatures and correct atomic arithmetic/state. A membership-only argument cannot replace joint knowledge of the authorized values. We have no complete reduction for either proposed proof backend or FS01 as a whole.

## Idealized lifetime screens

At the owner's 100M offers/year, the research scenario with 20 years, 100% inclusion, 90% ordinary operations and 10% two-leg swaps produces 2.2 billion legs and 4.4 billion slots of each type (CM and published NF). Some NFs are padding. This is an illustration, neither a population of 4.4 billion different wallet seeds nor an upper bound on adversarial submissions. Counts also depend on new assets, failing and abandoned messages, repeated accounts, supply operations, branch reorgs and the number of retained keys. [Reproducible arithmetic](lifetime.py)

| Isolated ideal function game | log2 quantum query scale | log2 classical serial trial scale |
|---|---:|---:|
| One fixed uniform 512-bit digest target | 256.00 | 512.00 |
| 4.4B independent recoverable fixed digest targets with coherent membership test | 239.98 | 479.97 |
| Chosen 512-bit CM collision / two-domain cross-value claw with suitable domains | 170.67 | 256.00 |
| 512 independently random seed bits, pooled 1M distinct owned-address targets | 246.03 | 492.07 |
| 256 seed bits, pooled 1M distinct targets | 118.03 | 236.07 |
| 256 seed bits, counterfactual 4.4B distinct aligned seed targets | 111.98 | 223.97 |
| 128 seed bits, pooled 1M distinct targets | 54.03 | 108.07 |

Fixed-target Grover scaling is roughly sqrt(2^n/T). It assumes cheap coherent recognition of any of T known, comparably testable targets with a shared evaluation function; actual index lookup, attack parallelism, verification, reversible hash cost and memory affect elapsed work. A chosen cross-value CM claw concerns two legal domains with different quantities, chosen salts/openings and affordable funding of one side. A free-form digest collision can be economically irrelevant. The BHT quantum query model uses order 2^(512/3) evaluations and table entries in the usual sampled-table construction, with coherent access assumptions. The classical birthday model uses order 2^256 evaluations and a large table in its simple form. These are separate query/time/memory scenarios. No claimed physical machine budget or qRAM-free wall-time bound follows. [Multiple-target search](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605034) · [Collision, claw and space/time tradeoffs](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9705002)

FS01 uses the specified 64 random backup bytes end to end. User-entered passwords, padded short seeds or backups drawn by a weak RNG do not have 512 uniform bits. A reused account is one seed target even if it controls many notes. A public descriptor provides a trial predicate; for a fixed owner recovery one can compare the derived A. A compromised backup bypasses preimage search. The 32-byte ML-KEM shared secret and AES-256 key have different bounds and objectives; HKDF or digest expansion does not grant those inputs 512 bits of entropy.

SHAKE256 has a 512-bit capacity and FS01 asks for 512 output bits. Treating every tagged function as a truly independent 512-bit oracle is a heuristic. Alagic et al., v2 Theorems 2.1–2.3, model a random public permutation and inverse and give loose q-dependent advantage bounds. The stated full-rate sponge collision/preimage upper bounds on success are O(q^5 n 2^-min(r,c)); their unrestricted-input search discussion gives a preimage algorithm of order min(2^(c/3),2^(r/2)) permutation queries. With c=512, even the ideal unrestricted sponge has an approximately 170.67-bit capacity-related algorithmic scale. The theorem's r-bit output, truncation to 512 bits, fixed padding, per-domain message shapes, mixed primitive use, adaptive funding and concrete Keccak-f all require separate correspondence. Neither the ideal fixed-target 239.98-bit number nor the 170.67-bit claw screen is a lower bound on instantiated monetary security. [The Sponge is Quantum Indifferentiable, v2, §2.2–2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16887v2)

For adaptive attempts with proved conditional failure p_i against the full prior transcript, a union bound yields at most min(1,sum p_i). Under an illustrative R=4.4B and an actual per-attempt bound 2^-128, this is about 2^-95.97. Reaching lifetime 2^-128 with that R by the uniform union bound requires each total attempt advantage ≤2^-160.04 (rounding up). Multiple independent failure mechanisms also consume this budget. A single lifetime adversary with Q coherent hash queries must be analyzed with its actual aggregate Q and target set; do not multiply by R again for the same exposure. Neither this probability target nor 20 years of operation is an owner-imposed resource cap. No theorem here bounds the adaptive conditional p_i for either proof registration.

## Joint privacy, receipt and retries

Compare two experiments with matched public fee, arity shape, role/expiry, branch history and allowed disclosures, and matched adversarially observed scheduling/traffic. Select two honest, uncompromised accounts or input histories with those traces. Give the observer eligible-root choices, ordered NFs/CMs, proof bytes, full receipt bytes, block and mempool order, rejected and abandoned attempts, authentication tag, and the exposed swap fields. The adversary can fund notes, supply chosen descriptors/envelopes, observe agreement outcomes and obtain separate authorized payments. It must distinguish challenge recipients/inputs or amounts beyond allowed information. Exclude trivially known challenges such as asking a payer which recipient or amount it chose. Later key compromise must be analyzed separately. Signed swaps additionally disclose role pairing, terms commitment, public expiry, ephemeral keys, proofs, fees and timing; each counterparty knows both negotiated legs and its tested receipt. Link-layer metadata is separately visible.

A proof needs adaptive, multi-instance quantum knowledge/direct monetary authorization and a zero-knowledge argument for these *correlated* statements. The simulator must also cover auth and NF from the same secret, receiver-chosen A, payer-chosen salts/outputs, duplicate CMs, 1-real padding and private/abandoned attempts. A witness-hiding isolated gate, repetition count or a generic Fiat–Shamir theorem is insufficient. SHAKE prefixes enforce syntactic separation; the computational pseudorandomness and joint security of the keyed uses remain assumptions.

ML-KEM-1024 confidentiality for a valid public key does not establish recipient/key anonymity across malicious descriptors, robust cross-key decryption of the exact KEM/KDF/GCM composition or adaptive joint proof/receipt privacy. Context binds chain, O, slot, CM, CT and descriptor; the encrypted note must match A/CM. A malicious sender can still make its own payment unavailable. Return no public scan acknowledgments or per-key error details. The GCM tag has 128 bits; conditions on messages/blocks per key, retry multiplicity and valid-query access matter in any lifetime forgery bound. Fresh encapsulation per envelope limits honest key/nonce reuse. Wrong-key and altered-ciphertext tests check behavior only. Compromise of the seed recovers historical envelopes and current spend authority; there is no forward secrecy for saved ledger ciphertexts. Standardized signature categories, KEM categories and hash widths do not automatically characterize this joint game. [Final ML-KEM](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final) · [GCM](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/38/d/final)

The matched-metadata receipt test independently resealed both notes to the same recipients. Both genuine ciphertexts, intent and auth changed; the two funded NFs repeated. The later one-real-input onward spend produced different NFs. These are clear-relation and real-receipt observations. Reposting identical wire bytes is directly linkable. Rebuilding with the same real input repeats its real NF; a one-real retry also repeats its deterministic dummy NF. A different anchor, different sealed outputs or new proof randomness cannot hide those repetitions. Changing which inputs fund a retry can expose additional NFs and change the relationship to competing proposals. The wallet retains intended inputs and privately records outbox state. Revealing a change in the winning branch or the isolated signer's abort may itself leak information. Equal-length spending and constant-shape common scans do not prove equality of the entire resulting joint distributions.
