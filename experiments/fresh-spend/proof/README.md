# Generic VOLE-in-the-head on the FS01 spend relation

**Keep this as a priced comparator.** The smaller of the two screened binary encodings needs **10,747,278 proof bytes** for the complete private 2×2 relation. A fresh proof of one actual 512-bit FS01 leaf opening verified in a separate public process. A complete monetary proof and a quantum authorization/privacy bound for either profile remain unverified.

The prover samples a fresh synthetic 128-byte private leaf opening and independent private 32-byte commitment coins in memory. Its circuit absorbs `f1 05 || occurrence[64] || note_commitment[64]`, applies SHAKE256 padding, evaluates all 24 rounds of Keccak-f[1600], and constrains all 512 output bits to a public digest computed independently by OpenSSL. It declares 1,024 witness wires and 38,400 ANDs. The public fixture is the 64-byte digest followed by the proof. After the producer exits, another process verifies the fixture using the hard-coded circuit. Changed digest, transcript context and proof each reject. No private input file is created. ## Payment geometry

The bit circuit handles SHAKE directly. XOR, NOT, rotations and wiring consume zero additional VOLE slots. Each nonlinear chi bit consumes one AND slot. The declared FS01 schedule has 99 Keccak permutations across 91 hash calls, 40,960 ANDs for ordered path routing across two depth-40 inputs, and 12,432 other ANDs for the monetary, slot, equality and type constraints. Two input witnesses, two output witnesses, one activation bit and one asset ID contribute 49,233 private bits. The canonical decoder and ledger independently constrain public framing, eligible anchors, current spentness, policy, receipts and atomic state. Programs and parameters must be selected by the validator. [Boolean schedule](../binding/side.py) · [Public framing](../binding/envelope.py)

For this specific unoptimized schedule `ell = 3,854,992 + 49,233 = 3,904,225`. In degree two, the assignment occupies `ceil(ell/8)` bytes. Each of the `tau−1` correction strings occupies `ceil((ell+3λ+16)/8)`. The remaining serialized fields are the 48-byte header, checked VOLE hash, two QuickSilver coefficients, vector openings, final challenge, IV and counter. The calculator evaluates all sections and compares the result against the library's compiled size function. FS01 pricing uses fixed uncompressed frames. The last two rows count locally generated PRG output, excluding further XORs, tree work, transposition and validation. They are unrelated to network traffic. The four simultaneous allocations are the butterfly scratch retained in the prover context, the VOLE matrix, the transposed matrix and a minimum of one 32-byte tag per witness or AND wire. Other circuit wires need tags too. These are source allocations, not measured peak RSS. For EM-256s the four allocations exceed 4 GiB; EM-256f illustrates the trade for a larger packet. The current legacy codec's 262,144-byte physical limit and the researcher's 4 GiB service screen have different meanings. A new chain can price larger packets. Replacing just its envelope cannot reduce these proof and verification costs. On the success path `prove_commit_ex` allocates butterfly scratch in `ctx->tctx` and calls `voleith_commit`, which allocates `ctx->state._v_buf`. The caller keeps that context through `prove_respond`. Its call to `voleith_qs_prove` passes the live V rows and allocates `tags` and `V_T` consecutively. At the point where transpose returns all four allocations still exist. Only after the response returns does our caller destroy the context, releasing both the original scratch and V. Zeroing a scratch region in place does not shorten its allocation lifetime. The Talos extension-field backends implement arithmetic over characteristic two. Native 64-bit integer addition needs carries. The FS01 binary circuit explicitly accounts for them. Treating the historical N7P2 Poseidon2/Goldilocks operations as single Talos field gates would change the monetary relation. A faithful port would need reduction modulo the Goldilocks prime, range checks and a canonical encoding. The Holonym Circom/R1CS implementation uses a different, 254-bit prime and its authors record an undersized code-distance parameter. Those routes would require separate geometry and a new reduction. ## Transcript and randomness

QuickSilver with an ordinary interactive VOLE channel gives its verifier secret correlated randomness. A public validator cannot replay its private-channel transcript. The Talos library instead builds VOLEs from an AES-PRG batch all-but-one vector commitment. It supports general Boolean constraints, while the authors' FAEST signature fixes an AES/Rijndael key-knowledge relation. Both 256 profiles are real upstream choices. In Table 3.2 of the v2 specification the smaller profile uses 22 repetitions, 6 grinding bits and opening threshold 218. The faster one uses 32, 8 and 234. Both have two blocks per leaf commitment. The 17,984/23,476-byte official EM-256s/f signatures in that table price a 2,688-bit extended Rijndael witness. [FAEST v2 specification, Tables 3.1–3.2 and Fig. 7.2](https://faest.info/faest-spec-v2.0.pdf) · [Generic proof source](https://github.com/TalosLogic/libtalos_voleith/blob/255f1a995b8542de453efef57db7aa47a09f3a03/proof/proof.c)

**Private commitment coins are separate from public transcript data.** Official v2 Fig. 7.2 includes the signer's secret key in H3. The component instead uses the library's documented two-phase API with fresh 32-byte OS randomness supplied privately to the commitment step. The verifier reconstructs a separate public challenge from the application domain, instance and commitment. The public replay used this exact interface. End-to-end hiding under this schedule still needs review. [Two-phase API](https://github.com/TalosLogic/libtalos_voleith/blob/255f1a995b8542de453efef57db7aa47a09f3a03/proof/proof.h#L370)

1. The generic component fixes the circuit and parameter set locally. Its v1 header binds circuit and parameter fingerprints. H3 derives the commitment root seed and IV from this header and private fresh coins. AES-based vector commitment and VOLE expansion yield commitments, corrections, mask and correlated verifier columns. The proof transmits the IV.
2. H2_1 absorbs the fixed application domain, canonical public instance and reconstructed commitment blob (header, commitment digest, corrections and IV). Its 168-byte challenge specifies the first consistency check. The published masked assignment binds witness bits and AND outputs.
3. H2_2 absorbs challenge one, checked VOLE hash, checked column hashes, and masked assignment. Its 104-byte result supplies QuickSilver's constraint-batching challenges. Each AND checks a quadratic identity over GF(2^256), while linear gates propagate tags. The mask and two transmitted coefficients hide and bind the batched polynomial subject to the missing simulator and extractor analysis below.
4. H2_3 hashes challenge two, all three masked coefficients and a four-byte counter into 32 bytes. Grinding and a bounded vector opening select the final disclosed views. The verifier pins its own circuit, reconstructs the columns, recomputes the missing coefficient and all challenges, and checks the opening and final challenge. The pinned C implementation serializes the counter with host byte order. This experiment specifies little-endian Linux and does not establish cross-platform interoperability.

For a proposed full verifier, the application domain would include the registered spend suite, circuit version and operation role, followed by the canonical 526-byte statement. The node would recompute the intent from the actual selected anchor, root, count, NFs, outputs, receipts and role-specific fields before verification. The current proof-producing binary registers only the leaf. The full circuit compiler, its parameter manifest and an acceptance path have not been implemented. ## Security correspondence and decision

The original general VOLE-in-the-head analysis does give a sequential multi-use notion and a classical programmable CRS+RO NIZK/knowledge bound. Lemma 4 uses a special extractor for the underlying IOP, an extractably binding vector commitment, a trapdoor indistinguishability term and the adversary's classical oracle query records. The FS term includes `μ·(Q_FS+Q_verify)·κ` plus separate commitment terms. Its simulator programs challenge responses and extends across multiple proof queries. Classical access to the full list of queries does not cover superposition queries against a quantum oracle. This particular transcript also changes the public header and seed schedule and uses the v2 batch vector commitment. [CRYPTO 2023, §§3–4, Thm. 5 and App. A definitions 21–22](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/996.pdf)

CRYPTO 2025 and v2 §9.7 derive QROM **signature EUF-CMA** for the particular FAEST signing/key generation experiments. This argument includes round-by-round soundness, a lossy-key switch, PRG/leaf assumptions, random-oracle reprogramming and signing/query-dependent losses. Theorem 9.42 includes distinct oracle and signing-query terms. An honest randomly generated signature public key and an adaptively selected funded ledger root play different roles in a reduction. An intentionally funded leaf usually makes an existential monetary statement true. Safety requires authorization of that same input opening and nullifier, or an equivalent direct monetary reduction. A proof of false-statement rejection alone cannot supply it. A simulation for a signature oracle also leaves open the joint view of related receipts, proofs over two owners, later spends and reorgs. The authors' subsequent v3 submission changes the check and mask construction again. This experiment implements the pinned v2-style generalization. [QROM paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/164) · [Corresponding v2 theorem and assumptions](https://faest.info/faest-spec-v2.0.pdf) · [v3 change list](https://faest.info/resources.html)

A quantitative decision would need the extractor's concrete time/success for the generic 3,854,992-gate circuit, the QROM losses for its three public challenges, hiding under the exact private-coin schedule, and composition with adversarially funded receipts, honest-user proof queries and shared oracle access. The public validator must precommit to its compiled relation independently of the received header. Domain separation and canonical encoding must survive composition with ledger SHAKE calls. Fresh private randomness and successful component verification supply no joint simulator. The 64-bit auxiliary hash evaluation in QuickSilver and the 128-bit IV in the public commitment also occur inside the source; assigning all terms a uniform 256-bit bound would omit them.

For scale, independent uniform 256-bit private coins over 4.4B proof attempts have a pair-collision upper bound near 2^-192.93. A hypothetical per-attempt bound of 2^-128 union-bounds to 2^-95.97 at the same analyst-selected count. The public IV's ideal 128-bit birthday bound there is about 2^-64.93; an IV collision alone establishes no monetary or hiding failure. Real extraction, many-user reprogramming, related instances and correlated tree state are not independent coin flips. We have no concrete 128-bit QROM advantage bound for this payment.

The decision applies to this concrete binary SHAKE relation and serialized VOLE proof. To reopen this profile on the existing network, a different commitment/proof geometry must materially reduce the gate-linear correction data, or a new service model must bear multi-megabyte packets and the verifier work. Either choice separately requires a theorem or direct security reduction for the actual monetary and privacy games.
