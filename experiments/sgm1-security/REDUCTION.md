# Can acceptance of the registered SGM1 proof yield a funded witness?

**The exact four-group verifier at `3d37e597` lacks a 128-bit post-quantum monetary knowledge bound.** An explicit polynomial-time decoder works given the right partial oracle data. The base-field degree challenge and final column test admit concrete bounds with those data fixed. The missing implication is that a quantum producer of an accepted packet supplies such data at the appropriate prefixes with adequate probability, including when it chooses the statement. This analysis identifies a gap in a reduction. It provides no estimate of an attacker's actual success.

## Which result addresses the claim?

Chiesa–Manohar–Spooner (CMS) Theorem 8.6(2) has an additive extraction threshold of order $t^2\kappa+t^3/2^\lambda$ in a fixed-output quantum random oracle. The query accounting includes the verifier's expansion of the proof. Definition 8.5 requires an efficient extractor on *partial IOP transcripts*. When an upcoming verifier coin can move a doomed prefix to an alive one with probability greater than the knowledge error, that prefix must already yield a witness. Definition 8.3 additionally prevents a prover message alone from rescuing a doomed state. These requirements make the point at which a column or degree response becomes available decisive. CMS §3.3 fixes the instance for the knowledge game. The adaptive ledger game also allows statement selection after oracle queries and observations of honest payments. [CMS §§3.3, 8.2–8.5](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/834.pdf)

Don–Fehr–Majenz (DFM) Corollary 15 preserves an existing quantum proof-of-knowledge property for their constant-round public-coin interactive protocol under their Fiat–Shamir transform. Fixed SGM1 geometry makes the nominal round count finite, but rejection sampling can consume further draws. Even for a bounded, faithful interactive encoding, quantum knowledge of the actual commitment protocol is a premise. Corollary 13's adaptive bound contains the factor $n!/(2q+n+1)^{2n}$ and additive errors summing over instances to $n!/|C|$. Corollary 15 gives a qualitative preservation argument. Neither supplies the missing commitment extractor or makes a many-round concrete loss affordable by itself. We pursue the CMS partial-oracle route. [DFM Definitions 9 and 11, Corollaries 13 and 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05207)

Chiesa–Di–Hu–Zheng (EUROCRYPT 2026) address adaptive straight-line quantum extraction for the extended BCS transformation on an interactive oracle reduction (IOR). Their modular approach uses a post-quantum extractable vector commitment, demonstrates the corresponding property of their Merkle construction and relates classical round-by-round knowledge to post-quantum state-restoration security. Applying it would require exhibiting the registered protocol as the analyzed composition, with the specified vector-commitment extraction and IOR state hypotheses, randomness and query accounting. The published paper specifically distinguishes classical commitments fixed before extracting from commitments submitted in superposition, and allows queries over different rounds in superposition. The fixed-target lemma below proves only the classical combinatorial input to such a comparison. The putative SGM1 IOR and extractor for its complete compiled transcript remain to be instantiated. [CDHZ publication, abstract and notes 1 and 4](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-25336-1_14) · [Author presentation, including the three modular implications](https://zihanhu.cn/assets/pdf/pq-bcs.pdf)

The empty challenge binds the ring profile and dimensions without generating coin entropy. ### What the masked circuit contributes at an intermediate prefix

Fix the decoded bit assignment and its 584 wide trace masks for this count-13 instance. Each serialized sum-check coefficient is an affine form with a distinct mask coordinate. With that fixed assignment one can recover the claimed unmasked polynomial. Its value at zero is constrained by the old sum and the value at one. The next fresh K coin folds this polynomial and produces the next affine claim. A discrepancy already fixed to a nonzero degree-d polynomial has at most d roots. On the released payment the census counts 120 cubic chi folds, 55 quadratic affine-map folds, 12 cubic private-order folds and 13 cubic scalar-predicate folds. Conditional on a fixed incorrect fold polynomial before each independent coin, a union for these fold events is at most (120×3 + 55×2 + 12×3 + 13×3)/2^256 = 545/2^256. This is an interactive bound for those events only. It excludes initial equality-table choices, endpoint batching, the quotient check, DECS and the transcript compiler.

Eight terminal product equations then use a quotient interpolant checked at a point outside eight product nodes. One masked coordinate supplies a fresh interpolation blind. The check yields two affine residuals and the verifier compresses its bindings and residuals before invoking the trace-coset ring terminal. In a CMS-style prefix state, a bad circuit claim must either persist after the fold or produce an identified bad coin relative to the *same* decoded assignment. The algebra above shows why choosing a new assignment after a fold would invalidate the conditional argument. It does not establish a state function for all partial oracle transcripts or joint hiding of the masked messages in the QROM. ## A constructive prefix decoder

For this lemma, supply a finite **classical table** $D$ of correctly typed query/answer pairs. It can be a candidate database basis element in a compressed-oracle argument. It is not a log accessible to the validator, nor a description of an adversary's quantum queries. Reject incompatible preimages wherever uniqueness is used.

1. Starting at the named root, look up internal-node preimages by output. Traverse to depth 12, retaining only correctly ordered paths whose terminal hash has a leaf preimage with the required index, lengths, canonical column and salt. Mark a missing subtree or leaf $\bot$. The resulting partial data $T$ define $J\subseteq\{0,\ldots,4095\}$. Separately look up the unique typed preimage of $h_R$ and interpret its exact 22,650 bytes as the sample matrix of 75 degree-at-most-150 F polynomials. If absent, this prefix decoder has no committed R.
2. Compute $U=\{i\in J:R(e_i)=m_i+\Gamma p_i\}$. Interpolate every component of $p_i$ on $U$. With fewer than 151 positions use the unique polynomial of degree below $|U|$, taking zero for the empty set. With at least 151 use the first 151 sorted points and check the rest. Flag inconsistency. The good-degree argument below makes the flag unreachable under its hypotheses. All loops run in polynomial time. The decoder does not enumerate 152-subsets.
3. Evaluate the first 647 recovered polynomials at the packing domain $0,\ldots,90$, concatenate rows and remove public end padding. Parse the first $\lceil w/16\rceil$ F symbols as $w$ little-endian logical bit coordinates. The next $16\tau$ symbols encode the $\tau$ wide trace masks in the registered extension basis. Then come the 544 ring-pad symbols. Those last 544 symbols are part of the 647 rows. The separate 32 mask-polynomial rows belong to the LCS check and are outside the money witness. At the measured anchor $w=783,808, \tau=584$.
4. Replay the public allocation schedule of `build_payment`. The money witness is interleaved with intermediate wires. Record the bit IDs of the active slot, common asset, both inputs' ASK, occurrence, native value, asset ID, quantity, salt, index and forty ordered siblings per slot. Record each output's owner, native value, asset, quantity and salt. Read these IDs from the decoded vector, repack the declared little-endian scalar and byte fields in the original FS01 witness order, and run the clear monetary predicate on the canonical $x$. Its acceptance returns a witness of the intended relation. Merely finding binary wires or an opening to either root would not establish this step. A failed money predicate returns no witness.

**Fixed-root stability lemma.** Fix the root, $h_R$ and geometry externally. For tables with unique typed preimages, insert one new node, leaf or R query/answer. If the decoded partial columns or R before and after insertion differ, the new answer equals the named root, named $h_R$, or a typed child digest in a previously present internal-node input. Indeed, follow any newly resolved root path up to its first newly resolved edge. Its incoming digest is already named by the root or the existing parent. An R change can only occur at its named digest. An index/length mismatch yields no valid coordinate. Therefore a fresh uniform 512-bit answer has a classical one-insertion flip probability at most $(2s+2)/2^{512}$ if the old table contains $s$ internal nodes, counting only insertions remaining collision-free. A separate new collision among $d$ previously present typed 512-bit answers costs at most $d/2^{512}$ for one such insertion. The bound also controls changes in any deterministic predicate of these fixed objects under this insertion. It does **not** assume that all leaves are present. The proof handles the larger leaf record and flat R for these fixed targets. It does not establish the norm bound for extracting a prover-selected full transcript from a superposition of tables. Compare CMS Definitions 5.6 and 5.11 and the fixed-tree reasoning in §6.1. [CMS](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/834.pdf)

**A quantum subcase for the original flat digest.** In an *interactive* experiment in which the producer outputs a classical $h_R$ before proceeding, the flat hash has an applicable online-extraction result. Model the framed R hash as a 512-bit ideal function. Take the extraction input to be the entire canonical framed sample table and the tag to be its hash, so Don–Fehr–Majenz–Schaffner's function $f(z,y)=y$ has both ambiguity parameters equal to one. With $q=q_1+q_2$ ideal queries, Corollary 4.7 bounds the simulator's probability of a later valid opening differing from its extraction at tag time by $8(q_2+1)\sqrt{2/2^{512}}+(40e^2(q+2)^3+2)/2^{512}$. The simulated and original output views have the separate first-term trace-distance bound. Reject any extracted preimage with the wrong framing. One can include the verifier's reconstruction and hash of R in the later algorithm and its query charge. In the actual noninteractive game an adversary can submit candidate commitments in superposition to Fiat–Shamir without ever releasing a classical value at that time. Its classical output is the final packet. The subcase therefore gives a genuine quantum result for the isolated digest at a classical interface, without granting that interface to the SGM1 extractor. [DFMS §§4.1 and 4.3, Corollary 4.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03085)

**Degree and sampling lemma.** Assume the partial columns $T$ are fixed before a uniform independent $\Gamma$. For any fixed 152-index subset of $J$, interpolate its $p,m$ vectors at the corresponding field points. Write $a,b$ for their degree-151 coefficient vectors. A cancellation with $a\ne0$ requires $\Gamma a=b$. Each independent F row contributes probability $2^{-16}$, so even taking a union over all subsets gives at most $\binom{4096}{152}2^{-1200}\approx2^{-267.439}$. Outside this event every $U$ agreeing with any degree-at-most-150 R after this challenge interpolates a single degree-at-most-150 $p$. If a 151-point interpolant disagreed at a further openable index, that subset of 152 would give the excluded cancellation. This also covers an R chosen as a function of $\Gamma$.

Suppose the returned Q differs from the polynomial induced by this decoded assignment, registered affine weights and the decoded LCS mask. At most 241 points of $U$ satisfy its opening equation, by the root bound in K applied at distinct embedded F points. For $|U|\le241$, one can instead bound the event that all selected positions are openable. In either case a fresh uniform distinct subset of 60 hits only the eligible positions with probability at most $\binom{241}{60}/\binom{4096}{60}\approx2^{-256.195}$. If Q coincides and the public sum check passes, the aggregate residual equals the fixed sum of the decoded LCS mask on the packing domain. An arbitrary committed mask need not have zero sum. For any nonzero fixed residual vector, the 258 fresh K aggregation weights hit any fixed such offset with probability $2^{-256}$. The separate Artin–Schreier square batch has error $2^{-256}$ conditioned on fixed pads and the prior messages. These last two claims concern the registered ring equations, not all the earlier circuit challenges. [SmallWood+ Appendix D.1 and D.5](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2880) · [Registered ring argument](../structured-keccak/ring/README.md)

## The missing transfer at the actual prefixes

The two lemmas isolate a usable commitment decoder and conditional local error bounds. To conclude CMS Definition 8.5 for the money relation, one needs a state on every partial transcript that gives the decoder these objects at the stated times, composes the remaining GKR, product, selection and monetary checks, and survives extension by prover messages. To conclude the noninteractive result one must also connect an accepting quantum strategy to that partial-oracle representation. Three exact discrepancies matter.

- CMS §§6.1 and 8.2 extract a binary tree whose leaves are the IOP alphabet symbols and a specified challenge/root hash chain. The registered depth-12 traversal supplies digests of columns, and the R commitment is a separate hash. The fixed-target lemma accounts locally for recovering both typed preimages. However, a verifier at the end can reconstruct R from 60 columns plus 91 completions whereas 60 column samples do not determine the degree-150 witness rows. Inserting the verifier's late R preimage or opened leaves into a classical table does not show that they were present when an earlier challenge was chosen. Appendix D.1 of SmallWood+ uses a chronological list of classical queries to obtain this ordering. Applying that argument to a measured compressed database would change the adversarial experiment.
- CMS's challenge and predecessor hash yield fixed-width words with a specified interleaving. Remark 8.2 and §8.6 show that even changing which hash-chain inputs include the verifier message can require a stronger partial state. In SGM1 a lone K draw has 256 output bits although the state and node values have 512. At a fixed former state and label, a table can contain up to $s$ continuation inputs proposing different 256-bit values. The elementary bound for subsequently matching a guessed value is $s/2^{256}$, not $s/2^{512}$. This local observation is neither an attack nor a global loss bound. Multi-element draws need a typed variable-output oracle, including the whole degree matrix and repeated draws for rejected indices or challenge nodes. A fixed public 512-bit seed followed by a deterministic expansion does not justify the independent-row calculation by counting alone: for fixed $a\ne0$ the seed-induced distribution of $\Gamma a$ has at most $2^{512}$ values, while the ideal uniform target has $2^{1200}$. A computational expansion hybrid would need its own model and loss. Actual SHAKE256 additionally requires an implementation-to-ideal-oracle assumption.
- A full proof would need a generator/extractor for the registered statement and all challenge/message links jointly, with a bound for changes to the selected prefix, and an adaptive statement treatment. The local lemma fixes two externally named digests. Substituting roots chosen after querying, measuring after every call, or charging only the 60 verifier column queries does not supply that theorem. The returned witness still has to connect to the specific funded occurrence and ASK, with separately budgeted hash binding, authorization, branch-spentness and swap-signature failures. Confidentiality or adaptive simulation of receipts follows from none of these local knowledge claims.

**Early-R candidate.** Sending all 22,650 R bytes immediately after the degree challenge, removing its 64-byte digest and 13,650 late completion bytes, adds 8,936 proof bytes with identical field geometry. The measured candidate ordinary packet is 192,160 bytes, including its 188,170-byte proof. The decoder can then read R directly from the explicit ideal transcript. The verifier checks $R(e_i)=m_i+\Gamma p_i$ on each of the 60 authenticated columns; the same local degree and subset lemmas apply. A global quantum reduction must still bind the early message in its *actual* state chain. For honest interactive coins and fixed $P,\Gamma$, the full R is uniform in the 75-polynomial space because its degree masks are independent uniform polynomials. Given R and the opened $p_i$, the revealed $m_i$ are already determined by the checked equation. The original honest verifier reconstructs exactly this R at the end. Early disclosure changes what can be correlated with subsequent oracle queries. This marginal argument cannot simulate the earlier salted Merkle root, the future masked circuit/Q messages and openings jointly under adaptive statements. The candidate addresses one commitment correspondence and alone does not establish 128-bit monetary knowledge or adaptive joint privacy. [Candidate role measurements](evidence/EAR1_PAYMENT.json) · [Exact predecessor schedule](evidence/SGM1_TRACE.json)

A theorem with a quadratic query-loss threshold would have to leave a useful extraction probability at the claimed adversarial budget and success level. At $t=2^{128}$ even the local $2^{-256}$ term multiplied by $t^2$ is one. The big-O terms in CMS do not provide calibrated constants. This observation limits that instantiation of the bound. It neither proves 128-bit work for an actual debit nor supplies evidence of a cheaper debit.
