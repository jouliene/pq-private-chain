# Privacy of an SGM1 payment in its history

Compare honest histories with the same permitted public metadata, eligible funding, recipient availability and observer role. Give the observer public proofs, receipts, rejected proposals it actually sees and subsequent spends. The public research fixture contains transparent grants and signed issuance, so the number of tree leaves alone would overstate anonymity. | Observer | Information available |
|---|---|
| Passive chain observer | Branch, time/order, suite, ordinary versus two-leg swap, fee, anchors, two nullifiers and two output commitments per leg, intent authorization, both sealed receipts and the proof. A swap exposes its linked legs, ephemeral approval keys, signatures, roles, terms digest and expiry. The fixed ordinary layout accommodates one or two real inputs. Issuance, fee payer and public side information can narrow the possible inputs. Transport anonymity is outside the local test. |
| Counterparty | Its own notes, negotiated assets and values, peer descriptor, both roles, approval keys, signed terms and its promised incoming receipt. It can identify that receipt and correlate retries and released authorizations with inclusion. It sees the other public draft and can withhold its signature. Knowing who received an output does not itself reveal the recipient's ASK or its future real nullifier. |
| Coordinator | Role pairing, offers, descriptors, session salt, public identity or approval keys conveyed to it, negotiated economics, approvals, arrival order and drafts or abandoned signed pairs that reach it. It can correlate the drafts with public inclusion and withhold traffic. In the tested model it cannot select the peers' independent enrollment channel or change the pinned KEM key and obtain a draft for the altered pair. An actual deployment needs an authenticated association between a person and an endpoint. |
| Payer, recipient or later seed holder | A payer knows its own output descriptors and plaintexts. A wallet with the original seed scans common history, authenticates the tag and checks owner and commitment. A node verifies the authorized receipt bytes without learning their decryptions. A malicious ordinary payer can still create an unusable receipt at its own expense. Later backup compromise permits retrospective decryption. |

On the pinned public history, replaying an identical packet preserves its nullifiers, commitments and ciphertexts. A conflicting ordinary spend and the first swap share two nullifiers despite having no equal receipts or commitments. Two different ordinary transactions with matching fee, anchor, role and length have none of those equalities. The previously established onward spend and its antecedent have no equality in those fields either. These observations test particular equality predicates. They place no bound on an observer using other transcript features, traffic or chosen challenge histories. A receipt contains a 1,568-byte ML-KEM-1024 encapsulation, 208 encrypted note bytes and a 16-byte AES-256-GCM tag. HKDF-SHA384 derives key and nonce with a descriptor-bound salt and context binding chain, occurrence, slot, output commitment and encapsulation. A recipient-anonymity reduction must cover correlated commitments, both challenge descriptors, retries, auxiliary information and recipient checks. The published anonymity analysis of pre-standard Kyber and hybrid encryption does not analyze this exact payment context.
