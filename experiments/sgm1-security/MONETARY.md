# What an accepted SGM1 spend must imply

The target is an honest holder's money. A successful adversary causes an unauthorized debit of a funded occurrence, obtains two canonical debits of one real occurrence, or increases the native or an existing asset balance outside registered issuance. Use probability one half as the reference for a 128-bit work factor. Assume independent uniform 64-byte wallet backups, unexposed local secrets, correct signed funding and an eligible selected branch. The isolated SGM1 ledger implements eligibility and rollback. Accounting uses one selected history at a time. The live PoW protocol continues to use profile C.

Give the adversary quantum hash queries, honest public histories and chosen payment submissions. Allow it to create funded notes, obtain legitimate wallet authorizations within the agreed scope, choose public statements and select when to submit. A spend of an adversary's own note is authorized. To prove monetary security, extraction must yield openings for *that accepted transaction*, with a reduction from a first unauthorized outcome for an honest holder to extraction failure, hash-binding or key-recovery events, an authorization failure or a failure of the state machine. Correlation with earlier honest proofs and receipts is part of the game. [Monetary circuit](../structured-keccak/payment/monetary.py) · [Admission](../sgm1-admission/ledger.py)

| Required implication | Implementation and conditional reasoning |
|---|---|
| Funded and eligible | The node selects the chain, root and count from its eligible branch anchor. Each real index is 40 bits and below count. The circuit computes owner = H(ASK), commitment = H(chain, owner, native, asset, quantity, salt), leaf = H(occurrence, commitment) and 40 ordered, level-tagged parent hashes. Given a valid extracted witness, compare its path with the actual tree at the same index. A different leaf or path with the same root yields an equal hash of distinct inputs somewhere on that path. Different note openings of the genuine commitment require corresponding commitment or owner binding. This assumes circuit/compiler correctness, domain separation and hash binding in the relevant chosen-target games. |
| Authority for these effects | Both real input openings require their own ASK. Public authorization additionally hashes the *first* ASK and intent. Intent covers chain/root/count, version, role, fee, both nullifiers, commitments, full receipts and swap extension. The proof binds the public statement. In a swap both wallets approve the same ordered cores after opening their own incoming receipt and checking terms. The node verifies both SLH-DSA-SHAKE-256s signatures, roles and expiry. Relaying a previously authorized packet is permitted. |
| One canonical debit | A real nullifier hashes ASK and funded occurrence under the real-spend domain. When both inputs are active their indices must differ. For one active input, the second record copies the first, contributes zero value and uses the dummy domain. The node rejects duplicate nullifiers in an operation and checks current spentness again under its write lock. With a binding opening, spending the same real occurrence repeats its nullifier. A collision between different notes can prevent a legitimate spend. SGM1 and EAR1 have no spending authority in the live C store. |
| Conservation | For every nonzero quantity the hidden tag must equal the common nonzero asset ID. Integer additions enforce widths and equality between active inputs and both outputs, including the native fee. Two legs can exchange different assets. Both legs and their single fee commit or roll back together. Issuance authorization and cap are checked in the separately pinned funding prefix. |
| Extracting the witness | From the correct committed polynomials, decode both real openings, ASKs, private indices, paths, amounts and outputs, then check them against the verifier-owned statement. The missing theorem must show that a successful noninteractive quantum prover yields these data at the required prefixes with adequate probability. [Prefix decoder](REDUCTION.md) |

## Interpreting the numbers

The degree and subset errors condition on the column assignment, an independent degree matrix and fresh final subset. The isolated square and weight tests have a joint union bound of 2^-255 under their hypotheses. The cubic folds and eight-product interpolation have distinct local bounds. None supplies the round-by-round knowledge error of the whole proof. [Calculator](bounds.py) · [Results](evidence/BOUNDS.json)

For CMS sensitivity, let t include verifier expansion, set λ=512 and substitute a hypothetical κ=2^-255 with unit coefficients. At t=2^64 the two terms are 2^-127 and 2^-320. At t=2^128 they are 2 and 2^-128, which cannot certify positive extraction at a half-success target. CMS gives asymptotic constants. For DFM, grouping the 215 observed interleaved coin blocks as hypothetical rounds yields a log2 multiplicative loss of about 26,589 at q=2^64. Challenge widths differ in SGM1 and rejection sampling has variable draws. Even with a matching transform, DFM also requires interactive quantum knowledge and has an additive term. Neither calculation demonstrates forgery. [CMS §8](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/834.pdf) · [DFM Corollaries 13, 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05207)

In a separate ideal marked-item calculation, give Grover search a uniform 512-bit candidate space with M distinct usable marked candidates and a coherent recognition predicate. After q iterations the success is sin²((2q+1)arcsin√(M/2^512)), bounded by min(1,(2q+1)²M/2^512). At 2^128 iterations this upper bound is about 2^-254 for one mark and 2^-221.97 for 4.4 billion marks. The approximate half-success counts are 2^254.65 and 2^238.63 iterations. The latter comparison charges 281.6 GB of raw 64-byte identifiers before a reversible lookup structure, hash evaluations and uncomputation. Actual targets may be hidden, share owners, have more than one preimage, be spent, or require unknown salts and openings. These calculations count ideal iterations, with no physical gate estimate or across-strategy lower bound. An arbitrary collision can involve two adversary-selected inputs unrelated to a victim's funded note. Actual SHAKE256 needs a justified idealization. [Boyer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605034) · [Zalka](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9711070) · [FIPS 202](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/202/final)

At 100M included operations/year, 90% ordinary and 10% two-leg swaps over 20 years produce 2.2 billion included legs and 4.4 billion output slots. If every adaptively conditioned leg had failure probability at most ε, a union bound gives 2.2e9 ε, costing 31.035 bits. A per-output guarantee similarly loses 32.035 bits. Independence is unnecessary for a union bound. Included operations place no limit on failed trials, prior hash queries, attempts at signing or offline search. A theorem covering a global adaptive quantum budget needs its own aggregation analysis.
