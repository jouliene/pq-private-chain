# Delayed certificates over independently admitted DLF1 payments

**Adopt D8C1 as an optional historical evidence format on the experimental D8 PoW branch. Defer a mandatory network rollout.** A full certificate lowered one-copy historical cost enough to cross the 5 TB scenario in the registered occupancy mixes. It misses the 1 TB scenario. Original individually verified DLF1 admission works with the archivist offline. The new format requires a new PoW commitment and continued availability of the chosen evidence. It cannot compact the existing byte-committing APV2 or DLF1 chains in place. The global blockchain objective remains active. ## Actual outage, pruning, recovery and fork

Two real autonomous CPU miners continued while eight independently funded wallets paid the same offline address, one sender at a time with no certificate service. Each transferred 150 from two 100-unit mined rewards, paid 3 and kept 47. The first eight commands took 6.37–9.56 s to observe inclusion and each transmitted one request. The builder later used only their public packets. The eighth payment on the initial branch and a separate 151-unit ninth payer on the other branch used the same H50 block anchor. They authorize distinct inputs. The work-selected replacement removed the eighth placement and admitted the ninth. All other seven payments occur in both histories. The trace measures controlled arrivals with real autonomous nonce search. Its finitely many successful payments give no worst-case time guarantee. The aggregate proof covers named statements independent of placement. The node checks the original height, branch anchor, current spentness, fees, ordered outputs and rewards on each branch. An eight-slot artifact can contain a now-orphaned slot; that slot has no effect on the winning chain unless separately included and eligible. A SID hashes the D8 network, anchor/height, two nullifiers, two output commitments, fee and both complete ordered ciphertexts under a versioned domain. The receipt digest is checked by the registered native decoder. The sealed core commits to the ordered SIDs and resulting state independently of proof serialization; mining rewards use the same ordered vector. Checks for changed canonical-length proof bytes still run when an attachment with the same semantic identity arrives. [Block format](block_format.py) · [Stage and binding](stage.py)

Compaction verified the new ciphertexts/effects against each old locally verified authorization, installed replacement references and deleted the originals in a single SQLite transaction with secure deletion enabled. A process was forcibly exited after the deletion instructions and before commit. On restart the entire serialized archive matched the pre-crash interchange byte-for-byte. A successful retry and vacuum left exactly one certificate and the single uncovered payment at the alternate peer. The disposable full-source peer then stopped. The full source and private test fixtures remained offline for the independent full-replay oracle.

A new validator started with empty databases. When all serving sources withheld the certificate it validated through H20 and refused to mark H21 or its dependent suffix verified. A second start made no monetary progress with the same missing item. The replacement peer returned the certificate. The validator independently verified the H56 branch, then switched to H57 by cumulative work. The two monetary states matched direct complete-proof replay and the separate arithmetic oracle: 5,600 then 5,700 units issued, 24 fees and 16 spent inputs at both endpoints. The ordered funded roots, selected anchors, output counts and spent-set digests matched. A separate client obtained missing evidence from a second location; another replay used only the full and partial certificates. The cold validation process has loopback and explicit public mounts, with no original packets, wallet homes or private fixtures mounted. Previously validated database state survives restart; evidence verification results are memoized in process memory only. ## Where the savings are available

At 100M included payments per 365 days the owner specifies a rate, with no annual traffic ceiling. We use the previous sparse and busy block mixes. In the sparse mix, widths 1/2/3/4 have weights 85/10/4/1% and one additional empty block per paid block. In the busy mix the weights are 5/10/15/70% with one empty per nine paid blocks. The certificate creation schedule is independent of block occupancy; an archivist can wait across blocks while wallets leave immediately. Every certificate is charged for its actual public inputs, complete active receipt pairs, per-record framing, 32 B per included SID and all cores including empty blocks. At full occupancy each warm builder approves 0.1411 payments/s. The 3.171/s scenario therefore consumes **44.03 CPU cores on average**, or **23 simultaneous measured two-thread workers**, without spare capacity. Their combined peak can approach 150 GiB. An ordinary four-core home node can verify archives and retain direct admission; serving all archival proving on that one node cannot keep pace. Missing builders grow the uncompressed original-packet backlog by **39.23 GB/day** at the scenario rate, per retained copy. In the observed finite outage the original branch had eight uncovered offers, all eight were later certified; the winning replacement introduced one more uncovered offer. Storing the tested partially overlapping certificate increased archive cost by 43,796 B compared with keeping the outsider's original. Coverage policy matters: where all other batches are full, about 80% of placements need compact coverage to meet the 5 TB scenario. Underfilled/mixed batches, discarded forks, duplicate remote replicas, serving indexes, recovery scans, archive transfers and downtime are additional liabilities. A verifier downloading retained data and one relay of an original payment incur separate costs. The nominal independent block capacity is still **63.072M payments/year** at four slots and two seconds. No 100M/year sustained service or deployed annual SQLite disk extrapolation has been demonstrated. [Executable cost model](costs.py) · [Independent scenario calculation](review/THRESHOLDS.json)

## Assurance, alternatives and next decision

The inner DLF1 proof is hiding. Its native verifier and the active fee/authorization rules stay pinned. The outer non-hiding circuit verifies eight such authorizations using a constant DLF1 verifier. Its only witness is public authorization material and publicly defined repeat-last padding. The active-prefix selectors and all public statement words are constrained. Verifiers check real slot count, order and both ciphertexts. After deletion one can still audit registered authorization, amounts' conservation as a proved relation, public state transitions, fees, issuance, work and receipt availability. One cannot recover an original proof's exact random transcript, determine which peers originally relayed it, independently rerun a different future audit of discarded bytes, or infer chain availability/freshness from a valid certificate alone. A fresh node can verify only cores and sources that its peers advertise and supply. This test uses a bounded inventory and an optional second proof source. It does not establish discovery of the best withheld branch, permanent archival availability, or censorship resistance. On reorg an archive-only node can revalidate covered effects, but a wallet or peer with an original packet is needed to resubmit it through direct admission. The design retains DLF1's conditional FRI, hash and Fiat–Shamir assumptions and adds another recursive soundness/knowledge obligation. The configured 128-bit screen gives no end-to-end quantum guarantee. Cumulative PoW, receipt correctness by a malicious sender, anonymity of the network layer and adaptation to private native assets and two-owner swaps still have separate obligations. A future swap requires one indivisible versioned joint authorization with a native fee and atomic ledger effect; certificate slots cannot independently authorize swap legs. [Source-linked assurance](assurance/ASSURANCE.md)

The fallback experiment partitioned original DLF1 data among six archival holders. Any four reconstructed the actual blocks and the ordinary verifier checked the authentic original proofs. On the real four-payment test its total retention grew from 574,984 B to 884,706 B, and cold reconstruction transferred 580,554 application bytes. This reduces the burden on each holder while requiring sufficient holders and preserving full verification data. [Measured custody alternative](assurance/CUSTODY.md)

**Next experiment.** Run several permissionless archivists against staggered independent PoW histories and a long enough offered load to measure duplicate work, unique coverage, recovery after real downtime, dissemination and retention of the last proof source. Compare dynamic selection of originals versus filling four to eight useful slots, including fork losses, and measure verifier/bootstrap throughput and disk. The immediate adoption boundary is optional local archival validation. A general-network decision needs a credible availability/incentive mechanism, sustainable builder capacity, and cryptographic assurance. Numeric coverage, not a trusted archivist identity, determines whether the archive scenario survives.
