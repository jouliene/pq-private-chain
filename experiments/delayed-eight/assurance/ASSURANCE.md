# DLF1 and D8C1 authorization and archival assurance

**The eight-slot certificate proves a registered relation over public DLF1 authorizations.** A fresh archive node must also verify the selected work, recover each authorized effect and both receipts, and apply regular chain rules at the original block height. The aggregate can replace original authorization evidence only on the D8 chain whose PoW commits the ordered effect identities. The previously released DLF1 chain requires the original bytes to reconstruct its PoW preimage. The [custody alternative](CUSTODY.md) preserves that older evidence contract.

## Exactly what is verified

* **Inner DLF1.** SHA-256 of the 1,481-byte verifier is `5d6cfc6ccff5792e4899799124b2fb1e7dac50f727825c4886485dc01a564ce8`. The source builds the spend circuit itself, binds both real/dummy input branches, key derivation, nullifiers, membership for real notes, new commitments and integer value conservation. Public words are four anchor words, one height, eight nullifier words, eight commitment words, one fee and four receipt-digest words. The receipts' digest is a free circuit input. The native checker verifies the proof, enforces fee three, reads both fixed 1,792-byte ciphertexts in order, checks their suite/canonical encodings and recomputes their digest. The prover uses row blinding and salted openings of witness-dependent oracles. The earlier wire audit found 3,052 allocated blinding rows in the 4,096-row trace and 78 distinct salt tuples in one inspected real proof. Fresh randomness and hiding of the entire adaptive inner transcript remain cryptographic prerequisites. * **Outer D8C1.** SHA-256 of the 1,713-byte verifier is `9d280dfe7b0b35c2fb768e927239da4a8bdbc9c2ccdde27127a17fe9ae96bdcd`. The registered outer circuit has 38,272 gates before finalization, 65,536 padded rows and 210 public inputs. Qnero's recursive helper places the exact inner verifier-only data in circuit constants, allocates proof targets from the registered common data and calls the recursive verifier eight times. Every slot verifies, even when fewer than eight carry useful statements. The first activity bit is one, every activity bit is Boolean, each active successor requires an active predecessor, and the public count is their sum. An inactive slot must match every public word of its predecessor. The honest builder repeats the last real **proof**. The circuit requires equal **statements** for padding and would also accept another valid proof of that statement. The authenticated sequence is `tag=0x443843310001, count, PI[0], …, PI[7]`. The public decoder demands exact proof round trip, checks count and pad suffix, verifies that outer proof, then rechecks the active real effects, receipt bytes and no duplicate nullifiers or output commitments within the group. * **Authorization of the verifier.** A canonical serialization with the right dimensions alone would allow the wrong program. The public role's caller checks both actual files by SHA-256 against the repository's registered keys and uses the explicitly approved native installation. The builder additionally reconstructs the outer circuit and compares it with the registry before proving. The network id's design names D8 and the registered DLF1 profile. The mapping of that D8 release to this exact outer key is therefore part of the local operator's trusted consensus software. Installing a different recursive verifier can change what a fresh compact archive accepts. An update must carry explicit version/activation and compatibility rules. * **Placement and spentness.** The statement identity is a domain-separated SHA3-256 over the D8 network id and the complete 3,756-byte effect: anchor, u32le height, both nullifiers, both new commitments, fee and both ciphertexts. This commits to the deterministically recoverable receipt digest through its checked bytes. Both the reward context and PoW template bind the ordered statement ids for that block. The certificate binds its own ordered public statements. It has no private proof of their particular historic height, transaction index, active branch or aggregate grouping. The node resolves each position in each selected core to its exact verified effect and applies anchor membership/window, uniqueness, current nullifiers, commitment tree, exact reward/fees and work/fork-selection rules there. A certificate may retain valid statements for a losing branch. Those statements consume bytes and confer no independent selected inclusion. A replacement that verified under one parent can still fail historic/current state checks on another. ## What removal preserves and what it gives up

A fresh cold process must obtain every needed selected core plus authentic direct or certificate evidence for each selected slot. The compact packet decoder compares the *entire* recovered 3,756-byte effect against a slot returned by its own checked proof source. A same-process cache is keyed by SHA-256 of proof bytes after actual verification. A persisted link, output list, prior successful-process flag, or an unchecked compact attachment is insufficient at cold start. Compaction validates replacement statements, changes links and deletes unreferenced evidence under one database write transaction. A stateful node may accelerate replay, but independent verification after deletion still needs the original branch headers, content, complete receipts, circuit constants and native rules. Omitting all such evidence for a selected slot stalls verification. [Archive implementation](../archive.py)

This establishes that an accepting authorization for the statement was proved, under the inner and outer knowledge assumptions. It does not reproduce the exact original DLF1 bytes, its arrival time or proof-message variant. An archived receipt does not prove a recipient could decrypt it: the leaf's public digest check binds what the payer published. The brand-new selected D8 identity names the payment effects; exact original-byte forensic retention is an additional storage role. Pruning must retain a verified source for detached cores needed for possible reorg, including orphan-only authorizations which share no certified selected statement. A full certificate remains necessary if it alone covers even one surviving useful slot.

## Concrete security and privacy boundary

Both layers use Goldilocks `p=2^64−2^32+1`, extension degree two, two permutation challenges, FRI inverse rate 32, cap height 4, 26 query rounds, no grinding and two-bit reductions. The outer effective reduction sequence has seven folds by four, the inner five. The fork constructor computes a **conjectured** query screen of 26×5=130 bits and caps it by the extension field. This is a configuration check. Actual proximity/list-decoding bounds, algebraic batching and opening errors at these degrees, Poseidon instantiation, adaptivity and Fiat–Shamir/QROM loss need separate accounting. The checkpoint adds another argument instead of increasing any of these security levels. In particular a composition reduction needs a way to **extract accepting inner proofs** from a malicious accepting outer prover, or another suitable recursive-composition theorem. Outer soundness for an existential statement alone gives no algorithm for extracting a computationally hard-to-find inner forgery. A union bound over eight slots is justified only after that reduction, its costs and the shared-oracle model have been established. [Pinned qp-plonky2 constructor](https://github.com/Quantus-Network/qp-plonky2/blob/632f23ca07712bfa5bc8ed082bc05c41c5929dfb/plonky2/src/plonk/circuit_builder.rs) · [FRI/Plonk-like Fiat–Shamir theory](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1071) · [Concrete noninteractive FRI analysis](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1161)

Both argument levels use original Poseidon over Goldilocks for proof hashing and challenges. Newer Poseidon2 note operations, SHA-256 PoW/CIDs and SHA3-256 D8 effects serve other jobs. Four base-field digest elements carry just under 256 bits. Ideal generic quantum collision-query cost for that output space is around 2^85.3 while generic targeted preimage search is around 2^128. This distinction does not translate either number into a known payment forgery or a concrete composition guarantee. Replacing just the archive checksum leaves both recursive proof hashes intact. An end-to-end PQ figure also needs the adversary's work/query budget, multi-target history length, dirty-key/parameter handling and a composable receipt/privacy analysis. [Pinned proof hash configuration](https://github.com/Quantus-Network/qp-plonky2/blob/632f23ca07712bfa5bc8ed082bc05c41c5929dfb/core/src/config.rs) · [Generic quantum collision result](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9705002) · [Multi-round quantum Fiat–Shamir](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05207)

The outer uses **no** witness-row blinding and **no** FRI leaf hiding. An honest builder receives already public canonical DLF1 proofs, active count, order and receipts. Its proof assignment is a function of these plus fresh or implementation randomness independent of wallet secrets. An observer already holding that view can generate the same output distribution. Under these premises outer postprocessing does not introduce new information about a private wallet opening beyond the original public view. This argument requires the inner proof and receipt privacy property for the entire observed family, including retries, and an explicit observable grouping policy. It does not make the outer zero knowledge relative only to its 210 public words, erase the originals from historical observers or protect selective grouping, retry timing and network-source correlations. Padding repeats a publicly known statement. Visible count, fee, nullifiers, anchor, order, outputs, ciphertexts and block time remain part of the view. An offline recipient's seed recovery still depends on scanning and authenticating the real ciphertext stream. The construction gives neither recipient anonymity against arbitrary ancillary data nor post-compromise secrecy. At 100 million inclusions per year, a perfectly filled aggregate must complete every 2.52288 seconds on average for a single serial service to keep up. If one complete certificate costs C CPU-seconds or W wall-seconds, the ideal full-slot population needs at least 0.3963724×C continuous CPU cores and ceil(0.3963724×W) serial worker equivalents before outages, reorg waste, setup, retries or peaks. Sparse/busy full-slot 5 TB comparisons leave 368,070 / 370,485 bytes for the actual outer proof with these core and receipt encodings. The 1 TB counterparts are 48,070 / 50,485 bytes. A nonempty count and eight checked statements do not establish eight useful selected payments. The cost ledger must count useful selected slots, including incomplete groups and post-reorg stranding. These are scenario thresholds, independent of construction security and permissionless availability. [Exact thresholds and occupancy definitions](../review/thresholds.py) · [Measured alternative when independent owners store redundant fragments](CUSTODY.md)

The measured two- and eight-active certificates both used a 179,806-byte proof. Eight active slots produced a 208,487-byte certificate including receipts and framing. At eight useful slots this proof is 188,264 / 190,679 bytes below the sparse/busy 5 TB proof allowances and exceeds both 1 TB allowances. The eight-active builder took 111.026 CPU-seconds for proving plus 0.057 for source checking and assignment, after setup. The request took 56.681 wall-seconds. At sustained full occupancy this implies approximately 44.03 continuously busy CPU cores and 23 parallel warm worker equivalents at the observed settings before overhead. The observed builder process reached 6,837,596 KiB peak RSS at the first proof. The node verified the complete eight-active certificate in 11.49 ms of native CPU. Padding made the two-active proof almost as expensive to produce (110.379 CPU-seconds); if two useful slots persisted, the builder load would grow by about four. These isolated timings do not demonstrate cooperative supply, sustained throughput, an admission deadline or archival availability.
