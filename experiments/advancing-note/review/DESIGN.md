# Independent N7P2 design review

The public verifier reconstructs 49 field words from the payment or signed leg. One registered gate connects these values to the actual funded relation. Reusing the verifier is justified for the isolated native experiment. The present proof and receipt arguments leave quantum spending knowledge and adaptive joint privacy open.

## Registered equality and transcript

I read the registered 1,764-byte key independently of qp-plonky2. The SHA3-384 of its bytes is `6512fef3faf2e077592284053014091d952dc1194849722d2d42272d8638d44470fbe37a93eda9f0c6f9b1e4a7b202bc`. Its seven gate types include the custom statement gate and the stock public-input gate. The stock public-input vector has length zero. The node obtains the registration from its executable. Each request gets a copy of its common data. The statement replaces the 49 evaluator operands in the copy, preserving the committed constants and copy-permutation cap. Both signer roles, ordinary wallets and the public service use this same registration. [Independent key reader](registration.py) · [Native binding and serialization](../proof/src/public.rs)

One physical row has 49 constrained, copy-routed cells among 60 routable wires. The assignments in order are seven root words, count, fee, twelve 32-bit little-endian context limbs, fourteen nullifier words and fourteen output-commitment words. For each position the gate enforces wire minus verifier operand. The authenticated circuit copies connect the first root to both real memberships as applicable, fee to conservation, and each digest to its ordered effect. The context limbs are public inputs whose meaning comes from the ledger's SHA3-384 of the approved genesis and complete framed header including receipts. The proof alone can establish equality to a supplied context but deriving the context demands this public parser. [Relation](../proof/src/relation.rs) · [Public packet parser](../lab/common.py)

The registered gate is type 2 in the first selector group of six. Its filter contains the five other gate indices and the disabled-selector value. The constraint before filtering has degree one. After filtering, each of the 49 terms has symbolic degree seven. At 16,384 rows each term has numerator degree at most 114,681. For a valid witness, division of this term by the trace vanishing polynomial leaves degree at most 98,297. Both fit the registered eightfold quotient budget. The encompassing PLONK evaluator combines the 49 positions within its 118 gate-constraint slots and randomizes terms using two alphas. These are degree and wiring checks. A same-witness extraction bound also requires arguments about commitment binding, permutation and low-degree checks, and the interactive-to-noninteractive transformation. [Pinned selector and gate code](https://docs.rs/crate/qp-plonky2/1.5.5/source/src/gates/gate.rs) · [Registered geometry](registration.py)

The transcript prehash inputs are the distinct N7P2 label, the fixed 48-byte circuit digest, a four-byte little-endian count of 49 and all 49 canonical eight-byte little-endian words. The resulting 48-byte SHA3-384 string enters the challenger as six seven-byte limbs and one six-byte limb. Every byte reaches the challenger. The prehash has its usual computational binding assumption. Native proving, compression, expansion, canonical recompression and verification all use that request's derived challenge context. The static registration preserves its original digest between requests. This design bypasses the upstream four-word Poseidon route for the transaction statement. The implementation's recursive evaluator specializes its public operands as constants. A reusable recursive verification protocol would need its own statement interface and review. ## Value, arity and visibility

The first private input must be positive. The second slot selects padding precisely when the sum of its two nonnegative 62-bit amounts is zero. The bound prevents wraparound and forces both amounts to zero. Positive token amounts carry a nonzero asset, and positive token lanes must agree on that asset across all notes. A real slot derives its owner and indexed nullifier with the same secret, checks its ordered depth-40 path to the public root and places its index below the public count. Two real slots must have distinct indices. Each lane's largest possible output sum plus fee is 2⁶³ + 1, below the Goldilocks modulus. The node enforces the public fee schedule and checks both legs of a swap against one current spent set before publishing their combined effects. [Monetary circuit](../proof/src/relation.rs) · [Atomic admission](../lab/state.py)

In a one-input payment the second public nullifier is a separately tagged hash of the spend secret and the first real nullifier. The wallet publishes two canonical nullifiers and two independently sealed, equal-length receipts in both arities. It seals a zero-value change note to the payer when required. There is no wire flag for the padding slot. Replacing a one-input proposal using the same occurrence repeats **both** nullifiers. An observer of abandoned or competing proposals can link the pair, even when the packet bytes or recipient change. Encrypted change, randomized ordinary output order and fixed proof shape do not establish that complete transcript distributions match. [Wallet](../lab/wallet.py) · [Receipt construction](../lab/crypto.mjs)

The funding fixture starts with eight transparent grants to five owners. Three owners have two grants. Counts, roots, fees, swap roles, timing and later conflicts narrow the information available to hide a sender. The published demonstration also discloses its test amounts and arities. At 100M operations/year, a 90/10 ordinary/swap mix produces 2.2 billion legs and 4.4 billion nullifier slots over twenty years. Padding occupies some of those slots. Up to 64 recent root/count pairs per branch are eligible at a time. A privacy claim must identify the compared public traces, information available to a malicious payer, correlated inputs and adaptive proof queries. A single backup compromise also exposes historical receipt decryption. [Fixture](../lab/run.py) · [Standardized KEM scope](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final)

For the registered six four-way folds, 32 queries and quartic openings, the upstream blinder allocates 2,852 independent ordinary rows and 2,856 pairs of copy-linked rows. The public gate precedes those rows. The fixed public operands add no new opened wire columns. A simulation must still cover the secret-dependent copy products, quotients, openings, all FRI oracles and final polynomial jointly with commitments, real/padding nullifiers and correlated ML-KEM/DEM receipts. An ideal permutation sponge result and a general QROM Fiat–Shamir theorem do not instantiate this concrete composition by themselves. ## Branch and resource interpretation

Anchor identity consists of the ordered-tree root and leaf count. Eligibility checks the most recent 64 selected states, including the parent. A branch reproducing the same root/count can preserve eligibility. A distinct detached root fails at the same count. Current spentness and commitment uniqueness are checked at admission even for older eligible anchors. Disconnect removes the durable block, restores counts, nullifiers, commitments, frontier and fee credit, and rolls back the eligibility window. The node keeps eight in-memory undo entries. A deeper test disconnect reconstructs the prefix by verification from genesis before unlinking the durable tip. The test control supplies the reorganization order. Distributed fork selection and miner competition remain outside this experiment. [Public ledger](../lab/state.py)

The warm trial processes nine distinct ordinary blocks and one two-leg swap with two approvals. It measures completed durable admission on two physical cores. Process startup or a fresh-node full replay has a different cost. Three repetitions on a short history give a range on the measured host. At 100M included operations/year the mean arrival rate is 3.169 operations/s and the 90/10 mix requires 3.486 proofs/s. The two-core budget is 0.631 core-seconds per mixed operation, including signature verification, public index updates and persistence. Each peer in a four-peer full-block fanout gets at most 197,235 bytes per mixed operation on the illustrated 20 Mbit/s uplink, before transport overhead or additional packet relay.

The node retains at most two occupied tree positions per level and keeps its uniqueness and fee index on disk. Its SQLite page-cache target is 2 MiB, its SQLite heap limit is 32 MiB, and memory mapping is disabled. These limits cover SQLite allocations as specified, while whole-process RSS also includes the verifier, Python and the arithmetic worker. At 100M operations/year two 56-byte nullifiers and two 56-byte commitments per leg add 24.64 GB of raw index keys. The measured 32,768-to-65,536-leg database growth was 274.125 bytes per leg, giving a linear projection of 30.15 GB/year at this mix. That calibration measures random-key B-tree occupancy at a small scale. Full-size database lookup latency and sustained storage I/O remain unmeasured. [Index calibration](../INDEX.json) · [Public state and replay](../lab/state.py)

Each service start deletes and rebuilds its derived SQLite index using the external founding identity and the selected blocks. Within a single-writer service, accepted input is verified before its index changes are staged. A durable block is written and fsynced, hard-linked at its height and the directory fsynced. Only then does the index transaction commit. Storage errors outside normal validation terminate the role and require a replay to resolve which operation reached disk. The index deliberately uses synchronous OFF. A saved or damaged cache has no role in recovery. This semantics relies on the selected block store, its availability, the local filesystem's durability contract, and its writer lock. Each block occupies a separate file. [Service and store](../lab/state.py)

The wallet reconstructs full paths, maintains its own in-memory index and trial-decrypts receipts. A 3,713-byte projection per leg yields 408.43 GB/year across 110M legs, assuming authenticated and complete scan records. This implementation replays complete payments. Twenty-year recovery requires the corresponding block archive and 4.4 billion receipt trials per retained key before accounting for compromise, selective disclosure or historical key rotation. Small-fixture restore time does not measure that service. [Wallet scan](../lab/wallet.py) · [Mixed workload model](../evaluate.py)
