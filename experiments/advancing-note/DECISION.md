# First-time payments and a reusable home validator

**Adopt N7P2 for further isolated devnet wallet and validation work. Keep MNG1/SLG1 as the funded product.** N7P2 can spend a first-time recipient's sole note and validate unrelated payments on an advancing branch with one registered key. The measured two-core machine clears the live CPU comparison. Full-body relay to four peers, unpruned archive growth and recovery from genesis still exceed the chosen home-node profile over a long operating lifetime. The experiment establishes neither quantitative post-quantum knowledge security nor anonymity of the complete payment. [Reproduction](README.md) · [Independent source review](review/DESIGN.md)

## What the new relation buys

N7P1 required the recipient to find a second positive note, accepted only the tip root, and rebuilt its verifier for each public statement. N7P2 retains the seven-field-word note family on a new genesis and places either one or two real same-owner inputs inside a single fixed relation. Slot zero must be positive. The private zero/positive predicate selects the second slot's behavior. Separate 62-bit native/asset bounds prevent zero by modular cancellation. An active second input must open the same spending authority, prove a path to the same funded root at a different index and conserve its private asset. An inactive second slot contributes zero and emits a domain-separated padding nullifier derived from the spending secret and first real nullifier. The ledger still checks *both* published identifiers for uniqueness. Both packet types carry two nullifiers, two commitments and two ML-KEM-1024/AES-256-GCM receipts, regardless of private input count. A public collision involving padding could deny a later spend. Retry/conflict comparisons, a known funding history, explicit test disclosures and timing can narrow an observer's inference. This is fixed public arity with conditional hiding. [Spend relation](proof/src/relation.rs) · [Wallet](lab/wallet.py)

A single deterministic 1,764-byte verification registration contains the fixed constants/copy-permutation commitment, circuit shape, selector configuration and native gate identity. The registered code defines the interpretation of the gates. Each statement supplies operands, and the gate equations, copy routing, cap and verifier program stay fixed. One row has 49 routable cells for root, count, fee, receipt/context limbs, nullifiers and output commitments. The gate enforces 49 scalar equalities. The node independently derives the statement and validates the branch. The proof transcript absorbs tagged SHA3-384 of the fixed circuit digest and all 49 canonical words before the first witness-dependent challenge. The complete 48-byte digest enters through seven injective field limbs. The same process verifies both input-count cases, both sides of a swap and ordinary transfers. A block producer supplies neither a key nor executable code. [Registered key and build check](proof/README.md) · [Gate and transcript](proof/src/public.rs)

The custom gate has unfiltered degree one and filtered degree seven. At 16,384 rows the term's numerator degree is at most 114,681 and its valid quotient at most 98,297. The registered quotient factor is eight. Its 49 wires fall within the 60 copy-routed wires. Row blinding contributes 2,852 single rows and 2 × 2,856 rows for paired queries before final padding. The 32 FRI queries, leaf salts, complete opening vector and terminal polynomial have the same registered blinding obligations for both input counts. A dedicated test checks each public coordinate's base and quartic gate residual independently of Fiat–Shamir. Separate tests change each public field of a valid proof and attempt fresh proving for incorrect root, selected nullifier, output and private monetary witnesses. [Independent binary key reader](review/registration.py) · [Executable controls](proof/check.py)

**Security boundary.** The target remains 128 bits of quantum spend knowledge against adaptive, multi-user adversaries, including chosen notes, replay, correlated receipts and eligible roots over time. No matching bound for this concrete PLONK/FRI transcript, statement-dependent operands, SHAKE challenger and actual blinding is established. For example, independent queries with hypothetical 1/4 agreement alone give (1/4)^32 = 2^-64, before list-size, transcript and extraction losses. A seven-word output's ideal quantum generic collision scale of about 2^149.3 is not a lower bound for the funded relation. In the sizing mix 20 years means 4.4 billion output slots and up to two billion blocks; only 64 recent root/count records are eligible at a time. Translating bounds for a single target or transcript to that lifetime requires an explicit adversarial game. The proof and receipt must also hide a *joint* choice, including ML-KEM recipient-key anonymity/robustness, later spends, exposure of a long-lived seed and known counterparties. FIPS 203 specifies the KEM, while the native SHAKE-based DEM derivation, GCM, approval keys, retry behavior and actual monetary statement require their own composition analysis. ## Which branch can accept a payment

The offline recipient starts with zero balance, no founding grant, and an exported receiving descriptor. Alice spends two real inputs, (5 native, 11 A) and (7 native, 23 A), to pay (7 native, 13 A) and a fee of 3. A fresh recipient process mounts one 48-byte backup, public history and an empty private directory. It recovers exactly that one note and proves an onward transfer of (2 native, 3 A), keeping (2 native, 10 A) after its fee. It has no helper input. During actual native proving a previously prepared, unrelated funded payment advances the tree. The overlapping start/end intervals are recorded using the shared Linux monotonic clock. Both proofs are checked independently on inclusion.

The node accepts an authorized recent root/count, checks all NFs and input-independent output uniqueness against the *current* selected branch, then durably writes the complete block. A different, genuinely proved spend of the same restored note fails while it is spent. It succeeds after rollback restores the common prefix, then is itself rolled back. Reordering the two eligible payments leaves the same spent set but a different output root at the same count. A third genuine proof was accepted on the original root and rejected on the reordered branch and after restart. These controls use explicit test disconnects and alternate valid PoW blocks. They exercise state eligibility and reversal, while the live PoW implementation remains the reference for autonomous fork choice. [Public ledger](lab/state.py) · [Funding, alternatives and controls](lab/run.py)

With one operation per block at 100M operations/year, the first eight-root pilot allowed about 2.52 s at the mean arrival rate. The recipient's native prove interval alone exceeded that time. We increased retention to 64 root/count pairs, costing 3,584 additional raw bytes per node, with eight independent undo deltas and verified prefix replay beyond that window. The mean 64-block interval is 20.20 s. Actual block arrivals and wallet delays vary. On the selected chain, Carol anchors her signed exchange leg at genesis and Dana anchors hers after nine funded transfers. Both verify their incoming outputs and change before approving the same pair. Carol gives 7 A for 30 B; Dana pays the one native fee. A separate branch accepts a funded ordinary payment at anchor age 63, then rejects it at age 64 with its inputs still unspent, including after restart. The latter boundary test uses one distinct funded update and subsequent empty PoW blocks. [Pilot record](PILOT.json) · [Anchor and persistence rules](lab/STORAGE.md)

## Measured costs

The author run has nine ordinary payments and one jointly signed swap on the final branch. It is replayed three times with independent fresh public processes, actual deterministic PoW checks, all eleven proofs and both swap signatures each time. The validators are restricted to two different physical cores of an i7-8700K. Wallets use four Rayon threads. The fixture/JSON serialization and cryptographic roles are isolated from private seeds. Public economic totals after height 10 are 82 native units including 30 miner fees, 67 A and 35 B. The node records 30 output occurrences including eight founding grants, and 22 nullifiers including padding. The latter number alone does not identify how many real notes were spent. Every block file and containing directory is fsynced. The SQLite index is derived, is bounded to a 2 MiB cache target and 32 MiB heap limit on the disk-backed node, and is rebuilt after restart. Exact spent-set hashing for diagnostic comparisons is outside the timed path. CPU includes the parent, the arithmetic and proof services, and reaped signature workers. At the wider request boundary, including JSON/hex transport within the harness, the full public process trees used 0.25 / 0.27 / 0.27 core-seconds. Subsequent storage, peer traffic and long-running contention remain open. A forced process exit just after the block fsync and before index commit recovered the correct fee and both NFs. ## Home-validator decision and next change

For the comparison assume two dedicated CPU cores, 16 GiB RAM, a 2 TB disk, 100 Mbps down / 20 Mbps up, 365.25 days/year, one operation per block and 90% ordinary / 10% swaps. Treat 100M offered operations as included for the sizing case and charge replicas independently. The resulting mean is 3.169 operations/s. The observed median full-admission CPU scales to **0.081 dedicated cores**; counting two complete admission passes gives 0.162 cores, before gossip, unrelated invalid submissions, synchronization, slow disks and consensus scheduling. Two cores clear the observed short-run validation gate. At ~0.026 core-s per operation the CPU cost is materially smaller than the previous 4.925 core-s per proof including specialization. These are distinct full-node and proof-only boundaries; the absolute costs are reported separately. [Measured and projected costs](COSTS.json) · [Baseline](../new-note/DECISION.md)

At 100M the first block copy needs 6.70 Mbps on average. Even sending each body only once to each of four peers needs 26.81 Mbps. A 2 TB drive holds about **27.6 days** of this stream before indices, forks or filesystem overhead. The source currently stores one file per block. It needs available full history to restart an empty validator and to reconstruct an offline wallet. The 20-year unique archive projection is about 529 TB. Raw NF/commitment keys add 24.64 GB/year. A **synthetic index-only** probe at 32,768 and 65,536 legs measured 274.125 incremental database bytes/leg, projecting about 30.15 GB/year under the same layout. It measured logical page/file occupancy under a fixed public key distribution. Long-run SSD performance, miner identities, fragmentation and pruning are separate. [SQLite probe and samples](INDEX.json) · [Calculator](evaluate.py)

**Next product experiment:** give a pruned node a crash-consistent, branch-bound persistent index and a rolling 14-day body store, and relay each accepted body once to two data-bearing peers with short inventory announcements to the others. At this load the rolling bodies cost about **1.014 TB**, the modeled 20-year index about **0.603 TB**, and two complete payload copies **13.41 Mbps** before protocol overhead. This would leave roughly 0.38 TB on the comparison disk before snapshot, forks, reorg tails and filesystem costs. Local storage savings require independent archive sources capable of serving the complete 26.44 TB/year; the dependency must be tested by bringing the original source offline. A proposed scan-only stream of 3,713 B/leg still costs ~408 GB/year before headers or authentication and needs a completeness rule. A decisive test would crash/restart the pruned node across its boundary, withhold then restore archive evidence, compare balances/fees/spent state against full replay and measure actual relay fanout under missing-body requests. This architecture has been priced only. The research input is a sizing scenario rather than owner acceptance of these annual costs. [Index measurement method](index_cost.py)

Existing MNG1/SLG1 and N7P1 funds retain their original spend rules. N7P2 has its own genesis, descriptors, note domains and registered verifier. No migration, asset issuance rule or consensus deployment is implied. The global permissionless private PoW project remains active.
