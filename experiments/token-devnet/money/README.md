The monetary identity is additive **per selected branch**. The founding row creates `(0 native,240 token)`. At each non-genesis block every ordinary transfer removes three units of native value from private note balances and conserves all tokens. The reward creates `(100+fees,0)`. The resulting net change is exactly `(100,0)`. Fees neither increase the initial token cap nor the native subsidy. `∑reward_amounts − ∑fee debits = 100 × height`. The 240-token cap rests on authenticated founding, verified openings/ownership/membership, two exact integer equations, global nullifier consumption and the exclusion of any other creation or mint path. A 112-byte **typed** reward opening plus CM includes the zero token lane; the serialized legacy-layout public reward record is 104 bytes since that lane is determined by consensus. The reconciler read 35 distinct complete block files. It independently reconstructed each raw 22-bit work ID, checked parent/height and a deterministic heaviest-tip/tie order, parsed complete TKN1 frames, extracted declared per-payment public fees and serials, and verified fresh commitments and exact reward amounts. It computed native fees, newly issued native, gross miner credits, token cap, spent-set digest and funded-set counts for each branch. Starting at the loser, it removed four blocks in reverse order and appended eleven from the common ancestor through H31. The final counts, fees, tips and commitment/serial-set digests matched a fresh reconstruction from the approved genesis. The ancestor need not be publicly identified by the wallet: the observer can find it in the public block graph. This finite run detached four blocks; the separate heaviest-branch and rollback logic for deeper forks has its own evidence. At H31, miner-a’s gross rewards were 1,412, miner-b’s were 703 and winning-miner’s were 1,000. Their wallet balances differ when they individually send/pay fees. The losing miner held `(403,0)` at its tip and `(0,0)` on the winning branch. Its four distinct reward commitments are absent from both H30 and H31. The invoice appeared once on the loser, disappeared at reconnection, and its re-anchored payment credited the shop `(3,7)` once on the final branch. A recreated eligible reward or later payment is evaluated by its own branch data, never by adding the old branch's gross totals. [Per-key and per-branch readbacks](AUTHOR.json)

The runner additionally compares the public scenario’s **voluntary** actor and amount disclosures with the public inclusion graph. For each accepted payment it checks the declared consumed totals against payee amount, change and fee in both lanes, then reconstructs the named participants’ balances from founding and public miner credits. The final `(native,token)` pairs were miner-a `(1409,170)`, miner-b `(677,0)`, winning-miner `(1000,0)`, fork-miner `(0,0)`, restored Mira `(5,18)`, restored next recipient `(2,32)`, payee `(4,13)` and shop `(3,7)`. An ordinary public node does not learn hidden values, key ownership or payer/payee labels from these frames. This calculator reads no wallet storage or note secrets, and does not verify PLONK proofs, typed reward commitments or Merkle roots cryptographically by itself. Its sibling public oracle verifies those against the approved binary and independent note primitives.
