# Retain MNG1 transfers and SLG1 swap legs on the local PoW devnet

**The hiding WHIR-style argument can verify the funded note relation. Keep it as an executable comparison. Retain the existing registrations for the devnet.** The measured comparison includes genuine wallet-created proofs, all original public fields and ordered ciphertexts, both owners' new joint signatures, and an onward spend from a restored spending seed. The research schedule increases complete traffic. The tested argument also lacks a concrete reduction to the project's 128-bit quantum-work target. No owner of previously funded notes needs to convert them. Original mint and burn operations continue to use MNG1.

## What was proved

The registered programs implement 136,975 rank-one rows for an ordinary transfer and 140,619 for one swap leg. They reconstruct the funded Poseidon2 openings, spend authority, both selected memberships up to 20 levels in the four-child tree, the input flags and nullifiers, and both output commitments. The two 62-bit monetary lanes conserve value as integers; the native lane includes the fee. A nonempty token lane uses one private asset ID with bounded limbs. A pure transfer has zero public asset-operation words and zero supply change. An SLG1 leg binds its eight ephemeral authorization-key words inside both output commitments. The candidate reuses the existing note and recipient cryptography. [Registered matrices and direct assignment controls](relation/README.md)

The verifier owns the matrix identity. It absorbs the entire canonical public statement before deriving challenges and recomputes the four-field Poseidon2 digest of both ordered ciphertexts. It does not prove that a malicious sender encrypted a useful note for a particular person. Before either swap signature leaves its owner, that wallet verifies both packets and opens the incoming payment and change under its own spending key. The signed pair checks network, expiry, paying side, both complete leg hashes, both signatures and the uniqueness of the four nullifiers and commitments. A selected-branch validator separately checks live funding, spentness and monetary state. [Versioned packet and joint check](packet.py) · [Per-owner proving and authorization](wallet/owner.py)

The reference and candidate reuse **identical** funded inputs and ciphertexts within each comparison. Each owner signs two different joint messages, one covering the released legs and one covering the candidate legs. Crossed signatures fail. The signed released encoding is mined on the disposable network. A fresh process with the recipient's backup seed and public branch replays the prefix and its newly mined extension, spends the 8 received native units to send 5 and pay a fee of 3, and generates both encodings of that spend. The released transaction passes ordinary ledger admission. The candidate has a separate public verifier; its block type has no registration on the live devnet. This design lets the comparison establish spendability of the exact same commitments without changing how a running node identifies an old block. [Fresh-process reproduction](README.md)

The registered proofs for the old and new profiles had the same 31 ordinary fields, the same 34 fields on each leg, the same 31 onward fields and all eight identical ordered ciphertexts. The two-real-input ordinary case spent native currency. The managed token lane carried positive value on the exchange. ## Independent review corrections

The first engineering run let one local test coordinator hold all private assignments. It established arithmetic compatibility only. The split implementation confines each owner to its own mount and PID namespaces, with one seed and read-only public branch data. Both signers explicitly check the branch, both proofs and the expected receipts. After they exit, an empty fourth worker rebuilds its inventory and membership paths from the backup and public blocks.

The first independent clean run stopped before signing. An older staged wallet accepted a managed ID in a helper call that expected a native-only receipt. The released checkout requires the all-zero asset ID whenever the expected token quantity is zero. We corrected the caller, pinned the current checkout's terms and authorization policy, and used the ordinary wallet's authenticated memo role to identify shuffled transfer outputs. Swap roles remain fixed at positions zero and one. The failed clean run and the preliminary run against the old staged policy receive no acceptance credit. [Corrective revision](https://github.com/jouliene/pq-private-chain/commit/ad0998099afae89ec544640f6fda6af5fd14e1d6)

## Security and admission

The fixed research schedule gives a conditional interactive error near 2^-130.90 under the stated list and agreement model. In the ideal compiler sensitivity calculation, the quadratic term alone approaches one at roughly 2^65.45 quantum queries. That number describes the loss of a sufficient bound. It supplies neither an attack nor an end-to-end proof of knowledge for a concrete hash implementation. The stronger same-domain screen raises the conditional interactive exponent to about 267.26 using seven guards, larger query counts and revised agreement slack. Its expected proof alone is 486,871 bytes. The experiment does not treat these theoretical costs as measured packets. [Independent profile, transcript and theorem mapping](review/GATE.md) · [Interactive construction](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/391) · [Quantum random-oracle compiler](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/834)

The signed frame has **21,060 fixed bytes** in addition to its proof. A proof must therefore fit in 241,084 bytes. For the research geometry the complete transfer is at most 249,793 bytes, but the signed-frame combinatorial bound reaches 267,012. A successful draw therefore cannot guarantee that every honest swap will fit. For the stronger geometry the expected signed frame is 507,931 bytes. Under independent uniform query positions, the bounded-differences inequality gives a per-draw probability of at most 2^-132.761 of fitting the stronger signed frame. Each individual draw can add at most an opened row, its salt and one 64-byte sibling per tree level. Adding a genuinely new leaf never reduces total encoded bytes, so changing a draw has at most that same magnitude, counted for both terminal trees. This is a format/traffic calculation under ideal sampling. The release implements only the research schedule. [Recalculate complete frame budgets](frame_budget.py)

Unchanged obligations include fixed-target and chosen-collision security of the legacy funded hashes, knowledge extraction for the guarded outer reduction, joint hiding of all direct and authenticated messages, and lifting the exact salted commitment/Fiat–Shamir implementation to a concrete multi-user quantum statement. A validator pins the SHA-256 of the published matrix. Replacing that fixed matrix and finding an arbitrary pair of colliding matrices are different games. The joint swap digest also remains SHA-256, and the two signatures remain SLH-DSA-SHAKE-128f. Their concrete games and multi-target losses still matter even if a stronger IOP schedule is selected. The receipt uses reusable ML-KEM-1024 public keys and the released ChaCha20-Poly1305 note and memo ciphertexts. A recording reveals the paired legs, selected anchor/height, fee, payer, expiry, commitments, nullifiers, ciphertexts and two authorization keys. Re-proving the same spent note exposes its same nullifier. Static receiving keys, future compromise, sender behavior, counterparty knowledge and network timing need their own privacy model. Compatible proofs cannot strengthen the binding of old commitments or conceal a previously recorded receipt. ## Next experiment capable of changing this decision

Keep the typed registered relation and compare a small-interleave constrained-code switch using distance-amplified Reed–Solomon codes from CFW §13. Register an explicit graph and its distance/list bounds. Charge each degree-d opened symbol for all d underlying evaluations and at least d times the original query-privacy coverage. Charge both independently timed commitments and terminal openings. Use the complete quantum-target estimate, including field and compiler losses, and 241,084 bytes as the **worst-case proof allowance for one signed leg**. Finish the budget and map the new transcript to a hiding/knowledge argument before generating a new funded leg. The paper's illustrative Merkle savings apply to its parameter regime. They do not establish that a concrete target profile fits here. This is a bounded next experiment. Live consensus, note migration and issuance are outside it.
