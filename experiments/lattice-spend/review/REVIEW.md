## The admissible wire and the hidden leaf

At the height-16 monetary geometry the prior result leaves 33,676 B for **two** private occurrence proofs, their equality to the notes consumed in the monetary proof, and any new public fields. A collision-resistant tree authenticates bytes at a position. The monetary verifier must independently bind each real redeemed note to those same bytes and that branch. A common hidden-value commitment needs a canonical bit encoding, the exact opening on both sides, hiding randomness, and bounds that exclude distinct integers representing the same residue. Dummy slots must follow the registered policy without revealing which slots are real. During a dual-root transition a note has one stable spent identity across both systems. A seed-only wallet needs authenticated history, the committed tree version and the encrypted note openings. [Original budget](../../packed-tcith/resources/NETWORK.json)

I derived the size of the literal 2016 LLNW transcript from Figure 2. At the paper's illustrative dimension `n=q=256, k=8, m=4096`, each depth-40 path reveals 39 blocks of `m` field residues and 80 blocks of `2m` residues per challenge. These masks or shifted masks alone occupy 815,104 B. A constant-weight binary vector of length 4,096 can be ranked in 4,090 bits and a permutation in 43,251 bits. With the three commitments, two openings and direction bits, a round occupies 857,801 B for challenge 1 or 1,249,411 B for challenge 2 or 3. For two independent paths and 219 rounds, the range is 375,716,838–547,242,018 B before the inter-proof binding. The exact inequality $(2/3)^t ≤ 2^{-b}$ gives 219 rounds for a 128-bit interactive error and 438 for 256 bits. Parallel use still requires a suitable extractor under the intended FS and adversary model. Rank coding and record-level padding are explicit accounting choices. The paper's intro example uses an 80-bit interactive-error target in a group-signature comparison and gives no contemporary 128-bit quantum estimate for these lattice ranks. ## Joint privacy and funded compatibility

The public observer sees the anchor, branch position in time, nullifiers, new commitments, two ordered receipt ciphertexts, native fee and, for a swap leg, its authorization key and paired frame. It can choose later statements based on those observations and correlate retries, branch changes and eventual spends. A simulator for the proof alone omits the ciphertexts that encrypt openings of those very commitments. A funded privacy claim needs a joint experiment covering these correlated outputs, multiple receiving keys including maliciously chosen keys, permitted corruptions, seed recovery, and the leakage from future compromise. Before either swap authorization, each recipient opens its incoming receipt and checks the intended private asset, amount, fee and spending authority against the specific jointly authorized frames. Binding a digest of a ciphertext authenticates the bytes. Spendability additionally depends on its private decryption and opening checks. ## Exact field conversion and independent-verifier builds

Goldilocks elements require canonical integer representatives when used under the 38-bit proof modulus. The proposed bit-polynomial conversion includes range bits for each canonical field value and its complement to `p−1`. Modular products and affine sums are split into ordinary convolutions of bits, bounded quotients and signed carries. Each checked coefficient residual must have absolute value below the proof modulus, and each polynomial degree must stay below 256 before using the negacyclic quotient. These two bounds make a ring equality imply the intended integer equation. The final carry and the zero positions beyond the used support matter. I checked the native parameter planner's use of the count, rank, coefficient norm, length and norm of each quadratic operand. When all private vectors have the same length and binary bound, repeated unit-rank templates supply the planner with these same scalar inputs. Such a run prices the proposed layout; the complete constraint matrices, spend proof and its wire are separate evidence. Each rank-one quadratic product `P` needs a challenge entry, at least three entries in the left row (right-hand side, rank-one term and a digit), and three in the right row.
