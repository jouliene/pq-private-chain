# Two hidden LLNW accumulator occurrences

The literal hidden-membership proof in Libert, Ling, Nguyen and Wang (LLNW), Fig. 2, exceeds the split's 33,676-byte allowance in the response to **any one challenge for one occurrence**, at the paper's own illustrative dimensions. It carries 815,104 masking residues of one byte each. The paper's concrete example is a group-signature comparison at n=q=256 with an 80-bit soundness target. Its quoted 61.5 MB group signature includes other operations. We isolate its membership protocol and extend its tree depth to 40 to hold 2⁴⁰ outputs. [Author PDF, §§3.2–3.5](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/benoit.libert/LLNW-16.pdf) · [Calculation](screen.py) · [Results](SCREEN.json)

## Protocol and costs

The author PDF SHA-256 is `0dae4eaed79d7bfb95ac694008a40dae15fae54d293989456ec9d26ce3b497a3`. LLNW uses k=ceil(log₂q), m=2nk, public A∈Zq^(n×m). A binary parent is the canonical k-bit decomposition of the n residues of A·(left,right). We use the authors' power-of-two q, so the binary decomposition has a unique representative. Other q need canonical coefficient ranges when linking a k-bit representation to a congruence. Accumulator binding reduces a hash collision to a nonzero SIS∞ solution with coefficients in {−1,0,1}. Their asymptotic worst-case reduction is not a concrete 128-bit quantum estimate for these dimensions.

A branch of depth h hides h directions, h siblings and the hidden leaf. At every level the witness extends its two nk-bit node strings into balanced m-bit vectors v*,w*, then branches them into two 2m-bit vectors z,y. Their linear constraints are A*z₁+A*y₁=Gu at the root and A*zᵢ₊₁+A*yᵢ₊₁=G*vᵢ* at the other levels, with the extended matrices of Eqs. (4)–(5). There are h−1 independent m-word masks for v and 2h independent 2m-word masks for z,y. The prover commits to three lists: masked constraint evaluations and permutations, permuted masks, and permuted masked witnesses. In the authors' [41] commitment interface each commitment is n residues, and each of the two revealed opening coins is m bits.

| Fig. 2 branch | Response in addition to the two opening coins | Complete bytes per round at h=40 |
|---|---|---:|
| 1, opens C₂,C₃ | (5h−1)m permuted uniform residues, h hidden-direction XOR bits, 2h randomly permuted balanced vectors | 857,801 |
| 2, opens C₁,C₃ | (5h−1)m witness-plus-uniform residues, h permutation-control bits, 2h permutations of m positions | 1,249,411 |
| 3, opens C₁,C₂ | (5h−1)m uniform residues, h permutation-control bits, 2h permutations | 1,249,411 |

Each size includes three commitments, two coins, one fixed-width rank per permutation or balanced binary vector, and rounding to a whole byte. Parameters, authenticated root and statement are assumed available to the verifier. Fiat–Shamir derives the challenge trits; a literal interactive version additionally sends a uniform ternary challenge vector. For 438 rounds its optimal fixed-rank encoding takes 87 bytes per occurrence. The verifier tests the balanced extensions and the two opened commitments in branch 1, the public linear relation offset and two openings in branch 2, or the masked linear relations and two openings in branch 3. Three successful responses to one first message yield the extractor's witness or a commitment collision. The interactive statistical-zero-knowledge argument requires hiding commitments and fresh masks.

The three-challenge knowledge error per round is 2/3. Exact integer comparison gives 137, 219, 438 rounds for the 80-, 128- and 256-bit screens respectively. A false input on just one of two required occurrences receives that input's soundness bound; the two costs add. At 438 rounds the plain two-path proof uses **751,433,676–1,094,484,036 B**. Its expected size for uniform trits is 980,133,916 B. The 257-bit-per-path sensitivity uses 440 rounds if assigning a union-bound bit to two extraction failures. These are interactive screens. Classical rewindability, a 256-bit probability screen and hash-based noninteractivity do not alone give a 128-bit quantum knowledge and joint-receipt-privacy theorem.

Even allowing all commitments, opening coins, permutations, balanced vectors, directions and linking work for free, the literal two-path masking vectors use 714,031,104 B at the paper dimensions and 438 rounds. Over every n≥1 and q≥2, a deliberately optimistic floor uses m≥2, h=40 and ≥1 bit/residue: 2×438×199×2/8 = **43,581 B**. The n=1,q=2 limiting dimensions are insecure. An injective encoding of four existing canonical Goldilocks CM words needs at least 256 available bits, so m≥512 and the weaker 219-round screen still gives **5,578,368 B**. These are bounds for Fig. 2 written as specified, even under ideal bit packing. A different seed/tree protocol could avoid transmitting raw masks for some challenges. Simply seeding unshifted permutations/masks still leaves the shifted branch-2 response in this layout. Worst-case admissible transcripts can contain that branch. We make no general lower bound for other proof compilers or lattice accumulators.

## Keeping the same funded note hidden across both proofs

Standalone LLNW proves membership of *some* d below the branch root. Here is a concrete additional linear linkage, included to prevent counting this statement as a spend. A new ledger maps each occupied note commitment c to a domain-marked leaf `1 || canonical CM256 || zeros` of nk bits, with all-zero empty children. Append occurrences in consensus order. Nodes derive the authenticated root from the selected branch, output count and network/version, including on rollback and replay. All 40 levels remain available. At n=q=256 the root is 256 B. Each wallet path has 10,240 B of private siblings and 5 B of directions. The raw uniform setup matrix is 1 MiB shared by all parties. A dense path evaluation reads up to 41,943,040 coefficient positions; tree caching, sparse bits and transposed multiplication change the actual operation count. Public counters do not measure wallet time.

For each occurrence publish a fresh blinded tag L=Bc+Cr mod q. Use independent public matrices B,C, fresh binary r of length 4,096, and one 256-residue output. The two 256-byte tags add **512 B**. Conditioning on fixed public B,C and c, hiding requires the random linear hash of r to be statistically near uniform. At q=256 the binary difference of distinct r has a coefficient ±1, a unit, so a uniformly sampled independent C gives a two-universal family. The leftover-hash bound averaged over setup has 4,096 source bits against 2,048 output bits before multi-use and setup losses. Distinct bounded openings would give a nonzero ternary SIS relation on [B|C], with 4,352 columns; concrete hardness of that enlarged matrix is a separate obligation.

Add an LLNW linear equation involving the actual terminal zₕ. Summing its two m-wide halves recovers vₕ*, regardless of the hidden direction. Select the canonical CM bits from its first nk positions and constrain its occupied marker and unused bits. Extend the new binary r to a balanced length-8,192 string and include its mask and permutation in the same Stern commitments. The new tag relation and fixed leaf-bit equations enter C₁ with the existing masked zₕ; C₂/C₃ gain the matching permuted r-mask/witness terms. Extending the string commitment's input length adds verifier/prover work. Under its published fixed-output interface, the linked pair at 438 rounds costs **759,507,404–1,112,029,076 B**, including both L values, before the extra monetary proof work or framing. No link tag equals a public unblinded CM.

On the monetary side derive the *same* four canonical field words c from the authorized note's pk, rho, typed opening and value; decompose them into canonical little-endian bits. Prove all 4,096 r bits Boolean. Each of 256 row equations has an integer dot product at most 1,109,760; enforce dot=Lᵢ+256tᵢ with canonical public Lᵢ and 13-bit unsigned tᵢ. Both sides are bounded far below Goldilocks, preventing modular aliasing. This adds 256 linear equations and 3,328 carry-bit checks per slot before costing the commitment-bit decomposition, setup binding, or dummy selection. Publicly hashing the two proof byte strings cannot establish this hidden equality.

Bind the anchor/header, height, auxiliary SIS root, setup, network, profile, ordered link tags and *complete public effects* into both statement/challenge contexts. The old public nullifier must still be derived from the same nk, rho and typed opening as the real input, and both old and new modes consult one branch-local spent-nullifier set. The original asset, native and fee conservation checks, dummy zero lanes, distinct nullifiers, encrypted receipt binding, and both swap owner authorizations remain necessary. For a dummy slot, an indistinguishable fixed two-path layout can use the other real input as cover: the monetary relation enforces the slot's own CM equality when real, and the actual real slot's CM on a dummy slot. At least one input is real. This avoids publicly revealing a dummy or reserving a spendable tree position. A versioned transition would have to derive the new index from all consensus-accepted prior occurrences; remove both the new root and nullifier updates on an orphaned branch, and enforce identical live/legacy nullifiers. No historical anchor, packet, wallet or ledger was changed here.

**Decision.** In the specific 2¹⁶-party TCitH split, the 207,408 B monetary estimate leaves 33,676 B of the 241,084 B signed proof allowance for both hidden paths and all links. Extra linking rows would change that monetary estimate upwards. LLNW has a transparent matrix setup, logarithmic witnesses and an interactive lattice-based proof with statistical hiding, but this complete protocol increases wire and changes the ledger's funded root. The arithmetic exclusion precedes any funded prover or rollout. It does not exclude a different lattice construction from the project. [Predecessor split](../../packed-tcith/DECISION.md)
