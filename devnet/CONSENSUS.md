# PQDEV1 local consensus

PQDEV1 is a fixed-target SHA-256 research network. Its independently named PoW blocks carry up to four individually verified APV2 payments and one publicly identifiable mining reward. Nodes choose their own executable and keys. The sole payment profile is SHA-256 `af88a0ac0caa5f0aa27d38d687b6cc39495a0aa2659ed4a7e5e74653724cc1b1`. Sender-provided programs, aggregated APV2 packets and historical synthetic endowments have no admission path here.

## Wire and work

All primitive integers below are unsigned little-endian except the PoW comparison. The network ID `N` is SHA-256 of the ASCII bytes `PQDEV1/network`, one zero byte, the 32-byte profile digest and the packed tuple `<BBBIQ` (difficulty bits 22, anchor window 8, max payments 4, undo horizon 8, subsidy 100). N is `672a640b8b0416d093895f096d83bfb5b59c810bdedd6f3852429ad2e70b136d`. Genesis is the protocol constant SHA-256(`PQDEV1/genesis\0` || N), `9f60d1091c747fd598c286772e9bd544c1d2144b9b7b52a562addb50e7d169a4`. It has height zero, work zero, empty commitment and spent sets, and zero issuance. Its transport body is empty. No wallet is funded by configuration.

Append a single u64 nonce to obtain the sealed block. Each packet is at most 262,144 bytes. Total block length ranges from 222 to 1,048,814 bytes. Require exact alignment, lengths, no suffix, matching network/difficulty, canonical field words and a locally verified singleton per occupied position. Neither the claimed height nor the nonce influences difficulty.

Compute `seed = SHA256("PQDEV1/template\0" || template)` once, then `id = SHA256("PQDEV1/work\0" || seed || nonce:u64)`. Interpret the 32 ID bytes as an unsigned big-endian integer and require `id < 2^234`. Each valid block contributes exactly 4,194,304 work units. Choose greatest verified cumulative work; for equal work choose the lexicographically smaller tip ID. A node independently reconstructs the expected template from the actual validated parent, actual packets and miner key before storing it. Another miner can use any u64 nonce or its own registered-as-data payout key. No producer list or fixed leadership schedule participates in validation. The low fixed target, no timestamp, no retargeting, no fork-finality promise and no network-wide Sybil defense are deliberate local-network constraints.

## Payment state and issuance

For a prospective block at H, independently verify each complete APV2 singleton with local verifier material. Check every published AP anchor against the exact ancestor at its declared height h and require 1 ≤ H−h ≤ 8. Check ordered sidecar ciphertexts, the fixed three-unit fee, within-block and current-chain nullifier uniqueness, and output commitment uniqueness. Distinct packets may use distinct eligible anchors. Every input must authenticate against an earlier block root. A block cannot spend newly created outputs or its own mining reward. APV2 authorizes zero-value dummies under their separate circuit rules. The devnet wallet can use one actual note plus a dummy, so an H reward can be spent as early as H+1 with its H anchor.

Let V be the byte range from offset 213 through the end of the template, including the count and every length-prefixed packet. The reward context is `SHA256("PQDEV1/reward-context\0" || N || parent_pow_id || u32(H) || miner_pk || SHA256(V))`. The pinned APV2 native effect decoder requires a fee of exactly three units on every ordinary payment. The exact reward is 100 plus the sum of those verified fees, giving 100, 103, 106, 109 or 112 at occupancy zero through four. A change to the fee policy requires a separately versioned protocol. The resulting note value must be below 2^62. The consensus helper computes the Qnero note under miner_pk at that exact value. Its rho and r are `Digest::hash_bytes([domain, context32, pk32, amount_u64le])` under ASCII `PQDEV1/reward/rho` and `PQDEV1/reward/r`, respectively. The reward record is `context32 || pk32 || amount_u64le || cm32` (104 bytes). Node and wallet each recompute the commitment. Spending the note requires the recipient's matching private authority under the genuine spend circuit. Public coinbase openings reveal the amount and miner spending key. Payees choose their own keys; consensus cannot force an operator to possess the corresponding secret. Competing seals for an identical template give the same reward commitment on different branches; only the selected branch's instance can enter the state, and its AP anchor commits the exact winning PoW ID.

Append ordinary commitments in packet order, two outputs in their proof order per packet, then append the unique reward commitment last. Insert ordinary nullifiers once. Recycle fees into the same reward; only the 100-unit subsidy expands the supply. At height H gross new issuance is exactly 100H. The implementation rejects totals where cumulative fees plus issued value reach 2^63. All counters and positions use checked integer/range rules. No transparent second mint or separate fee payout exists.

Define D by the advancing-payment binary domain hash: SHA3-256 over ASCII `advancing-payments/1/` || domain || 0x00, followed by each part's u64 byte length and its bytes, then increasing u32 counters starting from zero until all four little-endian 64-bit digest limbs are canonical Goldilocks elements. Genesis AP header is `zero32 || u32(0) || D("dev-genesis-state",N) || D("dev-genesis-body",N) || zero32`. The spend-anchor identifier is the existing Qnero hash of its 132-byte AP header. For a new block, the cumulative state word is D over domain `dev-state` with the seven parts N, previous AP state word, u64(new commitment count), u64(total new issuance), u64(cumulative fees), the reward record, and concatenated ordered ordinary transitions. Each transition is two nullifiers, two commitments and the u64 fee. The new AP header is `parent_ap_anchor || u32(H) || state_word || D("dev-seal",N,pow_id) || tree_root`. The preexisting sorted quaternary native commitment tree authenticates member values. The state word binds the ordered effects, and the header binds the winning PoW block and its ancestry. Competing nonce solutions with an otherwise identical template have distinct authenticated wallet anchors.

## Persistence, fork choice and service

One SQLite database stores complete public blocks, their validated cumulative work and header metadata, selected heights, ordered commitments, encrypted receipt rows, reward openings, current nullifiers and offered packets. Block validation/branch selection, detached effects, new selected effects and mempool reconciliation are one FULL-synchronous transaction. A losing branch is verified against its actual parent in a temporary transactional view. The node preserves its validated bytes and removes its temporary selected effects. For a winning branch, it reverses the detached suffix and rechecks already stored replacement bodies with a fresh genuine public proof verification per payment. Straight extensions append from the stored selected frontier without reading or verifying old payment bodies.

Intake accepts up to 64 pending, nonconflicting singleton packets. Mining picks up to four in FIFO order. A winning reorg tries detached payments first in original block/packet order, then existing offers; included duplicates disappear, remaining candidates must still verify under the new branch's anchors and current spentness. Capacity/conflict/expiry rejections leave no economic effects. The pool supplies selection hints; block verification independently rechecks all included proofs. An empty valid block earns the same base subsidy. Transactions and block IDs use separate digest domains/formats. Archive and side-branch storage grow with admitted blocks, and a 64-entry queue and per-block size limit do not constitute Internet-wide denial-of-service protection.

A divergence up to eight heights behind the old tip uses live suffix reversal. For deeper divergence the node automatically empties the selected effects back to genesis, then replays every common-prefix and replacement block in height order from the local archive, including work, each payment proof, rewards and header commitments, in the same SQLite transaction. A losing candidate leaves the original selected effects in place. A failed verification or interrupted writer cannot commit a partial replay. The node counts successful deep selections in a separate local metric; that counter can differ on a newly synchronized node with identical chain state. Automatic deep replay costs linear work in the candidate archive per deep side block and holds the local writer for its duration. The block limit bounds each new block and does not cap the cumulative cost of repeated competing branches. A separate new node can replay every complete canonical body in height order, check work, verify all APV2 proofs and reconstruct the entire state. Cold wallets can request selected tip/header and tip-pinned pages of all commitment words, ordinary commitment/ciphertext rows, public reward records and current nullifiers. A changed tip forces a view retry. The wallet's proofs check membership against this branch, but its choice of which chain is freshest still relies on its node or independent replay.

## Reward-spend observability

The block reveals the miner spending key, gross reward, exact funded note, rho and r. Reusing a payout key links those mining receipts directly. For a real input the pinned Qnero relation instead computes the nullifier as Poseidon2 under the NF domain on `nk || rho || r`. Here `nk = Digest::hash_bytes(["qnero/nk", secret_seed])`, and the public key is the PK-domain hash of the private nk and the separately derived `ak=H(AK,ask)`, in the order `ak || nk`. The wallet uses its secret nk when comparing each reconstructed reward note to the public spent set. Knowing the public reward opening alone therefore leaves an unavailable input to that calculation under the key/hash assumptions. A full viewing key contains nk and permits exact retrospective matching without spending authority. Neither statement supplies general spend unlinkability. On an empty-funded chain, the first real payment after a single mining reward necessarily consumes that reward as a matter of observable funding history; timing, repeated addresses and small feasible input sets can give further attribution. Deducing that a reward was spent can still leave ambiguity about which of a payment's two published nullifiers corresponds to that real input.
